# Draftly final presentation: story treatment

## Brief

- **Audience:** academic project evaluators in data science and engineering.
- **Format:** 22 slides, 16:9. Working assumption: a 20-minute slot, with 19:15 of planned speech (including a 2:30 demonstration) and 0:45 for handovers, pauses, or questions.
- **Thesis:** Draftly connects a measured statutory retrieval problem to a persistent, lawyer-controlled Registration of Title Act (RTA) matter workflow. It makes evidence, proposed facts, legal authority, checks, and professional decisions traceable.
- **Tone:** an academic project account: question, method, implemented artifact, evidence, negative result, and next validation. Avoid a startup sales pitch or claims of legally ready automation.
- **Visual direction for the eventual deck:** `$guizang-ppt-skill` Style B, International Klein Blue preset, with restrained typography, concise claim titles, and large source figures. Keep detailed methods and caveats in presenter notes. This is a visual plan, not a finished deck.
- **Logo and build guide:** the Draftly mark appears on every slide, using cropped transparent blue on light slides and transparent white on dark slides. `PPT_BUILD_GUIDE.md` contains exact projected copy, full speaker notes, image treatments, and build/QA instructions for the PPT skill.
- **Two sections:** slides 04–11 explain legal information retrieval; slides 12–20 explain software engineering and demonstrate the product. Slides 01–03 establish both problems, slide 21 credits each contributor, and slide 22 closes the project.

## The through-line

The opening slide of substance names **two problems in talk order**. First, legal retrieval may return one plausible statutory section while omitting another provision needed for a complete answer. Second, key matter facts are buried in PDFs and scans, which lawyers must read and reconcile. The accepted research paper measures the first problem; the software workbench addresses the handoff from documents to reviewed facts and cited authority. A missing provision or an incorrect parcel identifier can change the work. Three in-person practitioner consultations shaped the project. Anura Dhanaratna's notarial-practice curriculum informed the topic map and statutory coverage; supplied sample matter documents informed document-reading trials. This is design grounding, while lawyer validation of legal labels and field-level extraction scoring remain open.

The presentation then poses two connected questions. **Research:** can retrieval return *every* proposed indispensable provision for a scenario, rather than one plausible hit? **Engineering:** can a workbench keep machine-produced evidence and legal suggestions separate from verified facts and lawyer decisions? The contribution is the combination of a reproducible statutory benchmark and a deployed matter system with explicit review and refusal gates. The presentation should show each contribution on its own evidence.

**Part I, legal information retrieval**, now starts with data: the 52 principal and 60 amending Acts on slide 04 and the two case-law collections and provisional rule extraction on slide 05. The new slide 06 then gives the separate analogous-case retriever its own image and identifies its BM25, statute/topic/catchword graph, optional dense, fusion, and corroboration stages. The recorded 20-query case pilot exercised lexical and graph channels only; its LLM-judged initial 14/20 fell to 10/20 after a corpus rebaseline, so it is development evidence rather than lawyer-verified accuracy. Slide 07 shows how sixteen Law College examination papers became the provisional 50-question statutory QA benchmark, using the existing construction figure and a Hugging Face release badge. The metric slide defines complete-indispensable recall before the existing retrieval-system figure and measured result chart. On the 40-question, 32-matter test split, hierarchical retrieval recorded the highest observed C@20 at 0.375, hybrid fusion 0.344, and typed expansion 0.312; leading paired uncertainty intervals include zero. No system completed a question needing four or more provisions, and only two of 114 missed sections were one explicit edge away from a retrieved seed. A dedicated final slide for this section displays the accepted NeurIPS 2026 GlobalSouthAI workshop paper, its contributions, and the remaining attorney-label and retrieval-integration work. The paper's statutory labels are agent-proposed and await attorney review. Hosted search is BM25; embeddings and query rewriting are in progress and were not measured in the paper.

**Part II, software engineering**, now opens by explaining the scope choice. The team selected post-certificate RTA transactions because title certificates, cadastral or survey-plan information, prescribed requirements, and a bounded transaction path support a reviewable document workflow. Legacy handwritten pattiru or folio OCR, AT-form extraction, historical title reconstruction, and initial title settlement were deferred after the supervisor discussion; the presentation must not imply all RTA evidence is clean or machine-readable. The audience then follows one *generic workflow*, not an invented client case: open a matter; upload evidence; inspect candidates alongside their source; verify or correct facts; run governed checks; converse with the **Draftly Matter Agent** about authorized matter state; review cited statutory research; prepare a draft; pass preflight and lawyer approval gates; record export and audit events. Slide 14 pairs the actual experimental counts with a redacted real OCR overlay: four matters, 38 documents, and 282 pages in the inventory; a 26-page Vision text-coverage pilot; no scored field-level extraction accuracy. Slide 15 gives the complete nine-stage processing sequence its own image, then names the hosted stub and lawyer-review boundary. Slide 16 shows the **shipped research path**: the workbench and FastAPI research service call the internal BM25 section index and return cited passages or insufficient authority. A Hugging Face-generated illustration may supply the shapes; the PPT builder overlays exact, editable labels and keeps the embedding/query-rewriting upgrade dashed until deployed. Cited statutory answers come from the separate research workspace today; direct agent-to-research calling is the intended integration. The original document-processing and matter-agent figures supplied for this talk are earlier **design references**. Their Neon and Google Cloud Storage labels are no longer the hosted storage arrangement. Use cropped process/decision portions with explicit design-reference captions; the current hosted topology appears as a small inset on slide 16.

Return to the built system for a **demonstration**, then a project-wide testing rundown. A prepared synthetic RTA matter should show persistent matter and document records, source-linked candidate review, a check or preflight refusal, and cited research or an insufficient-authority response. The demo should expose the difference between a stub candidate and a lawyer-verified fact. A recorded walk-through and labelled screenshots can preserve the same proof points if a live step fails. Slide 20 then recaps five distinct evaluations: statutory retrieval, similar-case retrieval, held-out case-law rule extraction, the 26-page OCR text pilot, and the September platform tests. The separate `TESTING_EVIDENCE.md` records methods, dates, numbers, and limits for the PPT builder. The platform cycle recorded 3,906 passing backend tests, 348 passing frontend unit/component tests, three passing browser refusal tests, and 15 fixed defects. The broader browser suite had failures. Hosted document extraction is stubbed, dense/hybrid retrieval is an offline experiment, and registration-ready form wording has not been signed off. These are next evaluation targets, not completed tests.

The closing claim is bounded: Draftly has made the *path to a lawyer decision* inspectable and has measured one important research bottleneck. It has not established that automated document reading, legal answers, or final instruments are attorney-validated. The next phase requires attorney adjudication of provision labels, field-level document evaluation, form wording review, and complete authenticated browser journeys. Slide 21 gives each student a specific research or engineering contribution, with Himath's lead on statutory benchmarking and the research-paper first draft stated plainly. Slide 22 carries the closing claim. Confirm the contribution wording against the linked spreadsheet before building the final deck; it required Google sign-in during this edit.

## Source and claim boundaries

The content is grounded primarily in `final-submission/final-report.tex`, `final-submission/current-platform-system.md`, the figure inventory `final-submission/figures/README.md`, the documented team split in `.agents/context/CONTEXT.md`, and the mid-evaluation deck. The Google Slides draft URL provided by the user was inaccessible to the available browser and web tools, so its wording has not been reused. The mid-evaluation deck is a source for the original domain argument and project framing; its older corpus, implementation, and evaluation status is replaced by the September final report.

The platform and retrieval study have different evidence. **Offline** hybrid and hierarchical scores do not describe the hosted **BM25** index. Passing service tests do not establish end-to-end legal correctness. The held-out case-law extraction and multilingual OCR work are supporting research streams, not attorney-validated production capabilities. Only synthetic matter data is within the hosted demo's current operating policy.
