# Draftly product film script

## 1. The problem (0:00–1:05)

Production note: use synthetic documents and values throughout. Record the available prototype, including its visible gaps. Directions under Visuals and Recording notes are not spoken copy.

On-screen text:

"A property transfer starts with documents."

Visuals:

A staged client hands over a synthetic document bundle. Reveal pages labeled identity document, survey plan, title certificate and Form 8 transfer instrument. Show the lawyer moving between those pages, notes, legal sources and a form. Highlight one property detail being checked and copied. Keep "Synthetic demonstration" visible during document shots.

End with: "Read. Compare. Research. Prepare."

Narration:

"A client arrives with a simple request: help me transfer this property.

Behind that request is a careful legal workflow. The notary must read the documents, identify the parties, compare the property details, examine the supporting records, find the relevant law and prepare the instrument.

The information can be scattered across scanned pages, handwritten entries and documents in different languages.

Reading each page is only part of the task. The lawyer also has to establish how the documents relate to one another, which details can be relied on, and what still needs clarification.

Legal research adds another layer: finding a relevant provision, checking its context and connecting it to the evidence in the matter.

That connection is the problem our project addresses."

## 4. The manual workflow and the Draftly workflow (2:15–2:45)

On-screen text:

"Keep the detail connected to its evidence."

Visuals:

Compare the same task on two sides.

Manual workflow: source page → notes → repeated comparison → draft.

With Draftly: source page → proposed value → lawyer review → working draft.

Carry the same highlighted detail through both sequences. Avoid a stopwatch or an invented speed comparison.

Narration:

"In a manual workflow, the lawyer moves between pages, notes, legal sources and the draft, repeatedly checking the same details.

With Draftly, those documents belong to a matter. Proposed information can be reviewed beside its source, and confirmed particulars can support the working instrument.

The lawyer still decides what the evidence means. Draftly helps organize the information and preserve the connection behind each reviewed value."

---

## 5. Open and scope the matter (2:45–3:25)

On-screen text:

"Start with the right transaction."

Visuals:

Open the matter-creation workflow. Show the available routing questions, then the subject and transaction controls. Use a synthetic transfer with one parcel, a transferor and a transferee.

Use answers appropriate to the selected transaction. Show a checklist category, attach the bundle, then hold the created matter and its document list.

Narration:

"We begin by defining the matter.

The lawyer sets the registration system and intended workflow, then identifies the parties and property involved.

Within the matter, we select the transaction and assign its party roles. This tells us which person is transferring which property, and to whom.

If a party is a company, its authority and supporting records need their own review.

Draftly uses the selected workflow to organize the requirements into a checklist.

We then attach the document bundle, bringing the evidence into one workspace for review."

## 6. Read the documents and verify a fact (3:25–4:35)

On-screen text:

"Proposed information becomes a fact through review."

Visuals:

Show the processing results, then focus on one document-review screen.

Keep the original page and one proposed property detail visible together. Open its source, correct or confirm the value, and show the saved review state.

Pause so viewers can read the detail and understand the action. Briefly show manual entry tied to a source page if extraction leaves a blank. Show the selected transaction and subject before saving the review.

Narration:

"The original documents remain the evidence.

Processing proposes document types and the particulars it can read. The lawyer reviews the document grouping and checks each proposed value against its source.

Here, we open one document and inspect a proposed property detail beside its source page.

The lawyer corrects the reading if necessary, associates it with the right property and transaction, and confirms it.

If a scan is unclear or extraction leaves a blank, the lawyer can enter the detail manually and retain its source reference.

Now the reviewed value has a source the lawyer can return to. If that fact changes, affected checks and draft fields need a fresh review."

Recording adjustment:

If prepared results replace live extraction, display "Prepared extraction demo" and include:

"This recording uses prepared extraction results to demonstrate the review workflow."

## 7. Investigate a difference (4:35–5:30)

On-screen text:

"A difference is a question to investigate."

Visuals:

Open two synthetic records containing different property references. Highlight the relevant entries as "Reference A" and "Reference B."

Show the actual issue screen if an implemented check raises the issue. Otherwise, demonstrate manual comparison in the source viewer.

Show a decision supported by evidence, or keep the question open.

Narration:

"Review also means checking how the records relate to one another.

This bundle contains different property references. We need to establish what each reference describes.

The records may concern an earlier transfer, related land or a different part of the transaction history.

Where Draftly's implemented checks raise an issue, the lawyer can inspect its evidence and record a supported decision.

If the relationship is still unclear, the question stays open until the lawyer has the evidence needed to resolve it.

The useful outcome is a matter that shows what has been established and what still needs attention."

Narration:

"Once the required particulars have been reviewed, we move to the working instrument.

For Form 8, the lawyer selects the transaction, property and party roles. Draftly uses the eligible confirmed facts for that selection to populate the supported fields.

Here is the detail we reviewed earlier. The lawyer can inspect it in the draft and follow its supporting evidence. Missing or conflicting particulars stay visible for resolution.

The template wording and editable particulars are handled separately. Each populated field still needs the lawyer's review.

A populated form is still a working draft. The lawyer must examine its content, source text and completeness before deciding how it can be used."

## 10. Legal research and our retrieval contribution (7:05–9:15)

On-screen text:

"Find the provisions behind the question."

Visuals:

7:05–7:25: Open the Overview conversation, then the full Assistant view. Show that both display the same saved conversation. Enter a conveyancing question and show the result available in the recorded build. Use the separate Research workspace only if illustrating that existing journey, and label the change of workspace.

7:25–8:00: If an approved supporting passage is available, show the supported answer and open its citation. Hold the passage, source identity and available version/review labels long enough to read. Show the source location only to the precision supplied. If no approved grounded passage is available, show any permitted metadata or official source lead alongside the truthful gap. Do not manufacture an answer or replay a withheld legacy passage.

8:00–8:35: Show an appropriate case-law lead from the available corpus. Keep its unverified status visible. If an official judgment link is supplied, open it; otherwise inspect the returned excerpt. If no suitable result is available, continue reviewing statutory sources.

8:35–9:15: Show a simple research graphic:

"Scenario → Required provisions → Retrieved provisions"

Display a benchmark result only if the team supplies a verified report with the result, benchmark name, date and scope. Otherwise, keep the graphic and show how a reviewer compares available passages with the expected provisions. If no passage is available, show the missing-authority gap instead. Do not insert an invented performance figure.

Recording note: the source-release and amendment extension has received code review, but real source-content, rights and audience approvals remain pending. Transaction-date/current-date source inspection is implemented and has passed static/build checks and independent code review. Deployment and runtime acceptance have not been verified for this recording. History policy withholds answers and passages for unknown, legacy or unapproved releases. Keep the dated extension narrated as planned until it is accepted and available in the recording build; use a clearly labeled "Planned source inspection" graphic.

Narration:

"So far, we have examined what the documents say. The next question is what the law requires.

We can ask a legal question from the matter's conversation. The compact Overview panel and full Assistant view share the same saved history, so we can return to the question and its evidence later.

The retrieval challenge is finding the provisions a scenario needs. A transfer may raise several legal questions, with related requirements and amendments to examine.

Our legal information retrieval research studies how to find those provisions and make the supporting material available for inspection.

When an approved supporting passage is available, we open its citation and read the evidence behind the answer. Draftly's research workflow retains claims supported by the returned passages. When sources cannot support an answer, Draftly shows the gap and any available source leads.

The lawyer still reads the provision, checks its context and decides whether it applies.

Case-law results are research leads. The lawyer assesses the judgment's relevance and authority before relying on it.

For evaluation, a reviewer compares retrieved passages with the provisions required by a scenario. That makes missed authorities visible as well as useful matches.

Our planned source inspection will bring related amendments into view alongside the transaction date and current date. It will preserve unknown commencement dates for lawyer review.

The goal is research the lawyer can examine, alongside evidence the lawyer can trace."

The legal sources remain open to inspection. The decisions remain with the lawyer.

That is the purpose of Draftly: support the careful work behind a property transfer, through a focused workflow for Sri Lankan notarial and conveyancing practice.

Draftly. Follow the evidence. Keep the lawyer in control."
