# Project Logbook Entry - 16 July 2026

## Log Date

16 July 2026

## Task Description

Normalized the Sri Lankan legal-source corpus and prepared its deterministic
case-to-statute-section graph for the Draftly retrieval workflow.

## Task Details / Reflections

I diagnosed and repaired 47 CommonLII conveyancing records that failed to join
because several year groups were omitted when the remote citation index was
rebuilt. All 3,703 conveyancing judgments now join to complete citation and text
records.

I built a single file-based retrieval store under `data/processed/`. It contains
9,295 normalized Markdown documents and 9,177 case records covering statutes,
amendments, gazettes, institution guides, CommonLII judgments, official court
judgments, and historical law-report volumes. I converted the remaining scanned
legal sources using local OCR and a controlled Google Document AI route. The
cloud fallback used 208 pages and is protected by a 2,000-page limit and a
resumable page cache.

I generated 14,665 deterministic case-to-statute-section candidate links with
confidence labels and evidence snippets. I also retained 62,385 unresolved
section mentions instead of guessing their legal source and produced a balanced
30-row lawyer-verification sample. All generated links remain unverified until
a lawyer reviews them.

Finally, I extended the existing processing notebook with a production-store
stage and added repeatable build and verification scripts. The final checks
reported zero hard corpus inconsistencies, no missing or low-text processed
documents, matching file checksums, and complete processed-only retrieval paths.

The main reflection from this work is that provenance and review state are as
important as extraction coverage for legal retrieval. Ambiguous citations
should remain in a review queue rather than being converted into apparently
authoritative links.
