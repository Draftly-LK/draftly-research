### Draft answer

Issue: What steps the notary retained by the intending purchaser of a thirty-acre coconut estate with a bungalow in Kurunegala must take before, at and after the execution of the conveyance.

Rule: A sale of land is of no force in law unless the deed is in writing and signed by every executant and the witnesses in the presence of a licensed notary who attests it (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1)(a)). A notary may act only in the area and language for which he is authorised (Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule 22). Before drawing a deed affecting an interest in land he must search the registers in the land registry to ascertain the state of the title and whether any prior deed has been registered (s.31 rule 17(a)) and must obtain from the Registrar the certified extract of the folio showing ownership and registered encumbrances (s.31 rule 17(b)(i)); the registry books may be searched and certified extracts obtained by a person interested or his agent (Registration of Documents Ordinance s.42). The search matters because an unregistered instrument is void against a party claiming adversely for value under a later duly registered instrument (Registration of Documents Ordinance s.7(1)), and a transferee for value may lodge a priority notice which holds for six weeks and back-dates registration (s.30(5)). On a transfer the notary must use his best endeavours to obtain the vendor's title deed and endorse the new deed's particulars on it (s.31 rule 33). The deed must carry an accurate description of the land by boundaries, extent and situation with the district, Divisional Secretary's Division, local authority division and Grama Niladhari Division (s.31 rule 16(a)), and the true and full consideration must be ascertained and set out (Notaries Ordinance s.38(1)). The notary may not attest without an assurance that the required stamp duty is provided (s.31 rule 6), duty being payable by him (s.31 rule 7A(a)) and charged on every specified instrument (Stamp Duty (Special Provisions) Act s.3(1)). He must identify the executants from personal knowledge or their identity documents (s.31 rule 9), attest without delay under his signature and seal recording execution, identification, date, place, reading over, consideration paid in his presence and stamps (s.31 rule 20), and submit the deed for registration within thirty days of attestation (s.31 rule 30A). Two statutory restrictions must be excluded: agricultural land other than paddy may not be owned above fifty acres (Land Reform Law s.3(1)(b)), and land held on a state grant cannot be disposed of without the prior approval of the Government Agent (Land Development Ordinance s.19(6)); a consent certificate of the Board is required only for tea or rubber estates (Tea and Rubber Estates (Control of Fragmentation) Act s.3(1)).

Application: The estate lies at Wariyapola in the Kurunegala District and the notary is authorised to practise in Sinhala in that district, so he may accept the retainer and attest the deed. He should take full instructions, call for and examine the vendor's prior deeds, beginning with Deed of Transfer No. 1032 of 1998 and tracing back through the 1979 partition decree, and search the Kurunegala land registry folio in which that deed is registered, taking a certified folio extract. That extract should disclose Bond No. 2210 of 2021 in favour of the bank, and the notary must arrange for a registered discharge, or for the bank's release to be executed and registered simultaneously with the conveyance out of the purchase money; otherwise the client would take subject to that earlier registered interest. Because execution is not to take place until 20 May 2024, a priority notice should be considered so that no instrument executed by the vendor in the interval gains priority. The thirty-acre extent and the boundaries of Lot 1 in Survey Plan No. 4127 must be checked on the ground against the folio description and reproduced in the schedule with the village, Divisional Secretary's Division and district, the bungalow being described with the land. The whole thirty acres is coconut and the client owns no other agricultural land, so the purchase leaves him below the fifty-acre ceiling; coconut is neither tea nor rubber, so no consent certificate is needed; and as the chain shows private freehold with no state permit or grant, no approval of the Government Agent is called for. The full price of Rs. 42,000,000, including the Rs. 4,200,000 advance paid under the agreement of 10 January 2024, must be set out as the consideration, and the balance paid at execution recorded in the attestation with the details of any cheque or draft. Stamp duty on that value must be provided for and paid before attestation. At execution the notary must identify both parties from their national identity cards, have two witnesses of good repute sign, read over and explain the deed, then attest, sign and seal it without delay, endorse the particulars on the vendor's title deed, and lodge the deed for registration by 19 June 2024.

Conclusion: The notary's steps are, in sequence: confirm his competence for the area and take instructions; call for and examine the vendor's title deeds; search the Kurunegala land registry and obtain a certified folio extract; deal with the disclosed mortgage and consider a priority notice; verify extent and boundaries against the survey plan; satisfy himself that no ceiling or consent regime bars the purchase; draw the deed with an accurate schedule and the true and full consideration; provide for and pay stamp duty; execute and attest the deed with identified parties and two witnesses; and submit it for registration within thirty days of attestation. On these facts nothing in the statutory material bars the purchase, provided the bond is discharged and the deed is properly drawn, attested and registered in time.
