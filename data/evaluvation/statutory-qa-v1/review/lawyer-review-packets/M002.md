### Question

If you are retained by the said company as their Notary for the said purchase what are the documents you should check prior to purchasing the said land?

### Enriched background

Sea View Company (Pvt) Ltd wants to purchase a land in Galle facing the sea to construct a hotel. The company was incorporated in Sri Lanka in 2019 and holds a certificate of incorporation numbered PV 00218347. Of its 10,000,000 issued shares, 6,500,000 are held by three Sri Lankan citizens resident in Galle and 3,500,000 are held by Ocean Crest Holdings Pte Ltd, a company incorporated in Singapore. The board has resolved to acquire the property and has authorised its managing director, Ms. Ranmali Fernando, to sign on the company's behalf. The property offered to the company is Lot A depicted in Plan No. 2184 dated 12 January 2021 made by K. A. Wijeratne, Licensed Surveyor, in extent one acre two roods and ten perches, situated at Dadalla within the Galle Municipal Council limits and bounded on the south by the sea. The person offering the land as owner is Mr. D. P. Amarasuriya, who now lives in Melbourne and proposes to sign the transfer through his brother, Mr. L. K. Amarasuriya, acting under a power of attorney granted in Melbourne on 3 March 2024. Mr. D. P. Amarasuriya has produced only Deed of Transfer No. 4712 dated 8 August 2011 attested by S. Perera, Notary Public, and says the earlier deeds in the chain are with his family and can be produced later. A neighbouring family has fenced off and cultivated the northern portion of the land since 2009 and pays nothing to Mr. Amarasuriya. The price agreed between the parties is Rs 100,000,000, and the deed is to be signed at Galle.

### Draft answer

Issue: What documents must the notary retained by Sea View Company (Pvt) Ltd examine before the company buys the Galle land: the documents that prove the vendor's title, the documents that show whether the land is encumbered or affected by a claim, the documents that establish the purchasing company's capacity to take title, and the documents needed because the vendor proposes to sign through an attorney.

Rule: Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (17)(a) requires the notary, before drawing any deed affecting an interest in land, to search or cause to be searched the registers in the land registry to ascertain the state of the title and whether any prior deed has been registered, and rule (17)(b)(i) requires him to obtain from the Registrar of the relevant Land Registry the certified extract of the folio showing ownership and registered encumbrances. Registration of Documents Ordinance s.42 gives the right to search the registry books and take certified copies and extracts. The search matters because under s.7 of that Ordinance an unregistered instrument is void against a later duly registered instrument taken for valuable consideration, because s.11 makes a registered lis pendens bind a purchaser, and because s.32 lets a caveator have a later instrument rectified or cancelled. Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (33) requires the notary to use his best endeavours to obtain the title deed of the property, and rule (16)(a), read with s.13(1) of the Registration of Documents Ordinance, requires an accurate description of boundaries, extent and situation, which can only come from the survey plan and the prior deeds. For the purchaser, Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (17)(d) requires the notary to retain a certified copy of the certificate of incorporation and details of the board of directors, and rule (14) requires a copy of the board resolution authorising the signatory to be attached to the protocol; Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(2) requires that written board authorisation where the directors cannot attend. Companies Act s.5(3) makes the certificate of incorporation conclusive evidence of incorporation. Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act s.2(1)(b) prohibits transfer of title to a Sri Lankan company whose direct or indirect foreign shareholding is fifty per cent or above, s.4(3) requires the company secretary to furnish documentary proof of the foreign shareholding before the Registrar of Lands registers the transfer, and s.18 makes any alienation in contravention void. Where the vendor signs by attorney, Powers of Attorney Ordinance s.3(1)(a) requires the power to be registered with the Registrar-General, s.3B(1) requires the notary to examine the relevant volumes and folios in the Registrar-General's department, to be satisfied that the power has not been revoked or cancelled and to say so in the attestation, and to retain identity documents of grantor and attorney, and Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (30) requires a true copy of the power to be kept with the protocol and forwarded to the Registrar of Lands. Prescription Ordinance s.3 shows that ten years of possession adverse to the owner can defeat a paper title, so possession must be looked at alongside the deeds.

Application: The notary should first search the Galle land registry folio for Lot A in Plan No. 2184 and obtain the certified folio extract showing the current ownership and every registered encumbrance, mortgage, lease, lis pendens, seizure notice and caveat. Because Mr. Amarasuriya has produced only Deed of Transfer No. 4712 of 2011, the notary must call for the earlier deeds in the chain and for the title deed itself so that it can be endorsed, and must check that each link is registered, since an unregistered link can be defeated by a later registered instrument. The survey plan of 2021 and the descriptions in the prior deeds must be compared so that the schedule states the boundaries, extent and situation accurately and matches the folio. On the purchaser's side the notary must take a certified copy of the certificate of incorporation PV 00218347, the particulars of the directors and the board resolution authorising Ms. Fernando to sign, and must obtain from the company secretary documentary proof that the Singapore holding of 3,500,000 out of 10,000,000 shares leaves the foreign shareholding below fifty per cent, since the Registrar of Lands will require that proof and a transfer in breach would be void. Because Mr. Amarasuriya will sign through his brother, the notary must see the original power of attorney of 3 March 2024, confirm that it is registered with the Registrar-General, examine the relevant volumes and folios there for any revocation or cancellation, take copies of the identity documents of both grantor and attorney and keep a copy of the power with the protocol. The fenced and cultivated northern portion held by the neighbours since 2009 must be investigated on the ground, because possession adverse to the owner for ten years can defeat the paper title to that portion. Since the company is buying for hotel construction at a price of Rs 100,000,000, the notary should also confirm from the registry entries and the root of title that no State grant or permit condition and no acquisition notice affects the land.

Conclusion: Before the purchase the notary should check, at a minimum: the certified extract of the relevant folio of the Galle land registry showing ownership and registered encumbrances, including any lis pendens, seizure notice or caveat; the vendor's title deed and the earlier deeds forming the chain of title; the survey plan and the descriptions in the prior deeds; the certificate of incorporation, the particulars of directors and the board resolution of the purchasing company together with the company secretary's documentary proof that its foreign shareholding is below fifty per cent; and the registered power of attorney with a Registrar-General's search confirming it has not been revoked, along with the identity documents of grantor and attorney. He should also satisfy himself about possession on the ground and about the absence of any State grant condition or acquisition affecting the land.
