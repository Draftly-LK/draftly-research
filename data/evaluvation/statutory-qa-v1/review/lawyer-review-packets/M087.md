In 2013 she sold this property. Mrs Kanthi Perera died intestate on 1st September 2014. Kanthi had two children out of her marriage to Kamal De Silva namely Anusha and Anuk. Thereafter he gifted his share of the Estate to his two children. Amali Perea was living with his father and after her marriage to Anura Fernando he passed away. Amali met with an accident after one month of her mothers death and died intestate and issueless.

### Draft answer

Issue: The pedigree has to trace the devolution of Kanthi Perera's intestate estate from her acquisition of the land in 1999 and 2000 down to the present owners. Three questions arise: what remained in her estate at her death on 1st September 2014, how that estate devolved on her surviving husband and her three children, and how Amali Perera's inherited share devolved when she died intestate and issueless about a month later.

Rule: Inheritance ab intestato to immovable property in Sri Lanka is governed by Part III of the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance (s.21), which applies to the estate of a person who dies after the proclamation of the Ordinance and who was married after it (s.20), and which does not reach Kandyans, Muslims or Tamils subject to the Tesawalamai (s.2). A surviving spouse inherits one-half (s.22). Subject to that half, the inheritance passes first to descendants, then ascendants, then collaterals (s.23), and children are preferent to all others and take equally per capita (s.24). Where descendants fail, the estate goes to the parents, and only a surviving parent takes a half (s.25); where both parents have failed it goes to the brothers and sisters, whether of the whole or half blood (s.26). Where all the half-brothers and sisters are related to the intestate on one side only, they take the whole of that inheritance unless a grandparent or higher ascendant on the other side is alive (s.29), and persons equally near in degree take per capita (s.30).

Application: Kanthi Perera became entitled to Lot 3 of Plan No. 2131 by the Final Decree of 2nd December 1999 in DC Colombo case No. 523/P and to Lot 2 of the same plan by Deed of Transfer No. 456 of 6th February 2000, and she amalgamated and resubdivided the two into Lots 1, 2 and 3 of Plan No. 567 of 10th June 2001, each 20 perches. By Deed of Gift No. 1054 of 6th February 2005 she gifted Lot 1 to her daughter Amali Perera subject to her own life interest, so from 2005 Lot 1 no longer belonged to her and formed no part of her intestate estate, and her life interest in it ended at her death in any event. Her intestate estate on 1st September 2014 therefore consisted of Lots 2 and 3 of Plan No. 567. She left her husband Kamal De Silva surviving, so under s.22 he took one half. Under ss.23 and 24 the other half went in equal per capita shares to her three children, Amali of the earlier marriage and Anusha and Anuk of the marriage to Kamal De Silva, giving each of them one sixth. The Administrator's Conveyance of 3rd December 2015 in DC Colombo case No. 567/T conveyed the estate to those heirs and records, rather than creates, a devolution that took effect by operation of law at the death. Kamal De Silva then gifted his own one half to Anusha and Anuk, one quarter to each. Amali Perera died intestate and issueless about one month after her mother, leaving her husband Anura Fernando. Under s.22 he takes one half of her one sixth, that is one twelfth. Her descendants having failed and both her parents being dead, her mother on 1st September 2014 and her father after her marriage to Anura Fernando, s.25 gives nothing to a surviving parent and s.26 sends the other one twelfth to her brothers and sisters. Her only siblings are Anusha and Anuk, half blood on her mother's side, and on the footing that no grandparent or higher ascendant on her father's side survived her, s.29 gives them the whole of that share and s.30 divides it between them per capita, one twenty-fourth each.

Conclusion: The pedigree runs: Kanthi Perera, entitled under the Final Decree of 1999 and Deed of Transfer No. 456 of 2000 and holding Lots 1, 2 and 3 of Plan No. 567 from 2001, gifted Lot 1 away in 2005 by Deed of Gift No. 1054 and died intestate on 1st September 2014, whereupon Lots 2 and 3 devolved as to one half on Kamal De Silva and as to one sixth each on Amali Perera, Anusha and Anuk. Amali Perera then died intestate and issueless, her one sixth passing one twelfth to Anura Fernando and one twenty-fourth each to Anusha and Anuk. Kamal De Silva gifted his one half equally to Anusha and Anuk. The present owners are therefore Anusha and Anuk with eleven twenty-fourths each and Anura Fernando with two twenty-fourths, that is one twelfth. This is subject to the family being governed by the general law and to no ascendant of Amali Perera on her father's side having survived her; the scenario states neither expressly.

### Draft answer

Issue: What share of the property each heir presently owns. The property in question is Lots 2 and 3 of Plan No. 567, which is what remained of Kanthi Perera's holding after Lot 1 had been gifted away in 2005 and disposed of in 2013.

Rule: A surviving spouse inherits one-half of the property of an intestate (Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance s.22). Subject to that half, descendants come first and children take equally per capita (ss.23 and 24). Where descendants fail and both parents are dead, the estate goes to the brothers and sisters of the intestate whether of the whole or half blood (ss.25 and 26); where all the half-blood siblings are related on one side only they take the whole of that share unless an ascendant on the other side survives (s.29), and persons equally near in degree take per capita (s.30). Inheritance to immovable property in Sri Lanka is governed by these provisions (s.21), which apply to these estates (ss.2 and 20).

Application: On Kanthi Perera's death intestate on 1st September 2014, Kamal De Silva took one half of Lots 2 and 3 as surviving spouse, and Amali, Anusha and Anuk took the other half in equal shares, one sixth each. Amali Perera died about a month later, intestate and issueless, leaving her husband Anura Fernando; he took one half of her one sixth, that is one twelfth. Her descendants having failed and both her parents being dead, the other one twelfth passed to her brother and sister of the half blood on her mother's side, Anusha and Anuk, per capita, one twenty-fourth each, on the footing that no ascendant on her father's side survived her. Kamal De Silva then gifted the one half he had inherited to Anusha and Anuk, one quarter to each. Adding the three sources for each of them, Anusha holds one quarter by gift, one sixth by inheritance from her mother and one twenty-fourth from Amali Perera, that is six, four and one twenty-fourths, or eleven twenty-fourths; Anuk holds the same eleven twenty-fourths; and Anura Fernando holds two twenty-fourths. The three shares total twenty-four twenty-fourths.

Conclusion: Of Lots 2 and 3 of Plan No. 567, Anusha owns eleven twenty-fourths, Anuk owns eleven twenty-fourths and Anura Fernando owns one twelfth, that is two twenty-fourths. Kamal De Silva retains nothing, having gifted his half away. These shares assume the family is governed by the general law and that no ascendant of Amali Perera on her father's side survived her. Lot 1 is left out of account because it had been gifted to Amali Perera in 2005 and the scenario states that it was sold in 2013; the scenario does not make clear who sold it, and if only the reserved life interest was in fact sold, Lot 1 would have fallen into Amali Perera's estate and would now be held one half by Anura Fernando and one quarter each by Anusha and Anuk.
