## Original background

Nimal Perera is a Notary practicing in Colombo. Waruna Liyanage a client of his said that his father who is very sick and bed ridden living in Kandy wishes to execute a Partition Deed by himself together with his two brothers and wanted Nimal Perera to attest it. A Notary in Kandy is willing to visit the bed ridden father and get it executed by him. The other two brothers are willing to come to Colombo to execute it before Nimal Perera.

## M071-Q01 (keep, hop count 3)

### Question

What are the rules set out in the Notaries Ordinance in a situation like this?

### Legal issues

- ISS-M071-Q01-01: Whether a Colombo notary may attest a partition deed in respect of a signature placed in Kandy before a different notary, given the requirement that the executant and witnesses sign in the attesting notary's presence.
- ISS-M071-Q01-02: Whether the Notaries Ordinance permits one deed to be executed before two notaries at different times and places, and how the attestation, dating, numbering, protocol and duplicate duties are then divided.
- ISS-M071-Q01-03: Whether the Colombo notary could instead travel to Kandy and attest the bedridden father's execution there.

### Draft answer

Issue: What does the Notaries Ordinance require where one partition deed is to be signed by a bedridden executant in Kandy before a Kandy notary and by his two brothers in Colombo before Nimal Perera, and may Nimal Perera attest the whole deed?

Rule: Notaries Ordinance rule 31(12) forbids a notary to authenticate or attest any deed unless the person executing it and the witnesses signed it in his presence and in the presence of one another and he signed it in their presence, so a notary can attest only the execution that took place before him; rule 31(8) requires at least two witnesses to subscribe their signatures in letters. Rule 31(22), read with s.9 and s.4A, forbids a notary to attest in any area other than the area in which he is authorised to practise. Rule 31(25) expressly allows one deed to be executed or acknowledged before more than one notary: under 31(25)(a) the notary who first attests must comply with the whole of rule 31(20) and every other attesting notary with paragraphs (a) to (e) of that rule and with paragraph (g) for erasures in the signatures he attested; under 31(25)(b), read with rule 31(23), each notary numbers the deed in his own consecutive series; under 31(25)(c) the first attesting notary preserves the protocol required by rule 31(24) and the other notary supplies himself with a certified copy which is deemed his protocol; and under 31(25)(d) every attesting notary must, as far as possible, comply with the other provisions of s.31. Section 32(2) governs a deed executed by two or more parties who do not sign at the same time and place: for rules (6), (7), (23) and (25) it is deemed executed when first signed, and for rules (18) and (20) it is deemed executed whenever it is signed, while rule (19), which forbids erasure, alteration or interpolation after signature, applies from the first signing. Rule 31(18) requires each notary to insert in letters the day, month, year and place of the execution before him, and rule 31(20)(a) and (c) require each attestation to state that the party and witnesses signed in his presence and in the presence of one another and to give the date, the place and the attesting witnesses. Rule 31(28) puts the duty to transmit the duplicate to the Registrar of Lands on the notary who first attests and relieves the other notary of it; rule 31(29) adds a certified copy to the Registrar of Lands of the district where the land lies if that is a different district. Rule 31(26)(c) requires a part-executed deed to be included in the monthly list and allows the Registrar of Lands to call for the duplicate, and if the deed is not signed by all parties within three months of the first signature the notary may be required to deliver the duplicate to the registrar. Section 33 saves an instrument from invalidity for a failure in a matter of form only, and s.34(1)(c) makes breach of a s.31 rule an offence triable summarily, in addition to civil liability.

Application: The father cannot come to Colombo and the brothers will sign before Nimal Perera in Colombo, so the three executants will not sign at the same time and place. Rule 31(12) means Nimal Perera cannot attest the father's signature, which will be made in Kandy before another notary and outside his sight, and he cannot sign the attestation in the father's presence. He also cannot travel to Kandy and attest there, because rule 31(22) confines him to the area in which he is authorised to practise, and s.9 and s.4A tie that area to his warrant and to the judicial zone in which he resides. The lawful course is the one the facts already suggest: the Kandy notary attests the father's execution in Kandy, with two witnesses who sign in that notary's presence and in the presence of one another, and Nimal Perera attests the two brothers' execution in Colombo with two witnesses signing before him. Rule 31(25) then divides the duties. Whichever of the two attests first must comply with the whole of rule 31(20), keep the protocol under rules 31(24) and 31(25)(c) and transmit the duplicate under rule 31(28); the other confines his attestation to paragraphs (a) to (e) of rule 31(20) and keeps a certified copy as his protocol. Each numbers the deed in his own series under rules 31(23) and 31(25)(b), and each states the true date and place of the execution before him under rules 31(18) and 31(20)(c). Section 32(2) makes the two dates workable: the deed counts as executed at the first signing for numbering and for the multi-notary rule, and as executed on each occasion for the dating and attestation rules, and nothing may be altered after the first signature. Because the executions may fall in different months, rule 31(26)(c) requires the part-executed deed to be listed, and the execution should be completed within three months of the first signature. The facts do not say where the land subject to the partition lies or which notary will attest first, so rule 31(29) and the allocation of the first-attesting notary's duties can only be stated conditionally.

Conclusion: Nimal Perera may not attest the whole partition deed, and may not go to Kandy to attest the father's execution. Under the Notaries Ordinance the deed may lawfully be executed before two notaries: the Kandy notary attesting the bedridden father's execution in Kandy and Nimal Perera attesting the two brothers' execution in Colombo, each with two witnesses signing in his presence, each dating and numbering his own execution and each attesting only what he witnessed, with the notary who attests first holding the protocol and transmitting the duplicate under rules 31(25), 31(24) and 31(28), and s.32(2) supplying the timing rules. If Nimal Perera instead attested the father's signature he would breach rule 31(12) and commit an offence under s.34.
