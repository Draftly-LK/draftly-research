### Draft answer

Issue: How long does Romani, as a registered caveator who has been notified that an instrument affecting the land described in her caveat has been presented for registration, have to commence her action in a competent court, and from what date does that period run?

Rule: Under section 32(5) of the Registration of Documents Ordinance, where an instrument affecting the land described in a caveat is presented for registration while the caveat is in force, the caveator's remedy is an action commenced in a competent court within the period fixed by that subsection, running from the posting of the notice required by section 32(4). Section 32(4) is the notice given to the caveator when an instrument affecting the same land is subsequently presented, sent by registered letter to the address given in the caveat; the Registration of Documents (Amendment) Act No. 32 of 2022 s.2(4) recast it as a notice in Form XI of the Second Schedule. As originally enacted section 32(5) allowed thirty days, but s.2(5) of the same 2022 Act substituted 'a competent court within sixty days' for 'a competent court within thirty days'. In that action the caveator must prove that the instrument presented is or was void or voidable by her, or fraudulent as against her, or in derogation of her lawful rights, and the court may then order it to be rectified or cancelled and the register corrected. A caveat itself lasts a maximum of two years under section 32(3) as substituted by s.2(3) of the 2022 Act.

Application: Romani's caveat over the Battaramulla land was registered on 3 March 2025 and was still current in June 2025, so the caveat was in force. The document presented is a deed of transfer of the same allotment of land by a stranger, which is an instrument affecting the land described in her caveat, so section 32(5) is engaged. The Registrar posted the notice by registered letter to the Colombo address given in the caveat on 12 June 2025, and it is that posting, not Romani's receipt of the letter in New Zealand, that starts the period. Because the notice was posted in 2025, well after the 2022 amendment, the applicable period is sixty days and not the original thirty. Whether Romani can in fact obtain rectification or cancellation depends on proof that the transfer is void, voidable or fraudulent as against her, which the facts do not settle; the time limit applies regardless.

Conclusion: Romani must commence her action in a competent court within sixty days from the posting of the Registrar's notice, that is by about 11 August 2025 on a notice posted on 12 June 2025. She should be advised to instruct an attorney in Sri Lanka before she leaves, since the period runs from posting and not from her actual receipt of the letter abroad.
