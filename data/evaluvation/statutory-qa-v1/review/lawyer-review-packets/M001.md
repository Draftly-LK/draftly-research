### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Samantha, a citizen of the United States of America, can become entitled to the intestate estate of her father Suresh Perera, and to the further shares that came to her on the later intestacies, notwithstanding the Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act No. 38 of 2014 as amended by Act No. 21 of 2018.

Rule: The Act is deemed to have come into operation with effect from 1 January 2013 (s.1(2)), and the 2018 Amendment Act is deemed to have come into operation on 1 April 2018 (Amendment Act s.1). Section 2(1)(a) prohibits the transfer of title of any land situated in Sri Lanka to a foreigner, unless exempted under section 3. Foreigner means a person who is not a citizen of Sri Lanka, and transfer means any sale, donation, gift or any conveyance by or under which title passes to another person (s.25). Section 3(1)(d) exempts from section 2 any land the title of which is transferred by intestacy, gift or testamentary disposition to a next of kin who is a foreigner, in accordance with the applicable law of succession of Sri Lanka, and section 3(1)(e) separately exempts a transfer to a dual citizen. Alienation in contravention of the Act is void (s.18). Section 2 of the 2018 Amendment Act amends section 3(1) by substituting paragraph (b), altering paragraph (h) and adding a new paragraph (i); it leaves paragraph (d) untouched. Who is next of kin on an intestacy is settled by the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance, under which children take equally per capita (s.24) and, where descendants fail and one parent survives, half-blood siblings related through the deceased parent share in the sibling half (ss.25 and 27).

Application: Suresh Perera died intestate on 10 December 1995. The Act operates only from 1 January 2013, so it has no application at all to the vesting of Samantha's one-sixth in 1995; her entitlement from her father is outside the Act on temporal grounds alone. The shares that reached her on Rakitha's death on 2 July 2022 and Muditha's death on 5 June 2023 do fall within the operative period of both the principal Act and the 2018 Amendment. Assuming she holds no Sri Lankan citizenship, Samantha is a foreigner within s.25. Even so, s.2(1) bites only on a transfer of title, and transfer is defined as a sale, donation, gift or conveyance by which title passes, all of which are voluntary dispositions; a devolution by operation of law on an intestacy is not naturally any of them. More directly, s.3(1)(d) expressly exempts land whose title is transferred by intestacy to a next of kin who is a foreigner in accordance with the applicable law of succession of Sri Lanka. Samantha takes as a child of Suresh under s.24 of the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance and as a half-blood sister of Rakitha and Muditha under ss.25 and 27 of that Ordinance, so she is next of kin taking under Sri Lankan succession law in every instance. The 2018 Amendment does not disturb paragraph (d). Section 18 is therefore not engaged and nothing in her entitlement is void. If Samantha is in fact also a citizen of Sri Lanka, she is not a foreigner at all and s.3(1)(e) would apply independently; the scenario states only her United States citizenship and does not settle the point.

Conclusion: Yes. Samantha can become entitled to the intestate estate of Suresh Perera. The Act cannot reach the 1995 devolution because it operates only from 1 January 2013, and the shares that came to her in 2022 and 2023 are within the express intestacy exemption in s.3(1)(d), which the 2018 Amendment left intact. Her entitlement is not an alienation in contravention of the Act and is not void under s.18. One caution should be recorded: s.3(4) provides that where a transfer is effected under s.3 the Act applies to every subsequent transfer of that land, so any later disposition by Samantha would be tested against s.2 in the ordinary way.
