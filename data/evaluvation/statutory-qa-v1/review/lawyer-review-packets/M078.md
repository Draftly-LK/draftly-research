### Draft answer

Issue: Kamal has already leased the Nugegoda house to Jayantha de Siva by an Indenture of Lease and has since mortgaged the same house to a Lending Institution, which wants its Mortgage Bond to rank ahead of the lease. The question is whether the later mortgage bond can take priority over the earlier lease, and what Kamal and the lender must do to achieve it.

Rule: Priority between competing instruments affecting the same land is decided by the land register and not by the order of execution. Under Registration of Documents Ordinance s.8(b) every instrument that creates, charges or encumbers any interest in land is an instrument affecting land, which covers both an Indenture of Lease and a mortgage bond; only a tenancy at will, a tenancy for a month or less, or one determinable on a month's notice is excluded by proviso (xi) to s.8. Section 7(1) then provides that such an instrument, unless duly registered, is void as against a party claiming an adverse interest on valuable consideration under a subsequent instrument which is duly registered. Section 14(2) provides that an instrument is not duly registered unless it is registered in the proper book and folio for the land, and s.27(1) provides that for the purpose of priority the time of presentation in the day book is taken as the time of registration. Section 30(2) allows an intending mortgagee to register a priority notice before the bond is executed, in which case the bond, if registered while the notice is in force, is deemed registered at the time of the notice, and s.30(3) blocks registration of any competing instrument executed by the owner while a notice registered with his written consent is in force. Section 7(2) preserves an exception where the prior registration was obtained by fraud or collusion, and s.7(4) makes clear that registration confers nothing beyond that priority. Under Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (17)(a) the notary must search the land registry before drawing the bond.

Application: The Indenture of Lease creates an interest in the Nugegoda house in favour of Jayantha de Siva and the Mortgage Bond charges the same house, so both fall within s.8(b), provided the lease is for more than a month. The loan is valuable consideration, so the Lending Institution is a party claiming an adverse interest on valuable consideration within s.7(1). Everything therefore turns on the state of the folio for this land, which the notary must ascertain by a search under s.31 rule (17)(a) of the Notaries Ordinance. If the Indenture of Lease has not been registered, the Mortgage Bond, once duly registered in the correct folio under s.14(2), will under s.7(1) render the lease void as against the Lending Institution and the bond will rank first, priority running from the time of presentation under s.27(1). To make that outcome secure against anything Kamal might do in the meantime, the Lending Institution should register a priority notice under s.30 before the bond is executed, so that the bond is deemed registered from the date of the notice and no competing instrument executed by Kamal can be registered while the notice is in force. If, on the other hand, the lease has already been registered, s.7(1) gives Kamal nothing to work with: the lease is duly registered and prior, the later bond cannot displace it, and Kamal cannot honestly promise the Lending Institution first place. Kamal must also be warned that under s.7(2) fraud or collusion in securing the prior registration would defeat the priority so obtained, so concealing the lease from the lender or from the registry is not an option, and that under s.7(4) registration of the bond secures priority and nothing else. The facts do not state the term of the lease, whether the lease has been registered, or whether the bond has yet been executed, so the advice has to be given on both footings.

Conclusion: Advise Kamal that priority here is a question of registration, not of the dates of the deeds. A search of the folio for the Nugegoda land must be done first. If the Indenture of Lease is unregistered, the Lending Institution can obtain the priority it wants by registering a priority notice under Registration of Documents Ordinance s.30 and then having the Mortgage Bond duly registered in the proper folio while that notice is in force, whereupon the lease is void against the bond under s.7(1). If the lease is already registered, the Mortgage Bond cannot be given priority over it by registration, and Kamal should tell the Lending Institution so and take the matter up with the lessee rather than conceal the lease, since fraud or collusion would in any event defeat the priority under s.7(2).

### Draft answer

Issue: Who must pay the stamp duty on the Mortgage Bond securing Rs.3,000,000 over the Nugegoda house, to whom it is paid, and by what procedure.

Rule: Stamp Duty (Special Provisions) Act, No. 12 of 2006 s.4(g) makes a bond or mortgage for a definite and certain sum affecting property a specified instrument, and s.3(1) charges duty on it. Section 6 provides that, otherwise than in cases where there is an agreement to the contrary, stamp duty is payable by the persons it lists, and since no paragraph of s.6 names a bond or mortgage, paragraph (e) applies and the duty falls on the person drawing, making or executing the instrument. Section 8(1) requires the duty to be paid by affixing adhesive stamps to the required value prior to or at the time of execution, with the value specified on the face of the instrument. Section 8(2) provides that duty on a specified instrument relating to a mortgage or lease of immovable property may instead be paid to a prescribed bank prior to or at the time of execution, or, where that is impossible for reasons beyond the control of the person liable, within seven days from execution, and that the bank issues a certificate in the prescribed form which is affixed to the instrument. Section 8(3) requires the executant to cancel adhesive stamps at execution by writing his name or initials across each stamp and to perforate any stamp of fifty rupees or more, and s.8(4) deems an instrument bearing uncancelled stamps unstamped to that extent. Section 2 charges the Commissioner-General with the implementation and administration of the Act, and s.10 empowers an Assessor to assess the duty on the person liable and serve a notice to pay where an instrument is not duly stamped. Section 9 sends duty to a Provincial Council only where the instrument is one referred to in List I of the Ninth Schedule to the Constitution, and Western Province Financial Statute, No. 6 of 1990 s.37 charges provincial duty on transfers of immovable property, court documents and motor vehicle transfers alone. Under Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (6) a notary must not attest a deed or instrument unless he has an assurance that the required stamp duty is provided.

Application: The Mortgage Bond is a specified instrument under s.4(g). No paragraph of s.6 deals with a bond or mortgage, so paragraph (e) governs and the duty is payable by the person drawing, making or executing the bond, that is Kamal Perera as mortgagor. That allocation is expressly subject to any agreement to the contrary in the opening words of s.6, so if the bond or the loan agreement with the Lending Institution provides otherwise, that agreement prevails; a lending institution's bond ordinarily puts the duty on the borrower, which leaves it with Kamal in any event. Because the bond relates to a mortgage of immovable property, s.8(2) applies and the duty may be paid to a prescribed bank before or at execution, with the bank's certificate of payment in the prescribed form affixed to the instrument; if payment cannot be made by then for reasons beyond Kamal's control it must be made to the prescribed bank within seven days of execution. If adhesive stamps are used instead under s.8(1) they must be affixed to the required value before or at execution, the value must be specified on the face of the bond, Kamal must cancel them at execution under s.8(3) and perforate any stamp of fifty rupees or more, failing which the bond is treated as unstamped to that extent under s.8(4). The house is in Nugegoda, so s.9 and the Western Province Financial Statute have to be considered, but s.37 of that statute does not charge mortgage bonds, so the duty is not a Provincial Council receipt; it is national duty administered by the Commissioner-General under s.2. The notary attesting the bond must not do so unless satisfied under Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (6) that the required stamp duty is provided. If the bond turns out to be insufficiently stamped, an Assessor may under s.10 assess the duty payable by the person liable and serve a notice requiring payment within the time stated.

Conclusion: Kamal Perera, as the person drawing, making and executing the Mortgage Bond, is the person liable for the stamp duty under Stamp Duty (Special Provisions) Act s.6(e), unless the bond or the loan agreement provides otherwise. The duty is not payable to the Western Provincial Council, because Western Province Financial Statute s.37 does not charge mortgage bonds; it is payable under the national Act, which the Commissioner-General administers. As to procedure, the duty must be met prior to or at the time of execution, either by affixing and cancelling adhesive stamps of the required value with the value stated on the face of the bond, or, as is usual for a mortgage of immovable property, by payment to a prescribed bank whose certificate in the prescribed form is affixed to the bond, with the seven day fallback in s.8(2) where payment beforehand was impossible for reasons beyond his control. The notary will not attest the bond without an assurance that the duty is provided, and an understamped bond exposes Kamal to an assessment and a notice to pay from an Assessor.
