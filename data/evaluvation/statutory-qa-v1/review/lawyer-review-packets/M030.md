## Original background

A foreigner wishes to purchase a land in Sri Lanka for his Apartment business. You have been retained by him as his Attorney-at-Law and Notary.

## M030-Q01 (enrich, hop count 3)

### Question

How would you advise him?

### Enriched background

Mr. Anders Holmqvist, a citizen of Sweden holding a Swedish passport, wishes to purchase a land in Sri Lanka for his apartment business. He has never been a citizen of Sri Lanka, has not obtained dual citizenship, and is in Sri Lanka on a resident visa. You have been retained by him as his Attorney-at-Law and Notary.

The property he has selected is Lot 4 in Survey Plan No. 8172 dated 12 March 2019 made by K. A. Weerasooriya, Licensed Surveyor, an allotment of bare private land 38.5 perches in extent called 'Nelumwatta', bearing assessment No. 118/3, Hill Street, Dehiwala, in the District of Colombo, Western Province. It is held in freehold by Mrs. S. P. Dharmasiri, a citizen of Sri Lanka, who is willing to sell. The land is bare, with no building on it and no condominium plan of any kind registered over it. Mr. Holmqvist intends to construct a twelve-storey residential apartment building on it and to sell and rent out the apartments. The agreed price is Rs. 240,000,000, which he proposes to remit in full from his bank in Stockholm to a Sri Lankan bank before the deed is executed.

He instructs you on 2 September 2024 and wishes the deed of transfer to be drawn, executed and attested in October 2024. He also asks whether he can mortgage the property to a local bank to fund construction. He has not incorporated any company in Sri Lanka. He is not related to the owner, holds no approval of the Cabinet of Ministers and no project approval of any kind, is not a diplomatic mission or an international organisation, and is not a bank or a finance leasing institution. He tells you he is willing to restructure the transaction if that is what the law requires.

### Legal issues

- ISS-M030-Q01-01: Whether a person who is not a citizen of Sri Lanka may take a transfer of title to private land in Sri Lanka.
- ISS-M030-Q01-02: Whether any statutory exemption, in particular the condominium parcel exemption or a Ministerial exemption for a Strategic Development Project, is available on these facts.
- ISS-M030-Q01-03: What the legal effect is of a deed of transfer executed in contravention of the prohibition, and what the Registrar of Lands must do with it.
- ISS-M030-Q01-04: What lawful alternatives (a lease of up to ninety-nine years, or acquisition through a Sri Lankan company with foreign shareholding below fifty per cent) are open, and on what conditions.
- ISS-M030-Q01-05: Whether the land so acquired or leased can be mortgaged to a licensed bank to fund the apartment construction.

### Draft answer

Issue: Whether an Attorney-at-Law and Notary may lawfully draw and attest a deed transferring the freehold title of a private allotment of bare land in Dehiwala to a Swedish citizen who intends to develop and run an apartment business on it; if not, what consequences follow for such a deed and what lawful routes remain open to the client at October 2024.

Rule: Section 2(1) of the Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act, No. 38 of 2014 prohibits the transfer of title of any land situated in Sri Lanka to a foreigner, to a Sri Lankan company in which direct or indirect foreign shareholding is fifty per cent or above, or to a foreign company, unless exempted under section 3. Section 25 defines a foreigner as a person who is not a citizen of Sri Lanka, defines land to include State and private land and any interest in it, and defines transfer as any sale or conveyance by which title passes. Section 3(1) lists the exemptions, of which the only one relevant to an apartment business is section 3(1)(b), a condominium parcel specified under the Apartment Ownership Law where the entire value is paid upfront by inward foreign remittance before the deed is executed; section 3(2) allows the Minister, with prior Cabinet approval, to exempt a foreign entity in listed sectors engaged in a Strategic Development Project. Under section 9(4) of the Apartment Ownership Law, No. 11 of 1973 a condominium parcel may be held and dealt with as land only after a Condominium Plan is registered. Section 18 of the 2014 Act makes any alienation in contravention of the Act void, and section 4(1) forbids the Registrar of Lands to register such a transfer. The lawful alternatives are a lease under section 5(1), which may not exceed ninety-nine years and on which, by section 5A, no Land Lease Tax is charged for an indenture executed on or after 1 January 2016, or acquisition through a company incorporated in Sri Lanka with less than fifty per cent foreign shareholding, which by section 2(2)(a) must stay below fifty per cent for twenty consecutive years. Section 11(1) bars mortgaging or pledging such land to a licensed bank for five years from execution of the instrument.

Application: Mr. Holmqvist is not a citizen of Sri Lanka and has no dual citizenship, so he is a foreigner within section 25 and the prohibition in section 2(1)(a) applies to him. The subject matter is a 38.5 perch allotment of private land at Hill Street, Dehiwala, which is land within section 25, and the proposed deed of transfer for Rs. 240,000,000 is a transfer within that section. None of the section 3(1) exemptions is available on these facts: he is not a diplomatic mission or an international organisation, not a dual citizen, not the next of kin of the owner, not a bank or a finance leasing institution, and the land is bare land over which no condominium plan has been registered, so there is no condominium parcel he could buy under section 3(1)(b). His willingness to remit the whole price from Stockholm does not assist, because that condition attaches only to the condominium exemption. He holds no Ministerial Order under section 3(2) and his venture has not been identified as a Strategic Development Project. A deed executed in October 2024 in his own name would therefore be void under section 18, and the Registrar of Lands would be bound by section 4(1) to refuse registration, so he would part with the price and acquire nothing. Because he is willing to restructure, two routes remain: a lease of the land to him for a term not exceeding ninety-nine years under section 5(1), on which no Land Lease Tax is payable because the indenture would be executed after 1 January 2016; or incorporation of a Sri Lankan company in which his shareholding stays below fifty per cent, subject to the twenty-year holding condition in section 2(2)(a), breach of which would render the transfer void. On his question about construction finance, section 11(1) prevents the land from being mortgaged or pledged to a licensed bank for five years from the execution of the transfer or lease. If instead his object is to own completed apartments rather than to develop the site, he could buy condominium parcels in a project whose Condominium Plan is already registered, paying the entire value upfront by inward foreign remittance before execution.

Conclusion: He must be advised that he cannot buy this land in his own name: a deed of transfer to him would be void under section 18 of Act, No. 38 of 2014 and would not be registered. As his Notary you should decline to draw or attest such a deed. He should be advised instead to take a lease of the land for a term not exceeding ninety-nine years, on which no Land Lease Tax is now chargeable, or to acquire through a company incorporated in Sri Lanka whose foreign shareholding is and remains below fifty per cent for twenty years, or, if what he wants is completed apartments, to purchase registered condominium parcels with the entire value remitted from abroad before execution. He should also be warned that the land cannot be mortgaged to a licensed bank for five years, which affects his construction funding.
