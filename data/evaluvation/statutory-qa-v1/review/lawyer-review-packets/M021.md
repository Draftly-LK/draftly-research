### Draft answer

Issue: Two issues arise. First, what is the minimum age for executing a last will under the Wills Ordinance as most recently amended, and whether Sunil, who is 20, meets it. Second, what procedure Sunil must follow to execute a valid last will, given that he is seriously ill and wishes to dispose of both a house and a fixed deposit.

Rule: The latest amendment to the Wills Ordinance is the Wills Ordinance (Amendment) Act No. 29 of 2022. Section 2 of that Act repealed section 2 of the Ordinance and substituted a new section under which it is lawful for any person who has reached the age of eighteen years, residing within or outside Sri Lanka, to execute a will disposing of movable and immovable property (Wills Ordinance s.2(1)). Section 3 of the amending Act repealed section 3 of the Ordinance, the provision that had invalidated wills made below the prescribed age and that had earlier been amended by Act No. 5 of 1993 to replace twenty-one years for males and eighteen years for females with a uniform eighteen years. The age limit is therefore eighteen completed years. The word will in the Ordinance extends to a testament, a codicil and any other testamentary disposition (Wills Ordinance s.9). As to form, no will is valid unless it is in writing and executed as prescribed, and the testator must sign and affix his left or right thumb impression at the foot or end of the will before a notary public and two witnesses who are present at the same time; if the thumb impression cannot be obtained, another finger impression or the toe impression is to be affixed (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.4(1) and (2), as amended by Act No. 30 of 2022). The notary must not attest unless at least two witnesses have subscribed their signatures in letters (Notaries Ordinance s.31(8)) and unless the executant and the witnesses have signed in his presence and in the presence of one another, he himself signing in their presence (Notaries Ordinance s.31(12)). In the case of a will the notary must set out in the attestation that the testator was in good and sound mind to execute the will, and must enter in his deed register the number assigned to the will, its date and the name of the testator (Notaries Ordinance s.31(20)(h)). For a will the notary must also affix to the protocol a sealed passport size photograph of the signatory and keep a copy of his identity document with the protocol (Notaries Ordinance s.31(17)(b)(iii)). A will is excepted from the requirement that notarial instruments be executed in triplicate (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.16). A benefit given by the will to an attesting witness or to that witness's spouse is void so far as that witness is concerned, though the witness remains competent to prove the will (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.11). A will duly executed needs no other publication; it is proved in the District Court after the testator's death (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.9).

Application: Sunil was born on 14 March 2005 and is 20 years old at the intended date of execution, so he has reached the age of eighteen years and is competent under Wills Ordinance s.2(1) as substituted in 2022. His estate consists of an immovable property held on a deed of gift and a fixed deposit, both of which fall within the movable and immovable property that s.2(1) permits him to dispose of, and s.9 of the Ordinance means the same rule would govern any codicil he later makes. His illness does not by itself affect competence under the Ordinance; his physician's record that he remains lucid and able to sign and place a thumb impression means the notary can properly make the statement of good and sound mind that Notaries Ordinance s.31(20)(h) requires, and that the signature and thumb impression required by Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.4(2) can be obtained. Had the thumb impression been unobtainable, the proviso permitting another finger impression or a toe impression would apply. The procedure he should follow is therefore to have the notary draw the will in writing; to assemble the notary and his two neighbours as witnesses at his residence at the one time; to sign and affix his thumb impression at the foot or end of the will in the presence of all of them, with the two witnesses subscribing their signatures in letters in his presence and in the presence of one another and of the notary, and the notary signing in their presence, as s.4(2) and Notaries Ordinance s.31(8) and (12) require; and to allow the notary to complete the attestation stating the sound mind of the testator, to make the deed register entry, and to attach the photograph and the copy of the identity document to the protocol. Because his chosen witnesses take no benefit under the will, Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.11 does not cut down any of the gifts. Only one instrument need be executed, s.16 excepting wills from the triplicate rule, and no publication is needed during his lifetime, the will being proved in the District Court after his death under s.9.

Conclusion: The age limit for executing a last will is eighteen years, fixed by Wills Ordinance s.2(1) as substituted by the Wills Ordinance (Amendment) Act No. 29 of 2022, the former section 3 having been repealed by the same Act. Sunil, at 20, is competent to make a will disposing of both his house and his fixed deposit. To make it valid he must execute it in writing and sign it, together with his thumb impression, at the foot or end of the will before a notary public and two witnesses all present at the same time, the notary attesting it, stating in the attestation that Sunil was of good and sound mind, entering the will in his deed register and attaching Sunil's photograph and a copy of his identity document to the protocol. His illness is no bar so long as he is of sound mind at execution, and if his thumb impression cannot be taken another finger impression or a toe impression may be used instead.
