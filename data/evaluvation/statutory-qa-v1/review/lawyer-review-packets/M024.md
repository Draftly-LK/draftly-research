## Original background

Malani Silva is a Notary Practicing in Colombo .Her friend Rupa Perera who is the owner of a business premises in Galle wishes to lease out the same to a Gamini Sirisene for two (02) years at a monthly rental of Rs 150,000/. However Rupa is unable to come to Colombo to sign the Agreement

## M024-Q01 (keep, hop count 3)

### Question

How can this Agreement be signed by both parties?

### Legal issues

- ISS-M024-Q01-01: Whether a lease of business premises for two years falls within section 2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance and must therefore be executed in notarial form.
- ISS-M024-Q01-02: Whether both parties must sign the instrument at the same time and place before a single notary, or whether execution may be split.
- ISS-M024-Q01-03: Whether a notary practising in Colombo may attest the lessor's signature taken in Galle.
- ISS-M024-Q01-04: Whether the lessor may instead authorise another person to sign the lease on her behalf, and what form that authority must take.

### Draft answer

Issue: By what lawful means may a two-year lease of business premises in Galle be signed by a lessor who cannot travel to Colombo and by a lessee dealing with a notary who practises in Colombo?

Rule: A lease of immovable property for a period exceeding one month is within Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2, because the only leases excluded are a lease at will and a lease for a period not exceeding one month. Such an instrument has no force in law unless it is in writing and signed by every executant, or by a person duly authorised by the executant, and by the witnesses in the presence of a licensed notary public present at the same time and in the presence of one another, and is attested by that notary. Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (12) casts the same requirement on the notary as a duty, and rule (22) forbids her from attesting in any area other than that in which she is authorised to practise; s.4A treats the warrant as authorising practice in the judicial zone in which she resides. Notaries Ordinance s.32(2) deals expressly with a deed executed by two or more parties who do not sign it at the same time and place, and s.31 rule (25) governs attestation where an instrument is executed before more than one notary, the notary who first attests complying with the whole of rule (20) and every other notary with paragraphs (a) to (e). Rule (27)(c)(ii) contemplates completion within three months of the first signature. Alternatively, s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance allows the instrument to be signed by a person duly authorised by the executant. That authority is a power of attorney within Powers of Attorney Ordinance s.2, which if executed in Sri Lanka must be executed before two witnesses and notarially attested, must satisfy s.3A because the lease is a transaction within s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance, and must be registered with the Registrar General under s.3; Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (30) then requires the attesting notary to keep a true copy of the power with her protocol and forward a copy with the duplicate to the Registrar of Lands.

Application: The proposed lease runs for two years at Rs 150,000 a month, so it is well outside the one-month exclusion and must be executed notarially. The parties cannot cure Rupa Perera's absence by exchanging the document through the post and signing it apart, because both s.2 and rule (12) require signature in the presence of the notary and of the witnesses, all present together. Malani Silva practises in Colombo, and rule (22) with s.4A stops her from attesting a signature taken in Galle, so she cannot solve the problem by travelling to the lessor. Two routes remain. First, the one instrument may be executed in parts: Rupa Perera signs in Galle with two witnesses before a notary authorised to practise in that zone, and Gamini Sirisene signs in Colombo before Malani Silva, each notary attesting the signatures taken before her. That is the situation s.32(2) addresses, and rule (25) then allocates the attestation, numbering and protocol duties between the two notaries, the notary who first attests complying with the whole of rule (20). Rule (27)(c)(ii) shows the execution is expected to be completed within three months of the first signature. Second, Rupa Perera may grant a power of attorney to a person able to attend in Colombo. Because the lease is a transaction within s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance, that power must be notarially attested and comply with s.3A of the Powers of Attorney Ordinance, and must be registered with the Registrar General under s.3; the attorney then signs the lease before Malani Silva, who keeps a copy of the power with her protocol under rule (30). The facts do not say whether Rupa Perera is willing to appoint an attorney or whether a notary is readily available in the Galle zone, so the choice between the two routes is practical rather than legal.

Conclusion: The lease must be executed notarially. It may lawfully be signed either by the two parties executing the same instrument at different times and places before two notaries, Rupa Perera before a notary practising in the Galle zone and Gamini Sirisene before Malani Silva in Colombo, as contemplated by Notaries Ordinance s.32(2) and s.31 rule (25); or by Rupa Perera granting a notarially attested and registered power of attorney under the Powers of Attorney Ordinance to an attorney who signs on her behalf in Colombo, as permitted by Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2. What is not open to the parties is signing the agreement outside the presence of a notary and the witnesses, or having the Colombo notary attest a signature taken in Galle.
