## M006-Q01 (enrich, hop count 3)

### Question

Can Saman attest the said Deed? Explain.

### Enriched background

The said land is situated in Kurunegala. It is the allotment called "Kurunduwatta", 0.2023 hectares in extent, at Malkaduwawa village in the Kurunegala Divisional Secretary's Division of the Kurunegala District. Before drawing the deed Saman obtained Mr. Perera's title deed and a certified extract of the relevant folio from the Kurunegala Land Registry, and he intends to tender the completed deed for registration at that registry.

The draft deed is written in Sinhala. Saman proposes that Mr. Perera and the company's authorised signatory sign it before two witnesses on 20 April 2026 at his Hulftsdorp office in Colombo, and that he attest it there.

### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Saman, whose notarial warrant authorises him to practise within the Judicial Zone of Colombo and who is employed as a legal officer of the purchasing company, may attest a Deed of Transfer of land situated in the Kurunegala District, and if so on what conditions and with what consequential duties.

Rule: A notary's warrant entitles him to practise as a notary in the language in which he passed the conveyancing examination and within the judicial zone in which he resides (Notaries Ordinance s.3(1)), and s.9 binds him to keep his office within the area specified in that warrant. Rule (22) of s.31 provides that he shall not authenticate or attest any deed in any area other than that in which he is authorised to practise, nor in any language other than that in which he is authorised to practise, nor attest a deed drawn in such another language. The prohibition is expressed in terms of the area in which the notary attests and the language of the instrument; it says nothing about where the land affected by the deed is situated. That the situation of the land is not a bar appears from rule (29) of s.31, which deals expressly with a deed affecting land in a district other than that in which the attesting notary resides and requires him, besides transmitting the duplicate in the ordinary way, to deliver or transmit to the Registrar of Lands of the district in which the land is situated a copy certified by him as correct, with a list in duplicate, on or before the fifteenth day of the month following execution. Rule (30A) of s.31 allows sixty days instead of thirty for submitting for registration a deed required to be registered outside the jurisdiction in which the notary practises, and Registration of Documents Ordinance s.14(1) requires the instrument to be registered in the book allotted to the division in which the land lies. Attesting outside the authorised area is an offence: rule (22) is not among the rules listed in s.34(1)(a) or (b), so a breach attracts s.34(1)(c), a fine not exceeding fifty thousand rupees in addition to any civil liability. The zone may be changed only by a fresh warrant granted by the Minister under s.11. Employment as a legal officer is not a disqualification; rule (17)(c) of s.31 assumes a notary may be a legal officer of a company and requires him to state the company's registered address on the front page of the deed. The related disqualification is rule (13) of s.31, which bars a notary from attesting a deed to which he is a party.

Application: Saman's licence is for the Judicial Zone of Colombo, so by s.3(1) and s.9 his authorised area is Colombo and his office at Hulftsdorp is within it. The deed is to be signed by Mr. Perera and the company's authorised signatory before two witnesses, and attested by Saman, at that Colombo office on 20 April 2026; the attestation therefore takes place inside the area in which he is authorised to practise. The deed is drawn in Sinhala, the language of his warrant. Both limbs of rule (22) of s.31 are thus satisfied. The fact that the land, Kurunduwatta at Malkaduwawa, lies in the Kurunegala District does not put the attestation outside his area, because rule (22) looks to where the notary attests and not to where the land lies, and rule (29) of s.31 proceeds on the footing that a notary may attest a deed affecting land in a district other than his own. Saman is not a party to the deed and holds no shares or directorship in the purchasing company, so rule (13) of s.31 is not engaged; because he is a legal officer of that company, rule (17)(c) of s.31 requires the company's registered address to be stated on the front page of the deed. Because the land is in another district, he must, in addition to the duplicate he transmits to the Registrar of Lands of his own district, deliver or transmit a certified copy of the deed with a list in duplicate to the Registrar of Lands at Kurunegala on or before 15 May 2026, and, since the deed must be registered in the Kurunegala division under s.14(1) of the Registration of Documents Ordinance, he has sixty days from attestation to submit it for registration under rule (30A) of s.31. If instead he yielded to the urgency and travelled to Kurunegala to attest the deed there, he would attest in an area other than that in which he is authorised to practise, contrary to rule (22) of s.31 and punishable under s.34(1)(c).

Conclusion: Yes. Saman can attest the Deed of Transfer even though the land is situated in Kurunegala, provided he attests it within the Judicial Zone of Colombo, in which his warrant authorises him to practise, and in the language of that warrant. His territorial limitation restricts where he may attest, not where the land the deed affects may be situated. He must then discharge the extra duties that follow from the land lying in another district: a certified copy and list to the Registrar of Lands of Kurunegala by the fifteenth day of the following month under rule (29) of s.31, and submission of the deed for registration at the Kurunegala land registry within sixty days of attestation under rule (30A) of s.31. He cannot attest the deed in Kurunegala itself unless the Minister first grants him a fresh warrant for that zone under s.11 of the Notaries Ordinance.
