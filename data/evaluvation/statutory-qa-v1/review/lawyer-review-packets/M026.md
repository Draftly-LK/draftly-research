## M026-Q01 (enrich, hop count 3)

### Question

What documents would you call for the examination of title and why?

### Enriched background

Tea Exports (Private) Limited, a company incorporated in Sri Lanka, needs to purchase an office building situated in Colombo, ten perches (A0, R0, P10) in extent. The premises are Assessment No. 118, Sir James Peiris Mawatha, Colombo 02, within the administrative limits of the Colombo Municipal Council and within a declared development area; the building was put up in 2013. The land is depicted as Lot 1 in Plan No. 4372 dated 12 August 2011 made by a registered licensed surveyor, and the boundaries have not been checked on the ground since. The seller is Galle Face Holdings (Private) Limited, a company incorporated in Sri Lanka, which is in possession and holds the premises as registered proprietor. Its own title is under Deed of Transfer No. 3128 attested on 4 February 2012 by a notary practising in Colombo; the only earlier instruments produced so far are Deed of Transfer No. 1476 of 1994 and Deed of Gift No. 812 of 1986, and nothing before 1986 has been shown. Deed No. 1476 of 1994 was signed for the then owner by an attorney and not by the owner himself. The premises are mortgaged to a licensed commercial bank under Mortgage Bond No. 2205 of 2019, and the seller filed particulars of that mortgage with the Registrar of Companies. Two of the five shareholders of Tea Exports (Private) Limited are foreign nationals who hold thirty-five per cent of the issued shares between them, and the agreed price of Rs. 480 million is more than half the value of the assets of Tea Exports (Private) Limited before the acquisition. The Council carried out drainage improvement works on the premises in 2022, part of the recoverable cost is still unpaid, and rates receipts have been produced only up to 2021. The land registry for Colombo keeps the land on a deeds folio, and no order has been produced bringing it under the title registration system. Completion is intended for October 2024, and the seller has produced nothing about any intended acquisition of the land by the State.

### Draft answer

Issue: What documents should the purchaser's lawyer call for to examine title before Tea Exports (Private) Limited buys the ten perch property with the office building in Colombo, and what is the reason for each. Three groups arise: documents proving the seller's title and the extent of the land, documents disclosing encumbrances and claims, and documents dealing with the corporate capacity of the two companies and with the local authority and planning position of the building.

Rule: No sale or transfer of immovable property avails in law unless the deed is in writing, signed by the executants and the witnesses in the presence of a licensed notary and attested by him, and where the transferee is a corporate body whose board cannot attend, the board must authorise a signatory in writing (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1)(a) and s.2(2)(a)). An unregistered instrument is void against a later instrument taken for valuable consideration and duly registered (Registration of Documents Ordinance s.7), registration counts only if made in the proper folio or a folio opened in continuation of it (s.14), a lis pendens does not bind a purchaser until registered (s.11), and any person interested, or his attorney-at-law, may search the books and obtain certified copies and extracts (s.42). Paper title is not conclusive, because ten years of undisturbed and uninterrupted adverse possession founds a title (Prescription Ordinance s.3). An instrument that is not duly stamped may not be registered or acted upon (Western Province Financial Statute s.60), and a plan is receivable in evidence only if prepared or certified as the Survey Act s.13 requires. Where a company creates a charge on land it must deliver the instrument to the Registrar for registration (Companies Act s.102), and registration is notice of the particulars of the charge to all persons (s.105). A certificate of incorporation is conclusive evidence of incorporation (s.5), and a company may not enter into a major transaction, which includes acquiring assets worth more than half the value of its assets before the acquisition, except by special resolution, unanimous written shareholder consent or express authority in its articles (s.185). Transfer of land to a company incorporated in Sri Lanka is prohibited where direct or indirect foreign shareholding is fifty per cent or above, unless an exemption in s.3 applies (Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act s.2). A building in a Municipality may not be occupied until the Council certifies conformity (Municipal Councils Ordinance s.127), council expenses of the kind described are a first charge on the premises payable by successors in title (s.152), development activity in a declared development area requires a permit (Urban Development Authority Law s.8J), and land may be under notice of intended acquisition given to the owner (Land Acquisition Act s.4). Where land lies in an area declared under the Registration of Title Act s.1, the Certificate of Title under s.37 replaces the deed chain, and a registered power of attorney is required for a deed signed by an attorney (Powers of Attorney Ordinance s.3).

Application: Since the land is on a deeds folio in Colombo and not in a declared title registration area, the deed chain governs. The seller has produced only three instruments, the latest being Deed of Transfer No. 3128 of 2012 and the earliest Deed of Gift No. 812 of 1986; earlier deeds, or their registered duplicates or certified registry copies, must be called for so that the chain runs back well beyond any period in which a possessory title could have been perfected, and each deed must be inspected for the notarial attestation and for the stamp or endorsement of payment. A certified extract of the land registry folio and of every folio connected to it by cross reference must be called for, because that alone will show the mortgage bond of 2019, any other registered instrument taking priority, and any registered lis pendens. Because Deed No. 1476 of 1994 was signed by an attorney, the power of attorney, its registration particulars and evidence that it was unrevoked at that date must be produced. Plan No. 4372 of 2011 and a certified copy must be called for and, since the boundaries have not been checked on the ground, a fresh survey by a registered licensed surveyor is prudent to confirm that the ten perches match the description in the deeds and that the building stands within the boundaries. Possession must be proved independently of the deeds by rates and assessment receipts, utility bills and the tenancy or lease records for the office building. Because the seller is a company, certified copies of its certificate of incorporation, articles, directors' particulars and the resolution authorising the sale must be called for, a search made of the Registrar's register of charges, and the certificate of registration of the 2019 charge, the mortgage bond and the bank's letter agreeing to release the mortgage on payment obtained. On the purchaser's side, the certificate of incorporation, the articles, the share register and the latest annual return must be produced to show that direct and indirect foreign shareholding stands at thirty-five per cent and so below the prohibited level, and since the price of Rs. 480 million exceeds half the value of the company's assets, the special resolution or written consent of all shareholders approving the acquisition, together with the written board authority for the signatory to the transfer, must be in hand before execution. As the building was put up in 2013 within the Municipality and a declared development area, the approved building plan, the development permit and the certificate of conformity must be called for, along with a council statement of rates and other charges, which will disclose the unpaid drainage works expenses and the gap in rates receipts after 2021, and confirmation about street lines and building limits. Confirmation must also be obtained that no notice of intended acquisition affects the land, since none has been produced.

Conclusion: The purchaser's lawyer should call for, and give the following reasons for: the seller's deed and the prior deeds or their registered duplicates and certified registry copies, going back far enough to exclude a possessory claim, because only a notarially executed and duly registered chain gives priority; a certified extract of the land registry folio and connected folios, covering registered instruments and lis pendens, because registration decides priority and a registered pending action binds a purchaser; the power of attorney for the 1994 deed; the survey plan, a certified copy and if necessary a fresh survey, because only a properly certified plan is receivable in evidence and the extent must match the deeds; possessory documents, being rates receipts, utility bills and tenancy records, because ten years of adverse possession can defeat a paper title; stamp endorsements and receipts on every deed, because an unstamped instrument cannot be registered or acted upon; the seller company's certificate of incorporation, articles, directors' particulars and authorising resolution, a search of the Registrar's register of charges and the release of the registered mortgage, because registration of a charge is notice to all persons; the purchaser company's certificate of incorporation, articles, share register and annual return, because a transfer to a company with fifty per cent or more foreign shareholding is prohibited, together with the special resolution or unanimous written shareholder consent for what is a major transaction and the written board authority for the signatory; and the approved building plan, development permit, certificate of conformity, council rates and charges statement, street line and building limit confirmation, and confirmation that no acquisition proceeding affects the land. Title should not be accepted until the outstanding council expenses are settled or retained from the price and the mortgage is released on completion.
