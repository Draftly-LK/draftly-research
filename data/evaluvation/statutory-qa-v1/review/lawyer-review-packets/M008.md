### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Sureka, a Notary holding no written authority from Kamal, may inspect the title register and the cadastral map maintained under the Registration of Title Act, and whether she is entitled to certified copies of those documents and from whom.

Rule: Registration of Title Act s.34(1) provides that any person may, on application made in the prescribed form and on payment of the prescribed fee, inspect during office hours any title register and any cadastral map maintained under the Act. Section 34(2) entitles any person, on application made to the relevant Registrar of Title and on payment of the prescribed fee, to receive an extract of the Title Register certified by that Registrar of Title. Section 34(3) entitles any person, on application made to the relevant Superintendent of Surveys and on payment of the prescribed fee, to receive a copy of an extract of the cadastral map used for registration of title, certified on behalf of the Surveyor-General. Section 75 defines 'Registrar of Title' as any person appointed as such under the Act, 'Superintendent of Surveys' as any person performing those functions in the Survey Department, and 'Title Register' as the register under the Act, which fixes who the two custodians are. The forms and fees referred to are those prescribed by regulations made under s.67(2)(g) and (h). By s.35(1) a document purporting to be certified by a Registrar of Title is presumed to have been so certified and is accepted as prima facie evidence of the matter to which it relates without further proof.

Application: Section 34 confers each of these rights on 'any person'. It does not require the applicant to be the owner, a claimant, or the agent of one, and it calls for no proof of interest or written authority. Sureka is a person within s.34, so the fact that Kamal has given her no written authority or power of attorney, and that she is not herself a claimant to Lot 42, does not stand in her way; she may apply whether or not she is described as acting for Kamal. What she must do is apply in the prescribed form, pay the prescribed fee and attend during office hours, all of which she is willing to do. For the certified extract of the register relating to Lot 42 she must apply to the relevant Registrar of Title, that is the Registrar at the Homagama Title Registry, who certifies it. For the certified copy of an extract of the cadastral map she must apply not to that Registrar but to the relevant Superintendent of Surveys, here the Superintendent of Surveys for the Colombo District, and the copy is certified on behalf of the Surveyor-General.

Conclusion: Yes on both counts. Sureka may inspect the title register and the cadastral map under s.34(1) on application in the prescribed form, payment of the prescribed fee and during office hours, and she needs no authority from Kamal because the right belongs to any person. She is also entitled to certified copies, but from two different officers: a certified extract of the Title Register from the relevant Registrar of Title under s.34(2), and a certified copy of an extract of the cadastral map from the relevant Superintendent of Surveys, certified on behalf of the Surveyor-General, under s.34(3). Each is subject to payment of the prescribed fee. A certified document of the first kind is prima facie evidence of what it relates to under s.35. The prescribed forms and the amounts of the fees are set by regulations, which are not reproduced here.
