### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Martin Perera may execute a last will devising his house to his helper Siripala while leaving nothing to his two adult children, without stating any reason for excluding them, and on what statutory conditions the validity of such a will depends.

Rule: Wills Ordinance s.2(1) makes it lawful for any person who has reached the age of eighteen years and is residing within or outside Sri Lanka to execute a will disposing of movable and immovable property belonging to him at the time of death which would otherwise devolve on his heirs, in favour of a person not legally incapacitated from taking. Wills Ordinance s.2(2) gives every testator full power to make such testamentary disposition as he feels disposed and, in the exercise of that right, to exclude any child, parent, relative or descendant, or to disinherit or omit to mention any such person, without assigning any reason, notwithstanding any law, usage or custom to the contrary. The preferential right of children in Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance s.24 sits in the Part of that Ordinance dealing with inheritance ab intestato and so operates only over property not validly disposed of by will. Freedom of testation must, however, be exercised in the prescribed form: under Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.4 no will containing a devise of immovable property or a bequest of movable property is valid unless it is in writing and the testator signs it and affixes his thumb impression at the foot or end before a notary public and two witnesses present at the same time. Under Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.11 a benefit given by the will to a person who attests it, or to that person's spouse, is null and void so far as concerns that attesting witness, although the will itself remains valid. Wills Ordinance s.9 confirms that landed property in the Ordinance extends to houses and buildings.

Application: Martin is seventy-two, of sound mind and under no disability, so he satisfies the age and capacity condition in s.2(1), and he is governed by the general law rather than by Kandyan law, Thesawalamai or Muslim law. The house at No. 47/3, Temple Lane, Nugegoda is his own immovable property, held as sole owner, and it would devolve on Anusha and Dinesh if he did not dispose of it; Siripala, an unrelated domestic helper, is not legally incapacitated from taking it. His decision to leave the children nothing, and to give no explanation for that in the will, falls squarely within the words of s.2(2), and because the children's preferential right in Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance s.24 applies only on intestacy, they have no statutory claim against a will that disposes of the house. Two conditions must nevertheless be met. The will must be in writing and Martin must sign it and place his thumb impression at the end of it before a notary public and two witnesses present at the same time, as Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.4 requires. The attesting witnesses must not be beneficiaries or the spouses of beneficiaries, which the proposed neighbours are not; had Siripala or his wife attested the will, Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.11 would render the devise of the house to him null and void. Nothing in the facts shows whether the children are in need of support, and no maintenance claim against the estate arises from the provisions relied on here.

Conclusion: Yes. Martin Perera may execute the last will leaving the house to Siripala and giving his children nothing, and he need not assign any reason for excluding them, because Wills Ordinance s.2(2) confers that power in terms and notwithstanding any contrary law, usage or custom. The exclusion cannot be attacked merely because no reason is given. The advice is qualified only by the requirement that the will be executed in the manner required by Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.4 and that neither Siripala nor his spouse attest it, failing which the will or the devise to him would fail on grounds unconnected with the absence of reasons.

### Draft answer

Issue: What steps Nihal, as the executor named in Martin Perera's last will and the person who found the original will, is required to take after Martin's death in order to obtain probate and to administer and wind up the estate.

Rule: Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.9 requires that a will be duly proved and recorded after the testator's death in the District Court empowered to grant probate. Civil Procedure Code s.516 requires the person in whose custody the will was, or who finds it after the death, to produce it to the District Court of the district in which he resides or in which the testator died, as soon as reasonably may be, with an affidavit verifying the time and place of death and the nature and value of the property left. Under s.517 the person appointed executor may apply to the District Court of the district in which he resides, in which the testator resided at death, or in which land of the estate is situate, within the time limit and in the manner specified in s.524, to have the will proved and probate granted to him; s.518 shows the process is not optional, since if no application is made the court itself proceeds to grant probate to the named executor or administration to another competent person. Section 524 requires the application to be made within three months of the finding of the will, by petition and affidavit setting out in numbered paragraphs the making of the will, the details and situation of the property, the heirs to the best of the petitioner's knowledge, the grounds of entitlement and the character claimed, with the will annexed or brought into court, evidence proving due execution, and the charges for the notice under s.529. Section 529 requires the Probate Officer to publish notice in Sinhala, Tamil and English calling for written objections supported by affidavit. If no objection is filed, s.531(1)(a)(i) requires the court, on prima facie proof of the due making of the will and the petitioner's character, to declare the will proved, declare the applicant executor and direct the grant of probate; if objections are filed, issues are framed and under s.534 probate is granted if the prima facie proof is not rebutted. Section 539 then requires the executor to take the oath of an executor within fifteen days of the order and to file an inventory and valuation within one month of the oath, whereupon probate issues. By s.542 an unlimited grant carries power over the whole of the deceased's movable and immovable property in Sri Lanka. Section 551 requires a true and final account of the executorship, verified on oath with receipts and vouchers, within twelve months of the issue of probate or such further time as the court allows, and s.552 makes an executor who fails to pay over what is due within a year of the grant personally liable for interest.

Application: Nihal found the original will in Martin's safe on 17 February 2026, three days after the death, so he is the finder within the meaning of s.516 and must produce the will to the District Court of Colombo, being both the district in which Martin died and the district in which Nihal resides, as soon as reasonably may be, supporting it with an affidavit of the time and place of death and of the house and bank balance left within that jurisdiction. As the executor named in the will he should then petition the same court under ss.517 and 524 by 17 May 2026, within three months of finding the will, setting out the making of the will of 20 August 2024, the details of the house at No. 47/3 on Lot A in Plan No. 2214 and of the savings account, the heirs Anusha and Dinesh as well as the legatee Siripala, the ground on which he claims and his character as executor, annexing the will and affidavit evidence of the notary and attesting witnesses to prove due execution, and tendering the charges for publication. The Probate Officer will publish the notice under s.529 and the children may file written objections supported by affidavit within the period stated in it. If they do not, the court will declare the will proved and direct that probate issue to Nihal under s.531; if they do object, the matter goes to a final hearing on framed issues and probate follows under s.534 unless the prima facie proof is rebutted. Within fifteen days of that order Nihal must take the executor's oath, and within one month of the oath file the inventory and valuation of the house, the bank balance and the debts under s.539, on which probate will be issued to him. Holding an unlimited grant he may then get in the bank balance, pay the funeral expenses and the hospital bill of about Rs. 350,000, transfer the house at No. 47/3 to Siripala in terms of the will, and file his final account within twelve months of the grant under s.551, remembering that under s.552 delay beyond a year in paying over what is due exposes him to interest out of his own funds. Whether an extension of time will be needed, and what duty is payable on the estate, cannot be settled on the facts given.

Conclusion: Nihal must produce the will to the District Court of Colombo with the prescribed affidavit as soon as reasonably may be after the death, and within three months of finding it petition that court by petition and affidavit to have the will proved and probate granted to him, allow the Probate Officer's notice to be published and any objections to be dealt with, and on the order declaring the will proved take the executor's oath within fifteen days and file the inventory and valuation within a month of the oath. Once probate issues he must administer the estate, pay the debts, give Siripala the house devised to him and file a final account within twelve months, failing which he risks personal liability for interest.
