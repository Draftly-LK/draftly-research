### Question

Advise Wimala according to the latest amendments relating to the Prevention of frauds Ordinance No 7 of 1840.

### Enriched background

On 12 November 2025 Wimala proposes to sign, at the Sri Lanka High Commission in Canberra and before a consular officer there, a written instrument appointing Sunil to act for her in the purchase. She has asked a notary practising in Galle what she must do so that the deed she cannot sign will still be good in law.

### Draft answer

Issue: Wimala is the purchaser, and therefore the transferee, under a proposed deed of transfer of land at Galle. She cannot be in Sri Lanka when the deed is executed. The issues are whether a transferee must now sign the deed at all, what she must do if she cannot be present, how the instrument by which she authorises another person must be executed abroad and what it must contain, whether that instrument must be registered, and what the attesting notary must satisfy himself of.

Rule: Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1) requires that no sale, purchase or transfer of immovable property is in force or avails in law unless the deed is in writing, signed by every executant or by any person duly authorised by such executant and by the witnesses in the presence of a licensed notary public, all present at the same time and in the presence of one another, and attested by that notary, and unless the thumb impression of every executant is affixed beside the signature on the original, duplicate and protocol in the presence of the notary and the witnesses. Section 2(2), added by the Prevention of Frauds (Amendment) Act No. 4 of 2024, provides that in relation to a transfer deed both the transferor and the transferee must affix their signatures and thumb impressions as required by subsection (1), but that where the transferee is unable to be present and execute the deed she shall authorise in writing any other person to sign on her behalf, that person complying with the requirements of subsection (1). Where such an authorisation is granted for a transaction within s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance, Powers of Attorney Ordinance s.3A(1) requires it to be executed subject to s.3A(2) or (3). If it is executed outside Sri Lanka, s.3A(3) requires that it be executed before a person authorised under s.2 of that Ordinance, that it describe the land by metes and bounds and extent, that it refer to the volume and folio of any prior registration, and that it be accompanied by a copy of the bio page of the grantor's passport signed by the grantor and by notarially certified identity documents of the attorney. Powers of Attorney Ordinance s.2 defines a power of attorney executed outside Sri Lanka as one executed before an Ambassador, a High Commissioner, a Diplomatic Officer or a Consular Officer within the meaning of the Consular Functions Act, or a person authorised to attest such an instrument under the law of that country. Section 3 requires every power of attorney to be registered with the Registrar-General, the holder submitting it within three months of execution where it was executed outside Sri Lanka, with a notarially certified copy and the affidavit in Schedule I. Section 3C limits validity to five years from execution unless the instrument states its own period or it is earlier revoked. Section 3B requires the notary attesting the deed to examine the relevant volumes and folios at the Registrar-General's department, to satisfy himself that the power has not been revoked or cancelled and to state that in his attestation. Notaries Ordinance s.31, rule (16)(c), requires the attorney signing a deed to give the notary an affidavit that the power of attorney is genuine and in force and that the grantor is alive at execution, and rule (30) requires the notary to keep a true copy of the power with his protocol and to forward a copy with the duplicate to the Registrar of Lands.

Application: Wimala's purchase of the Galle land is a purchase and transfer of immovable property and so falls squarely within Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1). Since the 2024 amendment she cannot simply leave the deed to the vendor: s.2(2)(a) makes her, as transferee, a signatory who must also place a thumb impression, and the vendor Nihal Weerakoon, being in Sri Lanka, can attend and comply for his part. Because her employment prevents her from travelling before March 2026, she is a transferee 'unable to be present and execute the deed' and the proviso to s.2(2)(a) applies: she must authorise her brother Sunil in writing to sign for her, and it will be Sunil who signs, gives his thumb impression and appears before the notary and the two witnesses as s.2(1) requires. That written authority is a power of attorney granted for a s.2 transaction, so s.3A(1) of the Powers of Attorney Ordinance applies, and because she will sign it in Canberra, s.3A(3) governs. Signing at the Sri Lanka High Commission before a consular officer satisfies s.3A(3)(a) read with s.2(b). The instrument must set out Kahatagahawatta by its four boundaries and its extent of eighteen decimal five perches, must refer to Volume 214 Folio 85 of the Galle Land Registry as the prior registration, and must be accompanied by the bio page of her passport signed by her and by a notarially certified copy of Sunil's identity document. Once executed on 12 November 2025 it must be lodged by Sunil with the Registrar-General within three months, that is by 12 February 2026, together with a certified true copy and the Schedule I affidavit; since the deed is to be executed in the first week of December 2025 the authority should in practice be registered before then, because the notary must be able to examine the volumes and folios and certify under s.3B that it has not been revoked. The authority will still be current at that date, being well within the five-year period allowed by s.3C. At execution Sunil must give the notary the affidavit required by Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (16)(c) confirming that the power is genuine and in force and that Wimala is alive, and the notary will keep a true copy of the power with his protocol and send a copy with the duplicate to the Registrar of Lands under rule (30). Wimala being a citizen of Sri Lanka, nothing in Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act s.2(1) stands in the way of the transfer.

Conclusion: Wimala can complete the purchase without travelling. She should execute, before a consular officer at the Sri Lanka High Commission in Canberra, a written power of attorney appointing her brother Sunil to sign the deed of transfer for her, drawn so as to describe the Galle land by metes and bounds and extent and to refer to Volume 214 Folio 85, and accompanied by her signed passport bio page and Sunil's certified identity document. Sunil must register it with the Registrar-General within three months of execution, and in practice before the deed is executed, and must then sign the deed, affix his thumb impression and appear with the two witnesses before the notary, giving the notary the affidavit that the power is genuine, in force and that Wimala is alive. A deed executed in that way is in force and avails in law under Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2 as amended by Act No. 4 of 2024. If instead she simply stays away and lets the vendor alone sign, the deed will not satisfy s.2(2)(a) and will not avail in law. The precise date of operation of the 2024 amendment should be confirmed before the advice is acted on.
