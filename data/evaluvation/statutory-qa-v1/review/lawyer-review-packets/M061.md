### Draft answer

Issue: The question is how title to Lot A, allotted to Sujeewa Jayamini by the final decree of 20 October 2001, passed by Deed of Gift No.1100 and then through three successive intestacies (Dinesh Gamage in 2009, Neesha Gamage in 2012 and Kasun Gamage in 2015) to the persons who now own the land and house, and how that chain is set out as a pedigree. A subsidiary issue is whether the general law of intestate succession applies to this family.

Rule: Inheritance ab intestato to immovable property in Sri Lanka is governed by Part III of the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance No. 15 of 1876 (s.21), "immovable property" including land and things attached to the earth (s.3), and Part III applying to the estate of anyone dying after the proclamation of the Ordinance (s.20). The Ordinance does not apply to Kandyans, Muslims or Tamils of the Northern Province subject to the Thesawalamai (s.2); nothing in the facts brings any of these persons within those systems, and the analysis proceeds on the general law. A surviving spouse inherits one-half of the estate of the intestate (s.22), and the remaining half devolves in the order descendants, ascendants, collaterals (s.23). Children and remoter descendants are preferent to all others, all children taking equally per capita and the issue of a predeceased child taking per stirpes by representation (s.24). Where descendants fail and only one parent is alive, the surviving parent takes half and the full brothers and sisters, together with the half-brothers and sisters related through the deceased parent and the issue of any of them by representation, take the other half (s.25). Where descendants fail and both parents have failed, the estate goes to the brothers and sisters, whether of the whole or half blood, and their issue by representation (s.26); and where all the half-brothers and sisters are related to the intestate on one side only, they take the whole, unless a grandparent or higher ascendant on the other side is alive (s.29). Persons equally near in degree take per capita and not per stirpes (s.30). A deed transferring an interest in immovable property must be notarially executed to avail in law (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2).

Application: Sujeewa Jayamini became sole owner of Lot A under the final decree of 20 October 2001. By Deed of Gift No.1100 of 12 January 2006, notarially attested as the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2 requires, she gifted Lot A to her son Dinesh Gamage absolutely and irrevocably, reserving a life interest to herself; the gift therefore vested ownership in Dinesh at once, burdened only by that life interest, and her husband Thilak Gamage and her other son Iresh took no interest in Lot A. On her death on 21 April 2007 the reserved life interest came to an end and Dinesh held the entirety free of it. Dinesh died intestate on 23 November 2009 owning the whole of Lot A, which is immovable property within Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance s.3, so ss.21 to 30 govern. His widow Lochana took one-half under s.22. Under s.23 and s.24 the other half went to his descendants, who at that date were four children, Neesha (his daughter by his first wife Nimali), Kasun, Kavindu and Piyumi; being equally near in degree they took per capita under s.30, that is one-quarter of the half, or one-eighth of the land, each. Neesha died intestate on 20 May 2012 leaving her husband Rohan Jayaweera but no children. Under s.22 Rohan took one-half of her one-eighth. Her descendants having failed and both her parents having predeceased her (Dinesh on 23 November 2009 and Nimali on 15 July 2010), the other half of her share passed under s.26 to her brothers and sisters. Her only collaterals were Kasun, Kavindu and Piyumi, half-siblings related to her on her father's side alone, so under s.29 they took the whole of that collateral half, per capita under s.30, no grandparent or higher ascendant on the mother's side being disclosed by the facts. Kasun Gamage died on 5 July 2015 unmarried and issueless, holding his original one-eighth together with the share he had taken from Neesha. He left neither spouse nor descendants; his father Dinesh had predeceased him and his mother Lochana survived him. Under s.25 Lochana as the surviving parent took half of his share, and the other half went to his full brother Kavindu and his full sister Piyumi, equally under s.30. Neesha, who would otherwise have been a half-sister on the father's side, had already died in 2012 without issue, so no share fell to her or to anyone representing her.

Conclusion: The pedigree runs: Sujeewa Jayamini (owner under the final decree of 20 October 2001, married to Thilak Gamage, children Dinesh and Iresh) gifted Lot A by Deed of Gift No.1100 of 12 January 2006 to Dinesh Gamage subject to her life interest, which determined on her death on 21 April 2007, leaving Dinesh sole owner. Dinesh died intestate on 23 November 2009, and Lot A devolved on his widow Lochana and his four children Neesha, Kasun, Kavindu and Piyumi. Neesha died intestate on 20 May 2012 without children, and her interest devolved on her husband Rohan Jayaweera and on her half-siblings Kasun, Kavindu and Piyumi. Kasun died intestate on 5 July 2015 unmarried and issueless, and his interest devolved on his mother Lochana and his full siblings Kavindu and Piyumi. The present owners are therefore Lochana Gamage, Kavindu Gamage, Piyumi Gamage and Rohan Jayaweera. This devolution assumes that the general law of the Ordinance, and not Kandyan law, Muslim law or the Thesawalamai, governs the family, and that no maternal grandparent of Neesha survived her for the purposes of the proviso to s.29.

Issue: The question is what undivided fraction of Lot A, the land and premises at Assessment No.12, Rajamalwatta Road, Panagoda, in extent 20 perches, each surviving heir presently owns after the three intestacies of Dinesh Gamage, Neesha Gamage and Kasun Gamage.

Rule: A surviving spouse inherits one-half of the intestate's property (Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance s.22) and, subject to that half, the estate devolves first on descendants (s.23). Children take equally per capita (s.24), and persons equally near in degree take per capita and not per stirpes (s.30). Where descendants fail and both parents have failed, the estate goes to brothers and sisters of the whole or half blood and their issue by representation (s.26); where all the half-siblings are related to the intestate on one side only they take the whole, unless an ascendant on the other side survives (s.29). Where descendants fail and only one parent survives, that parent takes half and the full brothers and sisters, together with the half-siblings related through the deceased parent and their issue by representation, take the other half (s.25). The Ordinance governs intestate succession to immovable property in Sri Lanka (s.21, s.3) and does not apply to Kandyans, Muslims or Tamils of the Northern Province subject to the Thesawalamai (s.2).

Application: Dinesh Gamage owned the whole of Lot A when he died intestate on 23 November 2009. His widow Lochana took one-half under s.22. The other half went under ss.23, 24 and 30 to his four children then living, Neesha, Kasun, Kavindu and Piyumi, that is one-eighth of the land each. On Neesha's intestacy on 20 May 2012 her one-eighth split under s.22 as to one-half, namely one-sixteenth of the land, to her husband Rohan Jayaweera; the balance one-sixteenth passed under s.26 to her collaterals, who were only her paternal half-siblings, so under s.29 they took that whole one-sixteenth and under s.30 divided it equally, one forty-eighth each to Kasun, Kavindu and Piyumi. Each of the three then held one-eighth plus one forty-eighth, that is seven forty-eighths. Kasun died on 5 July 2015 unmarried and issueless holding seven forty-eighths. His father having predeceased him and his mother surviving, s.25 gave Lochana half of seven forty-eighths, namely seven ninety-sixths, and gave his full siblings Kavindu and Piyumi the other half in equal shares, namely seven one hundred and ninety-seconds each. Lochana therefore holds one-half plus seven ninety-sixths. Kavindu and Piyumi each hold seven forty-eighths plus seven one hundred and ninety-seconds. Rohan Jayaweera holds the one-sixteenth he took in 2012. Expressed over a common denominator of 192, Lochana holds 110/192, Kavindu 35/192, Piyumi 35/192 and Rohan 12/192, which together account for the whole.

Conclusion: The present shares in Lot A are: Lochana Gamage 55/96 (110/192); Kavindu Gamage 35/192; Piyumi Gamage 35/192; and Rohan Jayaweera 1/16 (12/192). These fractions total unity. They assume that the general law applies to this family rather than Kandyan law, Muslim law or the Thesawalamai, and that no maternal grandparent or higher maternal ascendant of Neesha Gamage was alive on 20 May 2012, since the proviso to s.29 would otherwise halve the portion taken by her paternal half-siblings.
