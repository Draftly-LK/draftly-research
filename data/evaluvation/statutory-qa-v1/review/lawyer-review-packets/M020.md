## M020-Q01 (enrich, hop count 4)

### Question

Discuss as to how all parties can sign the said Deed of partition.

### Enriched background

There are 4 parties in a Deed of partition of a property in Tangalle to be executed before a Notary who has a license to Practise in Colombo. One party to the deed of partition resides in Tangalle and is unable to come to Colombo. However, the other three parties are residing in Colombo.

The four parties are Ranjan Weerakoon, Malini Weerakoon, Suranga Weerakoon and Kanthi Weerakoon, the children of the late H. P. Weerakoon, who took the property in undivided one-fourth shares on his death in 2019. Ranjan, Malini and Suranga live at Kirula Road, Colombo 05, and Kanthi lives at the property itself.

The property is an allotment of residential land of thirty-two perches in extent called Muhudu Watta, bearing Assessment No. 118, Beliatta Road, Tangalle, within the Tangalle Urban Council limits in the Hambantota District. It carries a single dwelling house and a small coconut garden and is not a tea or rubber estate. Title is registered in Volume 214 Folio 63 of the Tangalle Land Registry, and the land is not subject to any mortgage or lease. The parties have agreed the four lots into which it is to be divided on a survey plan prepared in August 2025.

The Notary instructed is Mr. A. D. Jayasuriya, whose warrant authorises him to practise as a Notary in the Sinhala language within the Colombo judicial zone and whose office is at Ward Place, Colombo 07. Tangalle is not within that zone.

Kanthi Weerakoon is 78 years old, has been bedridden since a hip fracture in June 2025, and her physician has advised against any travel for at least six months. Mr. S. Kumarasiri is a Notary whose warrant authorises him to practise in the Sinhala language within the judicial zone that includes Tangalle; he practises from an office in Tangalle town and is willing to attend at Kanthi's residence. Kanthi is also willing, if it is more convenient, to authorise her nephew Nuwan Weerakoon of Colombo 05 to sign on her behalf; no such authority has yet been given. All four parties want the deed signed and completed during October 2025, and each of them holds a current national identity card.

### Draft answer

Issue: The issue is how the signatures of all four co-owners can lawfully be placed on a single deed of partition of land at Tangalle when the notary instructed holds a warrant limited to the Colombo judicial zone, three of the parties are in Colombo, and the fourth is in Tangalle and cannot travel. The sub-issues are whether the Colombo notary may attest in Tangalle, whether the parties may sign at different times and places, whether the absent party may sign through an authorised person, and whether execution before a public officer is an alternative.

Rule: A deed of partition affects immovable property, so Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1)(a) governs its form: it must be in writing and signed by every executant, or by any person duly authorised by such executant, and by the witnesses in the presence of a licensed notary public present at the same time and in the presence of one another, and attested by that notary. Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (12) states the same requirement as a duty of the notary. A notary's warrant runs only within the judicial zone in which he resides (Notaries Ordinance s.3(1), extended from division to zone by s.4A), and by s.31 rule (22) he shall not attest any deed in any area other than that in which he is authorised to practise; a breach is an offence carrying a fine of up to fifty thousand rupees besides civil liability (s.34(1)(c)), and s.33 does not save a breach of another written law. The Ordinance nevertheless allows a deed to be completed piecemeal. Section 32(2) applies where a deed to be executed by two or more parties is not signed by all of them at the same time and place, and s.31 rule (25) governs a deed executed or acknowledged before more than one notary: the notary who first attests complies with the whole of rule (20) and keeps the protocol, every other attesting notary complies with paragraphs (a) to (e) of rule (20), numbers the deed under rule (23) and keeps a certified copy as his protocol. By rule (28) the duplicate is transmitted by the notary who first attests, and by rule (29), the land lying in another district, a certified copy must also go to the Registrar of Lands of the district in which the land is situated. Rule (27)(c)(ii) means the deed should be completed within three months of the first signature. As an alternative, s.2(1)(a) of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance permits signature by a person duly authorised by the executant. Such authority must be a power of attorney within Powers of Attorney Ordinance s.2, executed before two witnesses and attested by a notary; because it is granted for a transaction within s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance it must also satisfy s.3A(2), namely notarial attestation in accordance with the Notaries Ordinance and the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance, a description of the land with metes and bounds and extent, the volume and folio of prior registration, the grantor's thumb impression and copies of identity documents. It must be registered with the Registrar-General within one month of execution (s.3), the attesting notary must satisfy himself that it has not been revoked or cancelled and say so in the attestation (s.3B(1)(b)), the attorney must give the notary an affidavit that the power is genuine, in force and the grantor alive (Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (15A)(c)), and the notary must keep a true copy with his protocol and forward a copy with the duplicate (rule (30)). A third route lies outside the Notaries Ordinance: by s.2 of the Deeds and Documents (Execution before Public Officers) Ordinance a deed which s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance would otherwise require to be notarially executed is valid if signed by the party, or by a person lawfully authorised by him, and by two or more witnesses present at the same time, before the District Judge for the district in which that party resides, and certified by that officer, subject to the conditions in s.4 and the duplicate and registration requirements of s.3.

Application: The deed is fully engrossed before any signature is taken, so signing may begin. Ranjan, Malini and Suranga are in Colombo and can attend Mr. Jayasuriya's office; they and two witnesses sign there in his presence and in the presence of one another, and he attests under s.31 rule (20), which satisfies Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1)(a) and s.31 rule (12) as to those three executants. Kanthi cannot come to Colombo and, being bedridden on medical advice for six months, cannot be waited for. Mr. Jayasuriya cannot go to her, because his warrant is confined to the Colombo judicial zone and Tangalle is outside it, so attesting there would breach s.31 rule (22) and expose him to the penalty in s.34(1)(c). Three lawful courses remain. First, the same deed may be taken to Tangalle and Kanthi's signature obtained at her residence before Mr. Kumarasiri, whose warrant covers that zone, with two witnesses signing in his presence and in the presence of one another; s.32(2) accommodates the different time and place, and s.31 rule (25) tells each notary what to do, Mr. Jayasuriya as the first attesting notary complying with the whole of rule (20) and keeping the protocol and Mr. Kumarasiri complying with paragraphs (a) to (e), numbering the deed in his own series and keeping a certified copy as his protocol. Mr. Jayasuriya then transmits the duplicate under rule (28), and because the land is in the Hambantota District a certified copy goes to that district's Registrar of Lands under rule (29). Second, Kanthi may grant her nephew Nuwan a power of attorney answering Powers of Attorney Ordinance s.2 and s.3A(2), which itself must be attested by a notary and can be attested where she is by a notary authorised for that area, register it with the Registrar-General within one month under s.3, whereupon Nuwan signs the partition deed in Colombo before Mr. Jayasuriya, who must verify under s.3B(1)(b) that the power stands unrevoked and state that in his attestation, take the attorney's affidavit under s.31 rule (15A)(c), and keep and forward copies of the power under rule (30). Third, Kanthi may execute the deed before the District Judge for the district in which she resides under s.2 of the Deeds and Documents (Execution before Public Officers) Ordinance, with two witnesses present at the same time and the deed read over and explained and the other conditions of s.4 met, the duplicate then being transmitted for registration under s.3. On any of the three courses the parties should complete all signatures well within three months of the first, as rule (27)(c)(ii) contemplates, and they intend to finish within October 2025.

Conclusion: All four parties can sign the deed of partition without the Colombo notary leaving his zone and without the Tangalle party travelling. The preferred course is for the three Colombo parties to sign before Mr. Jayasuriya in Colombo and for Kanthi to sign the same deed before Mr. Kumarasiri at Tangalle, the deed being executed before two notaries at different times and places under Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (25) and s.32(2), with Mr. Jayasuriya as first attesting notary keeping the protocol and transmitting the duplicate under rule (28) and sending a certified copy to the Registrar of Lands for the district in which the land lies under rule (29). Failing that, Kanthi may sign through an attorney under a power of attorney complying with Powers of Attorney Ordinance s.2, s.3A(2), s.3 and s.3B, used in accordance with Notaries Ordinance s.31 rules (15A)(c) and (30), or she may execute before the District Judge for her district under s.2 of the Deeds and Documents (Execution before Public Officers) Ordinance. What the parties cannot do is have Mr. Jayasuriya attest her signature at Tangalle, or have her sign out of the presence of an attesting notary and witnesses, since either course would breach Notaries Ordinance s.31 rules (22) and (12) and, in the second case, deprive the deed of the form required by Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1)(a). The availability of the District Judge route depends on Ordinance No. 17 of 1852 remaining unrepealed, which this analysis has not been able to confirm.
