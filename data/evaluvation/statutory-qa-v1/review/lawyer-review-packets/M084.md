## M084-Q01 (enrich, hop count 3)

### Question

Could the Notary attest the deed?

### Enriched background

Mr. K. A. Dharmasena is a notary public authorised to practise in Sinhala in the Colombo judicial zone. On 12 October 2019 Mr. Sunil Rajapaksha comes to his office and asks him to attest a deed of transfer, drawn by the notary himself, by which Mr. Rajapaksha transfers to Ms. Nirmali Fernando an allotment of land at Maharagama; Mr. Rajapaksha produces the title deed by which he bought the land. He brings two persons with him to sign as attesting witnesses. The Notary does not know the executant but he is well known to one of the witnesses. This witness is the Notary's own brother, Mr. P. A. Dharmasena, a retired schoolmaster of good repute who has lived next door to Mr. Rajapaksha for many years and can state his full name, his occupation and his address. The second witness is a clerk from the shop next to the notary's office who had never met Mr. Rajapaksha before that morning and cannot say what his occupation or his residence is. Mr. Rajapaksha produces no identity card, passport or driving licence. The notary is not a party to the deed and has no interest in the land.

### Draft answer

Issue: Whether the notary may attest this deed of transfer when he does not himself know the executant, when only one of the two attesting witnesses is well acquainted with the executant, and when that witness is the notary's own brother.

Rule: At October 2019 rule (9) of section 31 of the Notaries Ordinance provided that a notary shall not authenticate or attest any deed or instrument unless the person executing the same be known to him or to at least two of the attesting witnesses thereto; those words are recited as the words replaced by section 16(6) of the Notaries (Amendment) Act, No. 31 of 2022, and the replacing words, which allow identification by inspection of a national identity card, passport bio-page or driving licence, took effect only in 2022 and do not govern this deed. Where the notary relies on the witnesses rather than on his own knowledge, the same rule (9) requires him to satisfy himself, before accepting them as witnesses, that they are persons of good repute and that they are well acquainted with the executant and know his proper name, occupation and residence, and requires the witnesses to sign a declaration at the foot of the deed to that effect; the requirement is stated of the witnesses in the plural, and rule (8) of section 31 requires at least two witnesses to subscribe their signatures in letters. Section 31 imposes only one personal bar on the notary himself: by rule (13) he shall not authenticate or attest any deed or instrument to which he is a party. No rule in section 31 disqualifies an attesting witness by reason of his relationship to the notary.

Application: The notary does not know Mr. Rajapaksha, so he can proceed only on the witness limb of rule (9), which needs the executant to be known to at least two of the attesting witnesses. Only the brother, Mr. P. A. Dharmasena, is well acquainted with Mr. Rajapaksha and able to state his proper name, occupation and residence; the second witness had never met him before that morning and cannot state his occupation or residence. The executant is therefore known to one attesting witness only, the notary cannot satisfy himself of the matters rule (9) requires as to both witnesses, and both witnesses cannot honestly sign the declaration at the foot of the deed. The identity-document route was not open in October 2019 and in any event no such document was produced. The brother relationship is a separate point and does not add a bar: rule (13) prevents the notary attesting only where he is a party, and he is not a party to or interested in this deed, while nothing in section 31 excludes a relative of the notary from signing as an attesting witness. The weight later given to his evidence is another matter, but the rules do not disqualify him.

Conclusion: The notary could not properly attest this deed as the facts stand, and the reason is the identification requirement rather than the family connection. The executant was neither known to the notary nor known to at least two of the attesting witnesses. The brother is not disqualified by being the notary's brother, so the defect is cured if a second witness who is of good repute and well acquainted with the executant, and able to state his proper name, occupation and residence, is brought in and both witnesses sign the required declaration at the foot of the deed; the notary may then attest.
