### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Romani, the owner of a Battaramulla land who is leaving for New Zealand, may present a caveat for registration in respect of that land, and who is entitled to file a caveat under the Registration of Documents Ordinance as amended by Act No. 32 of 2022.

Rule: Section 32 of the Registration of Documents Ordinance (Chapter 117) provides for caveats. Its subsection (1) was repealed and substituted by section 2(1) of the Registration of Documents (Amendment) Act No. 32 of 2022. Before that amendment section 32(1) allowed any person to present a caveat for registration. Under the substituted section 32(1)(a) a person who has, or who claims to have, a right, title or interest to a land, an undivided land, a land parcel or a condominium parcel may present for registration a caveat substantially in Form VIII of the Second Schedule, requiring to be served with notice of the presentation for registration of any instrument affecting that land. Section 32(1)(c)(i) extends the term caveator to a body of persons, a beneficiary under any trust affecting a land, the lawful guardian or next friend of a minor or of a person of unsound mind or mentally deficient person, an attorney of a person, a judgement creditor, an executor or administrator of the estate of a deceased, a legal heir, and an intended purchaser who has entered into an agreement with the owner or a developer of a land. Section 32(1)(c)(iii) extends the term interest to interests arising from an unregistered deed incapable of immediate registration, a trust, testate or intestate inheritance, instruments relating to a mortgage or an agreement to sell, co-ownership, condominium ownership and the estate of a minor or of a person of unsound mind. Land itself is defined in section 3 of the Ordinance to include things attached to the earth and any estate or interest in land. Standing is not sufficient on its own: section 32(1)(b)(i) requires the caveat to be in duplicate with an affidavit of the caveator and a certificate of an Attorney-at-law substantiating the right, title or interest; section 32(1)(b)(iii) requires a certified copy of a duly registered power of attorney where the caveator is an attorney; and section 32(1)(b)(iv) requires the caveat to contain the caveator's name, signature, identity card, passport or driving licence number, an address of the caveator in Sri Lanka for service, the right, title or interest claimed, the grounds of the claim and the description of the land. Section 32(2) obliges the Registrar to register a caveat received and to retain it, and section 32(3) as substituted by section 2(3) of the 2022 Act limits a caveat to a maximum of two years. Section 2(5) of the 2022 Act substituted sixty days for thirty days in section 32(5), so a caveator served with notice of a competing instrument has sixty days from posting of the notice to sue. A power of attorney relied on must be registered with the Registrar-General under section 3 of the Powers of Attorney Ordinance, and by section 26(1)(d) of the Ordinance a notary may present an instrument for registration on behalf of an entitled person. Section 33(1)(a), inserted by section 3(2) of the 2022 Act, allows the caveator or an attorney to withdraw the caveat in writing.

Application: Romani is the sole owner of the Battaramulla allotment under registered Deed of Transfer No. 4821, so she has a right and title to a land within the meaning of the substituted section 32(1)(a), and the allotment with the house on it is land under section 3. She therefore does not need to rely on the extended meaning of interest in section 32(1)(c)(iii) or on any of the derivative classes of caveator in section 32(1)(c)(i). She can satisfy section 32(1)(b)(i), since she is ready to lodge in duplicate with her affidavit and an attorney-at-law's certificate substantiating her ownership. Her impending departure engages section 32(1)(b)(iv): the caveat must state an address in Sri Lanka at which notices are to be served, and her sister's Rajagiriya address answers that requirement, whereas a New Zealand address alone would not. Because she is the owner, the Form X notices to other owners under section 32(1)(b)(ii) do not arise. As the notary consulted, you may present the caveat on her behalf under section 26(1)(d). Two matters should be pointed out to her. First, under section 32(3) the caveat lasts a maximum of two years, which is shorter than her intended three-year absence, so it will have to be renewed. Second, if an instrument affecting the land is presented while the caveat is in force, notice will go to her Sri Lanka address and she must commence action in a competent court within sixty days under section 32(5) as amended; the registered power of attorney in favour of her brother would let him receive the notice and act, and under section 32(1)(b)(iii) a certified copy of that registered power of attorney must accompany any caveat he lodges as attorney, the registration being required by section 3 of the Powers of Attorney Ordinance.

Conclusion: Yes. Romani may file a caveat. As owner holding title under a registered deed she has a right, title and interest to the land and so falls squarely within section 32(1)(a) of the Registration of Documents Ordinance as substituted by section 2(1) of Act No. 32 of 2022, provided she lodges it in Form VIII in duplicate with her affidavit and an attorney-at-law's certificate under section 32(1)(b)(i) and gives an address in Sri Lanka for service under section 32(1)(b)(iv). More generally, a caveat may now be filed only by a person who has or claims to have a right, title or interest in the land, and not by any person at large as the section read before the amendment; by section 32(1)(c)(i) that includes a body of persons, a beneficiary under a trust affecting the land, the guardian or next friend of a minor or of a person of unsound mind or mentally deficient person, an attorney, a judgement creditor, an executor, administrator or legal heir of a deceased, and an intended purchaser under an agreement with the owner or developer. She should be advised that the caveat lasts a maximum of two years under section 32(3) and will need renewal during a three-year absence.
