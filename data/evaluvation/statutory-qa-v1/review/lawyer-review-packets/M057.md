## Original background

Kamal Rathnayaka is a Notary practicing in Galle. Sudath Gunawardana is a neighbour of Kamal Ratnayake and the owner of a commercial building in Colombo. He is an illiterate individual. Sudath wishes to lease out his commercial building for 4 years at a monthly rental of Rs. 60,000/- and Kapila Warnakulasooriya is ready to take on lease in accordance with the said terms.

After the execution of the Lease Agreement, the Notary Kamal Ratnayake remembered that he could not obtain the Yearly Certificate from the Registrar of High Court for the current year.

## M057-Q01 (keep, hop count 2)

### Question

What are the liabilities of Notary Kamal Ratnayake?

### Legal issues

- ISS-M057-Q01-01: Whether a notary who attests a deed during a year for which he has not obtained the certificate issued yearly by the Registrar of the High Court commits an offence, and what penalty attaches.
- ISS-M057-Q01-02: Whether the penalty runs per deed attested while uncertificated, and whether the offence can be compounded instead of prosecuted.
- ISS-M057-Q01-03: Whether any relief remains open to the notary for the missed certificate, and what further professional consequence the lapse may carry.

### Draft answer

Issue: What liability does Kamal Ratnayake incur for having attested the four-year lease of Sudath Gunawardana's commercial building at a time when he had not obtained, for the current year, the certificate issued to notaries by the Registrar of the High Court?

Rule: Notaries Ordinance s.27 requires every notary to apply for and obtain from the Registrar of the High Court of his zone a certificate that he is a notary duly authorised to practise there. Such certificates must be applied for and granted on or before the annual date fixed by s.27(2) and are in force for one year and no longer, so a fresh certificate is needed each year. Where the application was not made within the time limited, s.27(2) allows the High Court Judge, if satisfied that the delay was due to accident, misfortune or other unavoidable cause, to direct the Registrar to issue the certificate notwithstanding the delay. Section 30 provides that any person who acts as a notary without having obtained such certificate is guilty of an offence in respect of every deed executed or acknowledged before him as such notary while he was without the certificate, and is liable to a fine of not less than Rs. 5,000 and not exceeding Rs. 25,000. Under s.35(1) the offence under s.30 is compoundable: the Registrar-General may, instead of instituting criminal proceedings, accept such sum as he considers proper in composition, after which criminal proceedings shall not be taken or continued. Separately, where the High Court Judge reports after inquiry under s.21 that a notary has been guilty of an offence rendering him unfit, or of gross misconduct in the discharge of his duties, s.21(2) empowers the Minister to cancel his warrant or suspend him from office. Section 13, the offence of practising without a warrant, declaration, bond or filed copy of the warrant, is a different matter and is not engaged where the notary holds a warrant.

Application: The lease is of a commercial building for four years, well outside the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2 exception for a lease at will or for a period not exceeding one month, so it had to be executed before and attested by a notary; Kamal Ratnayake was accordingly acting in his capacity as a notary when he attested it. On his own recollection he had not obtained the yearly certificate under s.27 for the current year, and a certificate for an earlier year would in any event have expired, being in force for one year and no longer. He therefore acted as a notary without having obtained such certificate, and the lease is a deed executed before him as such notary while he was uncertificated, which is precisely what s.30 penalises. The penalty attaches in respect of every such deed, so the lease attracts one offence and any other deed he attested during the uncertificated period attracts its own. Whether the Registrar-General prosecutes or instead accepts a sum in composition under s.35(1) is a matter for the Registrar-General's discretion. If Kamal Ratnayake can show that his failure to apply in time was due to accident, misfortune or other unavoidable cause he may still ask the High Court Judge under the proviso to s.27(2) to direct the Registrar to issue the certificate, but that relief goes to obtaining the certificate late and does not undo an offence already committed by attesting while uncertificated. On the facts given, mere forgetfulness is not stated to be accident, misfortune or unavoidable cause, so the availability of that relief is open.

Conclusion: Kamal Ratnayake is guilty of an offence under s.30 of the Notaries Ordinance in respect of the lease he attested while without the yearly certificate required by s.27, and is liable to a fine of not less than Rs. 5,000 and not exceeding Rs. 25,000 for that deed, with a further such liability for each other deed attested during the uncertificated period. The Registrar-General may compound the offence under s.35(1) instead of prosecuting. He may apply under the proviso to s.27(2) for the certificate to be issued despite his delay if the delay was due to accident, misfortune or other unavoidable cause. He also stands exposed, on a s.21 inquiry and report, to cancellation of his warrant or suspension from office by the Minister. He is not liable under s.13, which addresses practising without a warrant.
