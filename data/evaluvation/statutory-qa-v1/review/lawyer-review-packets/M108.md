### Question

### Enriched background

Rehan de Silva is the owner of 2 Condominium properties. His brother Gihan de Silva intends to prepare a Deed of Declaration claiming 1/2 of the property, where the Condominium is situated and register the Deed under the Registration of Documents Ordinance.

The property Gihan has in mind is Nelum Residencies, a completed four-storey building at No. 118, Sri Sumangala Mawatha, Nugegoda, standing on a single land parcel of 32.5 perches. Condominium Plan No. 4127 for that land and building was registered on 5 May 2011 and has never been amended. Rehan de Silva is named in that registered plan as the owner of the whole land parcel and of the two condominium parcels he holds, Units 2B and 4A, and no share of the land parcel has at any time been transferred to Gihan. A management corporation for the building was constituted when the plan was registered, and it has passed no resolution about transferring any part of the land or of the shared areas to anyone.

Gihan de Silva owns no condominium parcel in the building and holds no deed, decree or other instrument of title to any part of the land parcel. The only document he proposes to rely on is a Deed of Declaration he intends to have attested by a notary in February 2018 and lodged at the Delkanda-Nugegoda Land Registry.

### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Gihan de Silva, who owns no condominium parcel in the building, can execute a Deed of Declaration claiming an undivided half share of the land on which Rehan's registered condominium stands, and register it under the Registration of Documents Ordinance, and what such a deed and its registration would achieve.

Rule: Once a Condominium Plan is registered, so much of the land parcel as is not comprised in any condominium parcel is common elements (Apartment Ownership Law s.26), and the common elements are held by the owners of all the condominium parcels as tenants in common in proportion to their share parcels (s.9(1)); each parcel with its appurtenant common elements is deemed absolutely owned by the person described in the registered plan as owner (s.9(5)). Section 11 forbids any share in the common elements being disposed of or otherwise dealt with except as appurtenant to a condominium parcel of that property, and s.16 gives the only route by which part of the common elements may pass, namely a special resolution of the owners of all the parcels directing the management corporation to transfer it. The Partition Law does not apply to land comprised in a registered Condominium Plan (s.23). On the registration side, a deed purporting to declare an interest in land is an instrument affecting land (Registration of Documents Ordinance s.8(b)) which its executant may present for registration (s.26(1)), and the Registrar's grounds of refusal (s.36) go to authority to present, form of execution, stamp duty and fees rather than to the truth of the title asserted; but s.7(4) provides that registration cures no defect and confers no effect or validity the instrument would not otherwise have, beyond priority. Where the two enactments conflict, the Apartment Ownership Law prevails (s.25).

Application: Condominium Plan No. 4127 for Nelum Residencies was registered in 2011, so the 32.5 perch land parcel under the building, apart from the condominium parcels themselves, is common elements held by the owners of the parcels shown in that plan, and Rehan, named in the plan as owner, is deemed to own his parcels with their appurtenant common elements absolutely. Gihan owns no condominium parcel and holds no instrument of title to any part of the land, so there is no parcel in his hands to which a share in the common elements could be appurtenant; a deed claiming a half share of that land is exactly the dealing s.11 forbids. The owners have passed no special resolution and the management corporation has executed no transfer, so the s.16 route has not been used. As a matter of form the deed can be drawn and attested, and because the Registrar is not required to adjudicate title before accepting an instrument affecting land, it may well be accepted and entered in the register; but by s.7(4) that entry adds nothing to a deed that conveys nothing, and to the extent the Ordinance is invoked to defeat the Apartment Ownership Law, s.25 makes that Law prevail. Nor could Gihan follow the declaration with a partition action, since s.23 excludes the Partition Law. Whether the Registrar would in fact accept the deed is for the Registrar in the individual case; the point is that acceptance would not assist Gihan.

Conclusion: Gihan may have such a Deed of Declaration prepared and attested, and it may physically be lodged and even accepted for registration under the Registration of Documents Ordinance, but he cannot lawfully acquire what he claims. A half share of the land on which the registered condominium stands is a share in the common elements, and s.11 of the Apartment Ownership Law bars any dealing with it except as appurtenant to a condominium parcel, which Gihan does not own. Registration would give him nothing, because under s.7(4) of the Ordinance it cures no defect and confers no validity, and under s.25 the Apartment Ownership Law prevails over the Ordinance. The deed would be of no legal effect against Rehan, and its registration would be open to challenge by an interested party in the District Court.
