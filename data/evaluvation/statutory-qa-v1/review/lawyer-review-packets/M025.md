Rule: Inheritance ab intestato to immovable property in Sri Lanka is governed by Part III of the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance (s.21(1)), which applies to the estate of any person dying after the proclamation of the Ordinance (s.20) and does not apply to Kandyans, Muslims or Tamils of the Northern Province subject to the Thesawalamai (s.2). A surviving spouse inherits one-half (s.22); subject to that, inheritance passes in the order descendants, ascendants, collaterals (s.23). Children and remoter descendants are preferent to all others and take equally per capita, the issue of a deceased child taking by representation (s.24). Where descendants fail, the estate goes to both parents if both are alive; if only one parent is alive, that parent takes half and the other half goes to the brothers and sisters of the full blood, the issue of deceased full-blood siblings by representation, and the brothers and sisters of the half-blood who are related to the intestate by the side of the deceased parent (s.25). A person is "unmarried" for the purposes of the Ordinance when he has no husband or wife living (s.3). Where those who succeed are equally near in degree they take per capita (s.30). Dispositions of immovable property must be by notarially attested writing (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2), and on an intestacy the District Court identifies the heirs and their shares in a testamentary proceeding (Civil Procedure Code s.531) and discharges the administrator once the estate has been duly administered and distributed (Civil Procedure Code s.724B).

Application: Gamini Jayasinghe held the entirety under the final decree of 25 October 2010 in Partition Case No. 582/P. Kasun died on 3 May 2013 intestate and unmarried, that is without a spouse living within the meaning of s.3, and without issue, so no half share arises under s.22 and the descendant class in s.23 is empty. That devolution was carried into effect in Testamentary Proceeding No.1589/2013/T, in which the heirs and their shares were determined (Civil Procedure Code s.531), and by the notarially attested Administrator's Conveyance No.6231 of 21 March 2014 executed by Thilak as administrator on the termination of the proceeding (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2; Civil Procedure Code s.724B). On Thilak's intestate death on 3 March 2017 his one-half devolved on his surviving children Sunil and Lalith in equal shares, one-quarter of the land each. On Sarathi's intestate death on 8 August 2018 her one-quarter devolved as to one-half on her husband Sena and, on the footing stated above, as to one-quarter on her surviving father Namal Silva and as to one-quarter on her brother Amal. The present owners are therefore Amal, Sunil, Lalith, Sena and Namal Silva. Two facts are open on the background as given: it does not name the grantees in Administrator's Conveyance No.6231, and it does not state in terms that Namal Silva is the father of Sarathi and Amal or that he is still living. The pedigree above reads the conveyance as a distribution to Kasun's heirs and treats Namal Silva as the surviving father; if instead both of Sarathi's parents were dead at her death, her non-spousal half would pass wholly to Amal under s.26 of the Ordinance.

### Draft answer

Issue: The question asks what undivided share of the land and premises bearing Assessment No.45, Colombo Road, Pugoda each heir now holds. This turns on quantifying the three successive intestate devolutions: Kasun's estate in 2013, Thilak's share on his death in 2017, and Sarathi's share on her death in 2018.

Rule: A surviving spouse inherits one-half of the intestate's property (Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance s.22), and the remainder passes in the order descendants, ascendants, collaterals (s.23). Children take equally per capita and are preferent to all others (s.24). Where descendants fail and only one parent survives, the surviving parent takes one-half and the other half goes to full-blood brothers and sisters and to half-blood brothers and sisters related to the intestate by the side of the deceased parent (s.25). Heirs equally near in degree take per capita (s.30). An intestate who dies unmarried, that is with no husband or wife living (s.3), leaves no spouse to take under s.22. The Ordinance governs inheritance to immovable property in Sri Lanka (s.21(1)).

Application: Kasun held the entirety when he died intestate, unmarried and issueless on 3 May 2013. No share passed under s.22. Under s.25 his surviving father Thilak took one-half, and Sarathi and Amal, the half-blood sister and brother related to him through his deceased mother Kamala, took the other half per capita under s.30, that is one-quarter of the land each. Thilak died intestate on 3 March 2017 with no surviving spouse disclosed; under s.24 his one-half passed equally to his surviving children Sunil and Lalith, giving each one-quarter of the land. Sarathi died intestate and issueless on 8 August 2018 holding one-quarter. Under s.22 her husband Sena took one-half of that quarter, namely one-eighth of the land. The remaining one-eighth fell under s.25: her descendants failed, her mother Kamala had predeceased her in 2011, and on the footing that her father is Namal Silva and that he survives her, he takes one-half of that eighth, namely one-sixteenth, while the other one-sixteenth goes to her full-blood brother Amal, her half-brother Kasun having predeceased her without issue. Amal therefore adds one-sixteenth to the one-quarter he took from Kasun's estate, giving him five-sixteenths.

Conclusion: On the footing set out above the present shares in the land and premises bearing Assessment No.45, Colombo Road, Pugoda are: Amal five-sixteenths; Sunil one-quarter (four-sixteenths); Lalith one-quarter (four-sixteenths); Sena one-eighth (two-sixteenths); and Namal Silva one-sixteenth. These add up to the entirety. The one-sixteenth attributed to Namal Silva depends on the reading that he is the father of Sarathi and Amal and was living at Sarathi's death, neither of which the background states in terms; if both of Sarathi's parents were dead when she died, her non-spousal one-eighth would pass wholly to Amal under s.26 of the Ordinance, giving Amal three-eighths and leaving Namal Silva with nothing.
