### Draft answer

Issue: The company has retained you on a purchase of a 41-acre rubber-planted estate in Galle. The question is what steps you must take, before the deed is drawn, at execution and after execution, so that the company acquires a title that is valid, unencumbered and registrable, and so that no statutory prohibition or consent requirement is overlooked.

Rule: Several statutes bear on a purchase of this kind. First, the transfer of ownership of a rubber estate is null and void unless a certificate of consent of the Board has previously been obtained by the transferor and the transfer complies with any conditions of that consent (Tea and Rubber Estates (Control of Fragmentation) Act s.3(1)), but the Act bites only where the land answers the definition of 'rubber estate', which requires both a minimum extent and registration as an estate under the rubber control legislation (s.25). Secondly, rubber land is agricultural land (Land Reform Law s.66); the ceiling on non-paddy agricultural land is fifty acres per person (s.3(1)(b)); where agricultural land is owned by a private company its shareholders are deemed to own it for the purpose of the ceiling in proportion to their shareholdings (s.8); and where a person becomes the owner of agricultural land in excess of the ceiling after the commencement of the Law, the excess vests in the Commission and is held on a statutory lease (s.5(1)). Thirdly, a transfer of title to a company incorporated in Sri Lanka is prohibited where foreign shareholding in it, direct or indirect, is fifty per cent or above (Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act s.2(1)(b)); an alienation in contravention of the Act is void (s.18); and the Registrar of Lands must satisfy himself on this point by requiring documentary proof from the company secretary (s.4(3)). Fourthly, the company cannot enter into a major transaction, which includes an acquisition of assets worth more than half the value of its assets before the acquisition, unless it is approved by special resolution, made contingent on one, consented to in writing by all shareholders, or expressly authorised by the original articles (Companies Act s.185(1) and (2)); and an obligation which a natural person would have to execute notarially is entered into by the company in writing signed under its name by two directors (s.19(1)(a)). Fifthly, no sale, purchase or transfer of immovable property avails in law unless it is in writing, signed by every executant and attested by a notary before two witnesses present at the same time, with the prescribed thumb impressions (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2, in the form substituted by the Prevention of Frauds (Amendment) Act No. 30 of 2022 s.2). Sixthly, before drawing the deed the notary must search the land registry to ascertain the state of the title and whether any prior deed has been registered (Notaries Ordinance s.31(17)(a)) and must obtain the certified extract of the folio showing ownership and registered encumbrances (s.31(17)(b)(i)); where a company is a party he must retain a certified copy of the certificate of incorporation and the details of the board (s.31(17)(d)); where a corporate body executes he must obtain the seal and the signatures of the board or of a person authorised by board resolution (s.31(14)); the deed must contain an accurate description showing boundaries, extent and situation (s.31(16)(a)); attestation requires an assurance that stamp duty is provided (s.31(6)); and the deed must be submitted for registration within thirty days of attestation (s.31(30A)). Finally, an unregistered instrument is void against a party claiming an adverse interest for valuable consideration under a subsequent duly registered instrument (Registration of Documents Ordinance s.7(1)), and the registry books may be searched and certified extracts obtained by an intending purchaser's attorney-at-law (s.42).

Application: Take the statutory checks in order. The estate is 16.6 hectares and has never been registered as an estate in the statutory register of rubber estates, so it does not answer the definition in s.25 and the prohibition in s.3(1) is not engaged; you should nonetheless obtain written confirmation of the registration position, because if the land were a rubber estate as defined the vendor would first have to obtain the Board's certificate of consent and a transfer without it would be null and void. The estate is planted with rubber and is therefore agricultural land within s.66. Because the purchaser is a private company, s.8 attributes the estate to the five shareholders in proportion to their shares, so each is deemed to acquire one fifth of 41 acres, that is 8.2 acres; since none of them owns any other agricultural land, no shareholder comes near the fifty-acre ceiling in s.3(1)(b) and the vesting sanction in s.5(1) will not operate. Had any shareholder been near the ceiling, the excess created by the purchase would have vested in the Commission, so the shareholders' existing agricultural holdings must be verified in writing before you advise the company to commit. All five shareholders are citizens of Sri Lanka and no share is held directly or indirectly by a foreigner, so the transfer is not prohibited by s.2(1)(b) and s.18 does not avoid it; but you should have the company secretary prepare the documentary proof of the shareholding that the Registrar of Lands will require under s.4(3), and advise the company of the continuing obligation to report on its foreign shareholding after registration. On the company's authority, the assets to be acquired are worth Rs. 41,000,000 against an asset base of Rs. 60,000,000, so the acquisition is greater than half the value of the company's assets before the acquisition and is a major transaction within s.185(2)(a); as the original articles contain no express authorisation, a special resolution under s.185(1), or the written consent of all five shareholders, must be obtained before the company binds itself, and any agreement to sell must be signed under the company's name by two directors and notarially executed as required by s.19(1)(a) read with s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance. On title, exercise the right of search under s.42, examine the Galle Land Registry folio E 214/86 as required by s.31(17)(a) and obtain the certified folio extract under s.31(17)(b)(i); call for the original of Deed No. 1420 of 1998 and the earlier deeds in the chain, since a photocopy proves nothing and only notarially executed instruments pass title under s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance. The 2019 mortgage bond is registered in the folio, so by force of the priority rule in s.7(1) the company's later deed would take subject to it; you must therefore arrange for the bank's discharge to be executed and registered, or for the redemption amount to be paid out of the purchase money at completion against delivery of the discharge. Furnish the notary with a certified copy of the certificate of incorporation and the details of the directors under s.31(17)(d), and with the board resolution and seal if the company is to execute anything under s.31(14); settle a schedule with an accurate description of boundaries, extent and situation as s.31(16)(a) requires, if necessary on a fresh survey; provide funds for stamp duty so that the notary has the assurance s.31(6) requires; and see that the executed transfer is submitted for registration within thirty days of attestation under s.31(30A), which is what secures the company's priority under s.7(1).

Conclusion: You should advise the company that the purchase can proceed, but only after the following steps. Verify in writing that the estate is not registered as an estate for the purposes of the fragmentation legislation, and if it is, require the vendor to obtain the Board's certificate of consent first. Verify the existing agricultural holdings of all five shareholders so that no one is taken over the fifty-acre ceiling by the deemed proportionate ownership. Confirm and document that there is no direct or indirect foreign shareholding, and have the secretary ready to prove it to the Registrar of Lands. Obtain a special resolution, or the written consent of all shareholders, before the company commits itself, since the acquisition is a major transaction. Search the Galle Land Registry, take the certified folio extract, call for the originals of the chain of notarially executed deeds and satisfy yourself on the vendor's title and possession. Have the registered mortgage discharged, or redeemed out of the purchase money at completion. Furnish the notary with the certificate of incorporation, the details of the board and any board resolution, settle an accurate schedule of the land, provide for stamp duty, have the deed executed by the vendor before a notary and two witnesses in the form then required, and see that it is lodged for registration within thirty days of attestation.

### Draft answer

Issue: The company has instructed you to examine the title to a forty-perch residential premises adjoining the rubber estate. The question is which documents you must call for in order to carry out that examination, and what makes each of them necessary.

Rule: The starting point is that only a notarially executed instrument passes title to immovable property: no sale, purchase or transfer of land avails in law unless it is in writing, signed by every executant and attested by a notary before two witnesses present at the same time (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2, in the form substituted by the Prevention of Frauds (Amendment) Act No. 30 of 2022 s.2). Next, before any deed affecting an interest in land is drawn, the notary must search or cause to be searched the registers in the land registry to ascertain the state of the title and whether any prior deed affecting the land has been registered (Notaries Ordinance s.31(17)(a)), and he must obtain from the Registrar of the relevant Land Registry the certified extract of the folio containing the last entry, showing the ownership and the registered encumbrances (s.31(17)(b)(i)). An intending purchaser's attorney-at-law may himself search the registry books and indexes and take certified copies and extracts (Registration of Documents Ordinance s.42). What the folio must be read for is fixed by the priority rule: an unregistered instrument is void against a party claiming an adverse interest for valuable consideration under a subsequent duly registered instrument (s.7(1)); a lis pendens does not bind a purchaser unless registered (s.11(1)); and a caveat registered by a third party requires notice to be given to him of any instrument presented for registration (s.32(1)). A deed cannot be drawn unless it embodies an accurate and clear description of the land showing boundaries, extent and situation (Notaries Ordinance s.31(16)(a)), which is why a plan is needed. Where a party to the deed is a corporate body, the notary must retain a certified copy of the certificate of incorporation or other instrument establishing it, and the details of the board (s.31(17)(d)). Documentary title is not conclusive: undisturbed and uninterrupted possession by a title adverse to or independent of the owner's, unaccompanied by rent or service, for ten years founds a prescriptive claim (Prescription Ordinance s.3). Finally, where a parcel has been registered under the title registration scheme the owner may obtain a Certificate of Title which is conclusive evidence of that interest (Registration of Title Act s.37), so the first question is which system governs the parcel.

Application: Since the parcel is not within any declared title registration area, the Certificate of Title route under s.37 is unavailable and the title has to be proved by the deeds and the deeds register, which fixes the documents you must call for. First, call for the original of Deed of Transfer No. 3120 of 8 July 2004 and the originals, or certified copies from the notarial protocols or the Registrar's duplicates, of the 1985 and 1972 deeds and of every other instrument in the chain, together with any instrument by which title devolved otherwise than by deed. The photocopy the vendor has produced proves nothing, and by force of s.2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance an instrument that was not notarially executed in the required form did not pass title at all, so each link has to be inspected and its attestation checked. Trace the chain back far enough to be safe against the ten-year prescriptive period in s.3 of the Prescription Ordinance and to see that no link is missing. Secondly, call for the certified extract of the relevant folio of the Galle Land Registry, which s.31(17)(b)(i) requires the notary to obtain and which s.42 entitles you to take: it will show the registered ownership and the registered encumbrances, and it is the only way to find out whether some instrument registered earlier would, under s.7(1), prevail over the company's deed. Thirdly, read the folio for the two matters the vendor has already hinted at: the security given for the 2016 loan, for which you must call for the instrument creating it and, before completion, an executed and registrable discharge; and the document lodged by the relative in 2022, which if it is a caveat under s.32(1) means that notice of any instrument presented for registration will go to the caveator, and if it is a registered lis pendens under s.11(1) means the company would take subject to the pending action, so the pleadings and the state of that action must be called for. Fourthly, call for Plan No. 2456 of 3 March 1996 and, since the parcel is to be conveyed to a company that has never seen it, a current figure of survey superimposed on the earlier plan, because s.31(16)(a) requires the deed to carry an accurate description showing boundaries, extent and situation, and because the occupied strip must be located on the ground. Fifthly, because a neighbour has occupied the shed and well since about 2010 without paying rent or rendering service, call for whatever documents the vendor has to rebut a prescriptive claim to that strip, such as a written permission, a tenancy or licence agreement, receipts for rent or acknowledgment of the vendor's title, and evidence of the vendor's own possession such as assessment and rates receipts. Sixthly, since the purchaser is a corporate body, the company's certified certificate of incorporation and the details of its board must be produced for the notary under s.31(17)(d), together with the board resolution authorising the purchase. You should also call for the local authority certificates and receipts that a purchaser of built-up residential property would ordinarily require, although these arise under legislation outside the statutes considered here.

Conclusion: Call for the originals or certified copies of the whole chain of notarially executed deeds beginning with Deed No. 3120 of 2004 and running back through the 1985 and 1972 deeds; the certified extract of the relevant Galle Land Registry folio showing ownership and registered encumbrances, together with a search of the registers for prior instruments, pending actions and caveats; the instrument securing the 2016 loan and a registrable discharge of it; the document lodged in 2022 and, if it evidences a pending action, the record of that action; Plan No. 2456 of 1996 with a current figure of survey; documents evidencing the vendor's possession and any permission given to the occupier of the shed and well; and the company's certificate of incorporation, details of its board and the resolution authorising the purchase. Each of these is called for because title passes only by notarially executed deeds, because priority depends on registration, because a registered pending action or caveat binds or warns a purchaser, because the deed must describe the land accurately, because ten years of adverse possession can defeat the paper title, and because the notary must have the corporate documents of a company that is a party.
