## M049-Q01 (enrich, hop count 3)

### Question

Can he claim prescriptive title?

### Enriched background

Nimal, a retired schoolteacher of Pelawatta, has been in possession of a land registered under Registration of Title Act for more than 10 years. The land is Land Parcel No. 214 delineated on Cadastral Map No. 520150 for the village of Pelawatta in the Homagama Divisional Secretary's Division, an area the Minister declared by Order published in the Gazette to be an area to which the Registration of Title Act applies. The Title Register for that parcel records Somapala as the registered owner, registered with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership on 3 March 2009. Nimal's name appears nowhere in the Title Register, either as owner or under any registered interest, and he holds no deed, lease or grant in respect of the parcel. He entered into occupation of the parcel in January 2011 and has occupied it continuously and without interruption since then, paying no rent or produce to Somapala and doing nothing from which an acknowledgment of a right in Somapala could fairly be inferred. In October 2021 he wishes to assert ownership of the parcel against Somapala.

### Legal issues

- ISS-M049-Q01-01: Whether ten years' undisturbed and uninterrupted adverse possession would satisfy the statutory conditions for prescriptive title to land.
- ISS-M049-Q01-02: Whether the Prescription Ordinance applies at all to a land parcel registered under the Registration of Title Act with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership.
- ISS-M049-Q01-03: Whether a person not named in the Title Register can assert an unregistered title against the registered owner, and whether the ten-year conversion route under the Registration of Title Act is open to such a person.

### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Nimal, a stranger in undisturbed and uninterrupted possession for more than ten years of a land parcel registered under the Registration of Title Act No. 21 of 1998 in another person's name with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership, can claim prescriptive title to that parcel.

Rule: Prescriptive title to land rests on Prescription Ordinance s.3, which entitles a person to a decree on proof of ten years' undisturbed and uninterrupted possession by a title adverse to or independent of the owner's, unaccompanied by payment of rent or produce or by any act from which an acknowledgment of another's right would fairly be inferred. That Ordinance is withdrawn from land brought on to the Title Register: Registration of Title Act s.57 provides that the Prescription Ordinance shall not apply to lands or interests in land registered under that Act with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership or a Second Class Title of Ownership, and s.14 shows that these are the classes with which a claimant is declared eligible to be registered on initial compilation. Registration of Title Act s.73 gives that Act effect notwithstanding anything to the contrary in any other written law, so the Prescription Ordinance cannot survive s.57 in this field. Alongside the exclusion, Registration of Title Act s.33(1) makes entries in the Title Register conclusive evidence of the ownership recorded and provides that they shall not be questioned in a court of law except as the Act provides, and s.38 denies entitlement to any title or interest in a registered land parcel unless that title or interest is itself registered. The only route by which ten years' possession improves a position under the Act is s.31(1), and it belongs to a person already registered with a Second Class Title of Ownership, who becomes eligible at the end of ten years from registration, absent an action under s.29, for conversion to a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership.

Application: The parcel Nimal occupies lies in a declared area and is registered under the Registration of Title Act, with Somapala entered in the Title Register as owner with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership since March 2009. Nimal's occupation from January 2011 to October 2021 is continuous, unaccompanied by rent or produce and free of any act of acknowledgment, so on its own terms it would answer the description in Prescription Ordinance s.3 and exceed the ten-year term. That does not assist him, because the parcel is land registered with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership and Registration of Title Act s.57 makes the Prescription Ordinance inapplicable to it; the rule he would invoke is simply not available for this land. Nor can he reach the same result by another statutory route. He is not named in the Title Register in any capacity, so under s.38 he is not entitled to any title or interest in the parcel, and under s.33(1) Somapala's registered ownership is conclusive and cannot be questioned in court except as the Act allows, which does not extend to a claim founded on adverse possession. The conversion right in s.31(1) is closed to him as well, since it presupposes registration with a Second Class Title of Ownership and Nimal has no registration at all.

Conclusion: No. Nimal cannot claim prescriptive title. Because the parcel is registered under the Registration of Title Act with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership, s.57 puts the Prescription Ordinance out of play, so no period of adverse possession, however long or however undisturbed, can ripen into title to it, and s.38 leaves him without entitlement while he remains off the register. This assumes the parcel was on the Title Register throughout the period relied on; if part of that possession ran before the land was brought under the Act, the position for that earlier period would need separate consideration.
