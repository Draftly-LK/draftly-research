## Original background

Kamal Rathnayaka is a Notary practicing in Galle. Sudath Gunawardana is a neighbour of Kamal Ratnayake and the owner of a commercial building in Colombo. He is an illiterate individual. Sudath wishes to lease out his commercial building for 4 years at a monthly rental of Rs. 60,000/- and Kapila Warnakulasooriya is ready to take on lease in accordance with the said terms.

## M056-Q01 (keep, hop count 3)

### Question

Can Kamal Rathnayaka attest this Lease Agreement? Discuss.

### Legal issues

- ISS-M056-Q01-01: Whether a lease of a commercial building for four years must be executed before and attested by a notary at all.
- ISS-M056-Q01-02: Whether a notary whose warrant authorises him to practise in the Galle area is competent to attest a lease of a building situated in Colombo, that is, whether the statutory limit attaches to the place of attestation or to the situation of the land.
- ISS-M056-Q01-03: Whether the notary is disqualified because the lessor is his neighbour, and whether the lessor's illiteracy prevents him from attesting.

### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Kamal Rathnayaka, a notary practising in Galle, may lawfully attest a four-year lease of a commercial building situated in Colombo granted by his neighbour Sudath Gunawardana, who is illiterate, to Kapila Warnakulasooriya.

Rule: Under Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2 no agreement affecting immovable property, other than a lease at will or for a period not exceeding one month, is of force in law unless it is in writing and attested by a notary. Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (22) provides that a notary shall not authenticate or attest any deed or instrument in any area other than that in which he is authorized to practice, nor in any language other than that in which he is authorized to practice. Notaries Ordinance s.4A deems a warrant issued for a judicial division to authorise practice in the judicial zone in which the notary resides, and s.9 binds him to have his office within the area specified in his warrant. The limit is therefore on where the notary acts, not on where the land lies: s.31 rule (29) expressly contemplates a deed or instrument affecting land situated in a district other than that in which the attesting notary resides, and requires him to deliver or transmit to the Registrar of Lands of the district in which the land is situated a certified copy, on or before the fifteenth day of the following month. As to personal capacity, s.31 rule (13) disqualifies a notary only from attesting a deed or instrument to which he is a party. Where an executant is or professes to be unable to read the instrument, s.31 rule (11) forbids attestation unless and until the notary has read over and explained it, or caused it to be explained, in the presence and hearing of that person and in the presence of the attesting witnesses.

Application: The proposed lease is for four years, far beyond the one month carved out of Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2, so it must be in writing and notarially attested to be of any force in law. Kamal Rathnayaka holds a notarial warrant and practises in Galle. Nothing in the Notaries Ordinance ties his competence to the district in which the leased property stands: s.31 rule (22) looks only to the area and the language in which he is authorized to practice, and s.4A extends that area to the judicial zone in which he resides. Provided the lease is drawn in a language in which he is authorized to practice and is executed and attested by him within that area, rule (22) is satisfied even though the building is in Colombo. That the land is in another district is dealt with expressly by rule (29), which assumes the attestation is good and adds a duty to send a certified copy to the Registrar of Lands of the Colombo district within the prescribed time. Kamal is Sudath Gunawardana's neighbour but he is not a party to the lease, which is between Sudath as lessor and Kapila Warnakulasooriya as lessee, so rule (13) does not touch him; his acquaintance with the lessor in fact assists identification. Sudath's illiteracy is likewise not a bar: it engages rule (11) and obliges Kamal to read over and explain the lease to him, in his presence and hearing and in the presence of the attesting witnesses, before attestation.

Conclusion: Yes. Kamal Rathnayaka can attest this lease agreement, and the lease in fact needs notarial attestation to be of force in law. His competence turns not on the Colombo situation of the building but on the execution and attestation taking place within the area, and the instrument being in a language, authorised by his warrant. The Colombo location affects only his later duty under s.31 rule (29) to transmit a certified copy to the Registrar of Lands of that district; his relationship as neighbour is no disqualification under rule (13); and the lessor's illiteracy obliges him to comply with rule (11) rather than to decline the work. Were the lease to be signed and attested outside the area of his warrant, or drawn in a language he is not authorised to practise in, the answer would be no.

## M056-Q04 (keep, hop count 3)

### Question

If the parties request the Notary to insert a lower amount on the Lease Agreement in order to save stamp duty, what are the duties and liabilities of the Notary in this situation?

### Legal issues

- ISS-M056-Q04-01: Whether the lease agreement is chargeable with stamp duty and on whom the duty falls, so that understating the rental would reduce the duty payable.
- ISS-M056-Q04-02: What duty the Notaries Ordinance imposes on the notary as to the consideration to be inserted in the instrument, and whether he may accede to the parties' request.
- ISS-M056-Q04-03: What criminal, civil and professional liabilities the notary incurs if he inserts the lower rental, and what he must do instead.

### Draft answer

Issue: What are the duties and the liabilities of Kamal Rathnayaka as a notary when the lessor and the lessee ask him to insert in the lease agreement a rental lower than the agreed Rs. 60,000 per month in order to reduce the stamp duty payable.

Rule: A lease or hire of any property is a specified instrument under Stamp Duty (Special Provisions) Act s.4, and by s.3 stamp duty is charged on every specified instrument executed, drawn or presented in Sri Lanka at the rate the Minister determines by Order published in the Gazette; by s.6 the duty on a lease is payable by the lessee unless there is agreement to the contrary. Notaries Ordinance s.38(1) makes it the duty of every notary to endeavour to ascertain the true and full consideration for the execution of any deed and to insert and set forth the same in the deed, and s.38(2) makes a notary who knowingly and wilfully inserts anything other than the full and true consideration, or abets the doing of it, guilty of an offence and liable to a fine in addition to any civil liability he may incur. Notaries Ordinance s.39(b) further provides that a notary who knowingly and wilfully, with intent to prejudice or defraud any person, inserts in any deed or instrument any figure or thing which ought not to have been inserted, or omits any which ought to have been inserted, is guilty of an offence carrying imprisonment. Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (20)(f) requires the attestation itself to state the number and value of the stamps affixed to the instrument and to its duplicate. Understating the consideration does not defeat the revenue in any event: under Stamp Duty (Special Provisions) Act s.10 an Assessor who is of opinion that an instrument liable to stamp duty has not been duly stamped shall assess the duty payable and require the person liable to pay it. Beyond the criminal law, Notaries Ordinance s.21(1) makes gross misconduct in the discharge of notarial duties, and repeated breaches of the rules under the Ordinance, grounds on which a report may be made to the Minister, who may suspend the notary or cancel his warrant.

Application: The agreed rental is Rs. 60,000 per month for four years, so that is the true and full consideration for the lease. The lease is a specified instrument chargeable with stamp duty at the rate in force, and the duty ordinarily falls on Kapila Warnakulasooriya as lessee. If Kamal Rathnayaka writes a lower rental into the lease at the parties' request, he inserts something other than the full and true consideration knowing it to be false, which is what s.38(2) forbids, and the same act would abet the parties' own attempt to underpay. Because the object stated by the parties is to save stamp duty, the false figure would be inserted with intent to prejudice the revenue, which brings the conduct within s.39(b) as well. He would also be certifying in his own attestation, under rule (20)(f), a stamp value corresponding to a figure he knows to be untrue. The parties gain nothing lawful by the exercise, since an Assessor may in any event assess the duty properly payable and require the lessee to pay it. Kamal Rathnayaka's duty is therefore to ascertain and insert the true rental of Rs. 60,000 per month, to explain to the parties that he cannot record any other figure, and to decline to execute the lease on the terms proposed if they persist.

Conclusion: Kamal Rathnayaka must refuse the request. His duty under Notaries Ordinance s.38(1) is to ascertain the true and full consideration and to set it forth in the instrument, which here means the agreed rental of Rs. 60,000 per month. If he accedes he commits an offence under s.38(2), punishable by a fine and leaving him civilly liable in addition, and, because the figure would be inserted with intent to defeat the stamp duty, a further and graver offence under s.39(b) punishable with imprisonment. He would also expose himself to inquiry for gross misconduct under s.21, on a report of which the Minister may suspend him or cancel his warrant. The proper course is to record the true rental, satisfy himself that the correct stamp duty is provided, state the stamp particulars accurately in his attestation, and refuse to attest the lease if the parties insist on the false figure.
