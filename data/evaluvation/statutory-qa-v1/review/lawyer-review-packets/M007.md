The second attesting witness, Ruwan Fernando, did not come to the office. On 13 January 2026 Saman took the deed to Ruwan Fernando's house and obtained his signature there, in the absence of Nimal Perera and of Kamala Silva, and then attested the deed as though the executant and both witnesses had signed in his presence and in the presence of one another. Saman also left the front page of the deed blank of his own name and initials, the number assigned to him as a notary, the address and telephone number of his office, and the registered address of the company employing him.

### Draft answer

Issue: Whether the Deed of Transfer attested by Saman can be invalidated because he failed to comply with rules set out in section 31 of the Notaries Ordinance, and whether the answer depends on which rule he broke.

Rule: Breach of a rule in section 31 does not of itself destroy the instrument. Notaries Ordinance s.33 provides that no instrument shall be deemed to be invalid by reason only of the failure of any notary to observe any provision of any rule set out in section 31 in respect of any matter of form. The protection is expressly limited by the proviso to s.33, which states that nothing in the section gives validity to any instrument that may be invalid by reason of non-compliance with the provisions of any other written law. Two of the rules in section 31 are engaged here. Rule (17)(c) requires the notary to write on the front page of the deed his name with initials, the number assigned to him, his office address and telephone number, and, if he is a legal officer working in an organisation, the registered address of that organisation; that requirement goes to how the face of the instrument is completed. Rule (12) forbids a notary to attest any deed unless the executant and the witnesses have signed in his presence and in the presence of one another and he has signed in their presence; the same requirement is imposed independently, and as a condition of the deed having any legal force, by the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1)(a), under which no transfer of immovable property shall be in force or avail in law unless the deed is in writing and is signed by every executant and the witnesses in the presence of a licensed notary public present at the same time and in the presence of one another and is attested by that notary. Notaries Ordinance s.34(1)(c) makes a breach of a rule in section 31 an offence punishable on summary trial before a Magistrate by a fine, in addition to any civil liability the notary may incur; it says nothing about the validity of the instrument, so the notary's liability and the standing of the deed are separate questions.

Application: Saman's omission of his name, notary number, office address and telephone number, and of his employer's registered address, from the front page is a failure to observe rule (17)(c). It concerns the completion of the face of the instrument and not the manner in which the parties bound themselves, and no other written law makes those particulars a condition of a valid transfer. On that default alone the deed would be protected by the main limb of s.33. The second default is of a different character. The executant Nimal Perera and one witness signed at Saman's office, but the second witness signed at his own house on the following day, in the absence of the executant and of the other witness, and Saman attested that all had signed in his presence and in the presence of one another. That is a breach of rule (12); more importantly, it means the deed was not signed by the executant and the witnesses in the presence of a licensed notary present at the same time and in the presence of one another, which the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1)(a) makes a condition of a transfer of immovable property being in force or availing in law. Because the deed affects a house and premises at Colombo 05, that Ordinance applies, and the proviso to s.33 withholds from this deed the protection the section would otherwise give. Saman attested at his own office at Colombo 07, within the Judicial Zone of Colombo for which he is licensed, so no separate objection arises under section 31 rule (22) as to the area of attestation. Independently of the fate of the deed, Saman remains exposed to prosecution and a fine under s.34 of the Notaries Ordinance, together with any civil liability towards the parties, and the urgency of his employer's instruction is no answer to either.

Conclusion: The Deed can be invalidated, but not simply because rules in section 31 of the Notaries Ordinance were broken. Defaults that are matters of form, such as the omission of the notary's particulars from the front page, do not by themselves affect the instrument, because of Notaries Ordinance s.33. The deed is at risk here because the failure to have the executant and both witnesses sign in Saman's presence and in the presence of one another is also non-compliance with the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2(1)(a); on the terms of that provision the transfer is not in force and does not avail in law, and by the proviso to s.33 the Notaries Ordinance does not cure it. Saman is in addition liable to be punished under Notaries Ordinance s.34 and to answer civilly to the parties for the loss.
