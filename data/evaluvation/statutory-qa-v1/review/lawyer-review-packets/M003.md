### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Herath must obtain the approval of the Divisional Secretary before mortgaging to a licensed commercial bank land that he holds on a grant issued under the Land Development Ordinance.

Rule: Land Development Ordinance s.2 defines a 'holding' as land alienated by grant under the Ordinance, defines 'disposition' to include a mortgage, and defines 'licensed commercial bank' by reference to the banking legislation. Section 19(6) requires every grant to contain conditions that the owner shall not dispose of a divided portion or undivided share smaller than the unit of subdivision or the minimum fraction, and shall not dispose of the holding except with the prior approval of the Government Agent. Section 19(7) then provides that notwithstanding paragraph (b) of subsection (6), the approval of the Divisional Secretary shall not be required when mortgaging such holding to a licenced commercial bank, the State Mortgage and Investment Bank, the Development Finance Corporation of Ceylon, the National Development Bank of Sri Lanka, the National Housing Development Authority, the Housing Development Finance Corporation Bank of Sri Lanka, any registered society or other prescribed institution. Section 43(b) independently provides that the owner of a holding shall not mortgage it to any person other than a licensed commercial bank or one of the same listed institutions. The position of a permit-holder is different: by s.46 no permit-holder may effect any disposition of the land alienated on the permit, except that with the written consent of the Government Agent he may mortgage his interest to a registered society of which he is a member, and any other disposition is null and void.

Application: Herath is not a permit-holder. He paid the whole purchase amount, complied with the conditions of his permit, and a registered grant of the land was issued to him, so the land is a 'holding' and he is its owner. A mortgage is a disposition, so the condition in his grant that he shall not dispose of the holding except with prior approval is engaged on its face. The proposed mortgagee is a licensed commercial bank, which is both within the class of permissible mortgagees under s.43(b) and within the class that triggers the exemption in s.19(7). That exemption is expressed to displace paragraph (b) of subsection (6), which is precisely the approval condition, and it applies to the whole of the holding, which is what Herath proposes to mortgage rather than a divided portion or an undivided share. Had Herath still been a permit-holder, no approval would have helped him, because a permit-holder may mortgage only to a registered society of which he is a member and then only with the written consent of the Government Agent, and any other disposition would be null and void.

Conclusion: No. Herath does not require the approval of the Divisional Secretary to mortgage the land. Because he holds under a grant and the mortgagee is a licensed commercial bank, Land Development Ordinance s.19(7) expressly removes the approval requirement that s.19(6)(b) would otherwise impose, and s.43(b) permits a mortgage to such a bank. The advice should be qualified in two ways: the exemption covers only the approval condition, so the other conditions of the grant, in particular the bar in s.19(6)(a) on disposing of a divided portion or an undivided share below the prescribed unit, continue to apply; and the exemption depends on the bank in fact falling within the definition of a licensed commercial bank, which should be confirmed before the bond is drawn.
