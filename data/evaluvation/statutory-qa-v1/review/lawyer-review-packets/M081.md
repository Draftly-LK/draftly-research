## Original background

In 1978 March Nimal Perera had purchased a land in extent of 26 Perches situated in Kohuwala for Rs.120,000/- and constructed a house on it. The cost of construction of the house was Rs. 2,900,000/- He wishes to gift this house and land to his son Aruna and instructs you to prepare a Deed of Gift. The present market value of the house and property is Rs.5,700,000/-

## M081-Q01 (keep, hop count 3)

### Question

How would an Assessor attached to the Western Provincial Council arrive at the value of the property for the purposes of Stamp duty in this Deed of Gift?

### Legal issues

- ISS-M081-Q01-01: Whether a Deed of Gift of land and a house in Kohuwala falls within the Western Provincial Council's stamp duty charge, so that a Provincial Assessor of Revenue values it.
- ISS-M081-Q01-02: What 'value' the Assessor must ascertain for gifted immovable property, and which limb of the statutory definition applies where the donor acquired the property in March 1978.
- ISS-M081-Q01-03: How the cost of the house built after acquisition and the present market value of the whole property enter that computation.

### Draft answer

Issue: How a Provincial Assessor of Revenue attached to the Western Provincial Council arrives at the value of the Kohuwala land and house for stamp duty purposes on the proposed Deed of Gift from Nimal Perera to his son Aruna.

Rule: Stamp duty is charged on every instrument relating to a transfer of immovable property situated in the Western Province (Western Province Financial Statute No. 6 of 1990, s.37(a)), and 'Transfer' is defined in s.106 to include a gift, so a Deed of Gift is chargeable. By s.48(1) the duty is calculated on the 'value' of the property conveyed. 'Value' is defined in s.106. Because a 'gift' is a transfer made voluntarily and without consideration in money or money's worth, the gift limbs of that definition apply rather than the ordinary open-market limb. Where the gifted immovable property was acquired by the donor after 31 March 1977, paragraph (c) of the definition of 'value' requires the Assessor to compare two figures: (i) the price which in his opinion the property would have fetched if sold in the open market on the date the donor acquired it, increased by the cost of improvements, alterations and additions made after acquisition and before the date of the deed of gift; and (ii) the price which in his opinion the property would have fetched if sold in the open market on the date of the instrument of gift. The value is whichever of the two is lower. Both figures are the Assessor's own opinion, and for that purpose s.63 lets him call for any document or other evidence needed to prove the facts bearing on chargeability and amount, on an adjudication under s.62 which ends in a written determination and an endorsement under s.64.

Application: Nimal Perera acquired the 26-perch Kohuwala land in March 1978, which is after 31 March 1977, so paragraph (c) of the definition of 'value' governs and paragraph (b), which uses a 31 March 1977 base, does not. For limb (i) the Assessor forms an opinion of the open-market price of the property on the date of acquisition in March 1978; the Rs.120,000 paid on the 1978 purchase is the evidence of that price, although the test is the Assessor's opinion of the open-market price and not the recited consideration. To that he adds the cost of the improvements made after acquisition and before the deed of gift, namely the Rs.2,900,000 spent on constructing the house, giving Rs.120,000 + Rs.2,900,000 = Rs.3,020,000. For limb (ii) the Assessor forms an opinion of what the land and house together would fetch in the open market on the date of the Deed of Gift, which on the instructions is Rs.5,700,000. He then takes the lower of the two, and Rs.3,020,000 is lower than Rs.5,700,000. In arriving at each figure the Assessor may under s.63 require production of the 1978 deed of purchase, the building plan and the bills, receipts or a quantity surveyor's statement evidencing the Rs.2,900,000 construction cost, and material on current prices in Kohuwala; he is not bound by the parties' own figures, so both limbs remain open until he is satisfied on the evidence. The notary drawing the deed must independently satisfy himself under s.78(2) that the value on which duty is paid represents the value of the property conveyed.

Conclusion: The Assessor does not simply take the present market value of Rs.5,700,000. Applying paragraph (c) of the definition of 'value' in s.106 of the Western Province Financial Statute No. 6 of 1990, he compares the March 1978 open-market price of Rs.120,000 increased by the Rs.2,900,000 cost of constructing the house, that is Rs.3,020,000, with the open-market price at the date of the gift of Rs.5,700,000, and adopts the lower figure. The value of the property for stamp duty on this Deed of Gift is therefore Rs.3,020,000, subject to the Assessor's own opinion of the two open-market prices on the evidence produced to him.
