## Original background

Lot 3 of the land called Mahatenna in extent of 30 perches in Plan No. 251 dated 5/7/1999 made by A.Samaranayaka Liscensed Surveyor is registered in the Homagama Land Registry under Vol/Folio A 350/25. The owner, sub divided Lot 3 into 2 lots namely Lots 34 and 3B as depicted in Plan No.255 dated 8/12/2000 made by the same Surveyor. He sold Lot 3A to Dininda Perera by Deed No.351 attested by Gamini Herath, Notary Public. Lot 3A was sold at a price of Rupees 7 million but the Commissioner of Revenue of the Western Provincial Council in his opinion of valuation for stamp duty, values the property at Rupees 7.5 million. Dininda Perera constructed a house on Lot 3A and Leased it out to Nimal Fernando for 2 years on a monthly rental of Rs. 75,000/-, He obtained 1 years rent as an advance to set off against 2nd years rent. When the Lease Agreement was submitted for registration the Land Registry informed that it could not be registered because the sub division of Lot 3A is wrongfully registered in the same folio as the original land (Lot 3) is registered namely in Vol/Folio A 350/25.

## M098-Q02 (keep, hop count 2)

### Question

Write the procedure to follow to get this error corrected.

### Legal issues

- ISS-M098-Q02-01: Whether registering the divided Lot 3A in Vol/Folio A 350/25, the folio of the parent Lot 3, is an error or omission made in registering an instrument within section 35(1) of the Registration of Documents Ordinance.
- ISS-M098-Q02-02: What steps the Registration of Documents Ordinance requires to have that registration corrected, and which officer takes each step.
- ISS-M098-Q02-03: What effect the correction has on priorities accrued before it, and what happens where notice to a party to Deed No. 351 cannot be issued.

### Indispensable provisions

- PROV-M098-001 Registration of Documents Ordinance No. 23 of 1927 s.35((1)) [verified] corpus `23-1927/s35`
  Excerpt: "Where it is shown to the satisfaction of the Registrar-General that any error or omission has been made in registering any instrument, whether before or after the commencement of this Ordinance, the Registrar-General shall issue notice in writing in the prescribed form (a) in the case of a deed to the parties thereto; or (b) in the case of a will to the executor or administrator, as the case may be; or (c) in the case of any other instrument to the person who presented it for registration, and order such error or omission to be corrected"
  Why: This is the operative correction power. It fixes the first three steps of the procedure: the error must be shown to the satisfaction of the Registrar-General, who then issues written notice in the prescribed form to the parties to the deed (Deed No. 351), and orders the correction. Registering the sub-divided Lot 3A in the parent folio Vol/Folio A 350/25 is an error or omission made in registering an instrument. Temporal: Subsection (1) was repealed and re-substituted by section 3 of the Registration of Documents (Amendment) Act, No. 50 of 1982; the consolidated corpus text is that substituted text and it is the text in force in October 2018. No later amendment in the corpus (Nos. 5 of 1990, 21 of 2013, 32 of 2022, 18 of 2024) touches section 35.
- PROV-M098-002 Registration of Documents Ordinance No. 23 of 1927 s.35((1)) [verified] corpus `23-1927/s35`
  Excerpt: "and order such error or omission to be corrected and the correction shall then be made by the Registrar concerned in the prescribed manner, but shall not affect any priority accrued before the correction is made"
  Why: Fixes the last two steps: after the Registrar-General's order the correction is made by the Registrar concerned (here the Registrar of Lands, Homagama) in the prescribed manner, and it does not disturb any priority that accrued before it. Without this the procedure is incomplete and the effect of the correction on priority is unstated. Temporal: Same 1982 substituted text as PROV-M098-001; in force at the matter reference date of October 2018.
- PROV-M098-004 Registration of Documents Ordinance No. 23 of 1927 s.14((1)) [partially_verified] corpus `23-1927/s14`
  Excerpt: "Every instrument presented for registration shall be registered in the book allotted to the division in which the land affected by the instrument is situated and in the folio in which 1974] the first registered instrument affecting the same land is registered, or in another folio (whether of the same volume or of another volume) bearing a separate number, opened in continuation thereof, cross reference being entered in the prescribed manner so as to connect the said folios"
  Why: Supplies the standard that was breached and so identifies what the error is. It is what makes the registration of the divided Lot 3A in the parent folio of Lot 3 wrong: the sub-divided lot should have been carried to a separate folio opened in continuation, connected by cross reference. Without it the assertion that there is an error in registering has no statutory support. Temporal: Amended by Act No. 14 of 1974; the corpus consolidated text carries the 1974 wording (the stray '1974]' marker is an artefact of the consolidated source). In force at October 2018.

### Draft answer

Issue: By what procedure under the Registration of Documents Ordinance can the wrongful registration of the divided Lot 3A in Vol/Folio A 350/25, the folio of the parent Lot 3, be corrected, so that instruments affecting Lot 3A, including the lease to Nimal Fernando, can be duly registered?

Rule: Registration of Documents Ordinance s.14(1) requires every instrument to be registered in the book allotted to the division in which the land lies and in the folio in which the first registered instrument affecting the same land is registered, or in another folio bearing a separate number opened in continuation of it, with cross references entered in the prescribed manner to connect the folios; proviso (a) allows the Registrar, if he thinks fit, to enter an instrument in a new folio with cross references to any previous registration affecting the same land or any part of it. By s.14(2) an instrument is not deemed duly registered unless it is registered in accordance with s.14. Where it is shown to the satisfaction of the Registrar-General that any error or omission has been made in registering any instrument, s.35(1) requires the Registrar-General to issue notice in writing in the prescribed form, in the case of a deed to the parties to it, and to order the error or omission to be corrected; the correction is then made by the Registrar concerned in the prescribed manner, but does not affect any priority accrued before the correction is made. The proviso to s.35(1) allows the Registrar-General to make the same order on proof by any person interested where, by reason of death, legal incapacity or any other sufficient cause, that notice cannot be issued. The form of the notice and the manner of the correction are prescribed by regulation under s.49 and by the forms in the Second Schedule under s.50, and by s.2(6) the Registrar-General may himself exercise a Registrar's powers.

Application: Lot 3A is a divided and defined portion of Lot 3, carved out by Plan No. 255 of 8/12/2000 and conveyed to Dininda Perera by Deed No. 351. Under s.14(1) the registration of Deed No. 351 should have been carried to a folio bearing a separate number opened in continuation of Vol/Folio A 350/25 and connected to it by cross reference, or under proviso (a) to a new folio with cross references; entering it in the parent folio of Lot 3 itself is therefore not registration in accordance with s.14, and by s.14(2) it is not due registration. That is an error or omission made in registering an instrument for the purposes of s.35(1), and it is the reason the Land Registry says the lease cannot be registered. The procedure is administrative. A person interested, Dininda Perera as proprietor of Lot 3A or the party who presented the lease, applies in writing to the Registrar-General of Lands through the Homagama Land Registry, setting out the error and producing the material that shows it: Deed No. 351, Plan No. 251 and Plan No. 255, and a certified extract of Vol/Folio A 350/25. If the error is shown to the Registrar-General's satisfaction, s.35(1) obliges him to issue notice in writing in the prescribed form; because the instrument concerned is a deed, notice goes to the parties to Deed No. 351, that is the vendor and Dininda Perera, and he orders the error to be corrected. If a party cannot be noticed because of death, legal incapacity or any other sufficient cause, the proviso lets the Registrar-General make the order on proof to his satisfaction by any person interested. On that order the Registrar of Lands, Homagama, makes the correction in the prescribed manner, carrying the registration of Lot 3A to a separate folio opened in continuation of, and cross referenced to, Vol/Folio A 350/25. By the closing words of s.35(1) the correction does not affect any priority accrued before it is made, so it cannot prejudice interests already ranked in the old folio. Once the folio is corrected the lease to Nimal Fernando can be presented again for registration in the folio of Lot 3A. If instead the Registrar makes a formal order refusing registration, a separate route exists in s.38(1), an appeal to the Registrar-General within thirty days, but that addresses the refusal and not the folio error.

Conclusion: The error is corrected administratively under the Registration of Documents Ordinance and not by court order. An interested party applies to the Registrar-General of Lands showing the error; on being satisfied, the Registrar-General issues notice in the prescribed form to the parties to Deed No. 351 and orders the correction under s.35(1); the Registrar of Lands, Homagama, then makes the correction in the prescribed manner by carrying the registration of Lot 3A into a separate folio cross referenced to Vol/Folio A 350/25 as s.14(1) requires; and the correction leaves untouched any priority that accrued before it was made. The lease can then be presented for registration in the corrected folio.
