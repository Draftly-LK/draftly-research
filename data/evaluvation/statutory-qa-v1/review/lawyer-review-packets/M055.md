## M055-Q01 (enrich, hop count 3)

### Question

Can he fulfill his desire?

### Enriched background

Nimal Siriwardhana wishes to gift his property registered under the Registration Title Act No.21 of 1998 to his two daughters subject to life interest of him. The property is a single land parcel of about forty perches at Meegoda, in the Homagama Divisional Secretary's Division of the Colombo District, separately delineated as Parcel No. 214 on the cadastral map for that area and entered in the Title Register kept at the Homagama Title Registry, where Nimal is recorded as the sole owner with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership and no encumbrance stands registered against the parcel. His daughters, Kanchana Siriwardhana and Dilini Siriwardhana, are both over eighteen years of age. Nimal wants the two of them to take the whole parcel together, in undivided equal half shares, under one deed of gift, and he intends to keep for himself, for the rest of his life, the right to live on the land and to take its produce. The parcel has never been sub-divided; no sub-division plan of it has been surveyed or registered, and Nimal has made no application to the Registrar of Title to sub-divide it. He proposes to have the deed drawn and attested by a notary public in October 2021.

### Legal issues

- ISS-M055-Q01-01: Whether a gift of a land parcel registered under the Registration of Title Act may reserve a life interest to the donor and still be registrable.
- ISS-M055-Q01-02: Whether one registered land parcel may be gifted by a single instrument to two donees in common.
- ISS-M055-Q01-03: Whether the donor may instead gift a divided portion of the parcel to each daughter, and what he must do first.
- ISS-M055-Q01-04: What form the gift must take and when it takes effect.

### Draft answer

Issue: Whether Nimal Siriwardhana can carry out his wish to gift his land, registered under the Registration of Title Act No. 21 of 1998, to his two daughters while reserving a life interest to himself: that is, whether a deed of gift of the registered parcel to both daughters in common, subject to his life interest, can validly be made and registered, and if not, what he must do instead.

Rule: The land is a land parcel registered under the Registration of Title Act No. 21 of 1998, so section 39 applies: no registered land parcel or interest in it may be transferred or dealt with except in accordance with the Act, and every disposition otherwise effected is void. A deed of gift is an 'instrument' within section 75, a document having the effect of conveying title to and interest in a land parcel in the prescribed form, and a life interest is an 'interest in land parcel' under the same section, being an interest less than full ownership. On the life interest, section 46 forbids the Registrar of Title to register any instrument purporting to convey title or an interest on the happening of any event or the fulfilment of any condition at a future time, but the proviso to section 46 makes a conveyance of title subject to life interest acceptable for registration. On the two donees, section 48 provides that, except in accordance with the provisions of the Act, an instrument pertaining to the ownership of a registered land parcel made in favour of two or more persons in common is void and shall not be registered. Section 47 adds that no part of a parcel registered with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership or a Second Class Title of Ownership may be transferred or otherwise dealt with unless the registered owner has first sub-divided it and the sub-divided parcel has itself been registered, and section 36(1) allows a registered owner to sub-divide by registering a sub-division plan in accordance with the Act. Any instrument must be in the prescribed form, signed by the parties and two witnesses and attested by a Notary Public under section 43; it is registered only if the Registrar of Title is satisfied that the requirements of the Act are met under section 45(3); it takes effect only on registration under section 49; and under section 38 no donee is entitled to any title or interest until it is registered.

Application: Nimal is the registered sole owner of a single land parcel with a First Class Title of Absolute Ownership, so section 39 governs everything he proposes to do with it. The reservation to himself of the right to live on the land and take its produce for the rest of his life is a life interest, an interest less than full ownership within section 75, and not a conveyance postponed to a future event or condition; a gift of the parcel subject to that reservation therefore falls within the proviso to section 46 and is acceptable for registration on that ground. The difficulty lies in the other half of the plan. He wants both daughters to take the whole parcel together in undivided equal half shares under one deed, which is an instrument pertaining to the ownership of a registered land parcel made in favour of two persons in common. Section 48 makes such an instrument void and directs that it shall not be registered, and under section 45(3) the Registrar of Title would refuse it; by section 49 it would in any event take effect only on registration, and by section 38 neither daughter would acquire anything. Nor can he sidestep this by gifting a divided half of the existing parcel to each daughter, because section 47 bars any dealing with part of a registered parcel until the owner has first sub-divided it and the sub-divided parcels have been registered, and on these facts the parcel has never been sub-divided and no sub-division plan has been surveyed or registered. What he can do is proceed under section 36(1): as registered owner he may apply to sub-divide the parcel and register a sub-division plan, and once each of the two resulting land parcels is registered he may execute a separate deed of gift of one parcel to each daughter, each gift reserving his life interest under the proviso to section 46, each in the prescribed form and attested by a Notary Public under section 43, and each taking effect on registration under section 49. Whether the forty-perch parcel can in fact be divided in two depends on the prescribed extent and on whether the area has been declared one in which the minimum economic unit principle applies under section 70, which cannot be determined on the facts given.

Conclusion: Only in part, and not in the form he proposes. The life interest is no obstacle, because the proviso to section 46 expressly allows a conveyance of title subject to a life interest to be registered. But a single deed of gift of the one registered parcel to both daughters in common would be void and unregistrable under section 48, and a gift of a divided portion to each is barred by section 47 while the parcel remains unsub-divided. Nimal can achieve substantially what he wants only if he first sub-divides the parcel under section 36 and registers the sub-division, and then gifts one resulting parcel to each daughter by a separate instrument reserving his life interest, subject to the sub-division being permissible under the prescribed minimum extent for the area.
