### Draft answer

Issue: What documents an attorney-at-law and notary must call for in order to examine the title of a twenty-acre rubber estate in Kalutara that a Sri Lankan private limited company proposes to buy, and which statutory duties fix that list.

Rule: Before drawing any deed affecting an interest in land the notary must search or cause to be searched the registers in the land registry to ascertain the state of the title and whether any prior deed affecting the land has been registered (Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (17)(a)). The registry books, duplicates and indexes may be searched by a person interested or his attorney-at-law, and certified copies of or extracts from them may be obtained (Registration of Documents Ordinance s.42), so the search is reduced to a certified extract of the relevant folio. That extract governs the purchaser's position because an unregistered instrument is void against a later instrument taken for valuable consideration and duly registered (Registration of Documents Ordinance s.7). The notary must also use his best endeavours to obtain the title deed of the property (Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (33)), and only notarially executed and attested writings can operate as dispositions of land at all (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2). The deed and the registered instruments must describe the land by boundaries, extent and situation (Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (16)(a); Registration of Documents Ordinance s.13(1)), which requires the survey plan and the schedules of the earlier deeds. An instrument chargeable with stamp duty cannot be registered or acted upon unless duly stamped (Stamp Duty Act s.33). Paper title is not conclusive: ten years of undisturbed adverse possession entitles the possessor to a decree (Prescription Ordinance s.3). Where the property is a rubber estate within the Tea and Rubber Estates (Control of Fragmentation) Act, a transfer of ownership is null and void without a previously obtained certificate of consent of the Board, which must be attached to the deed (s.3(1) and s.3(4)); but a rubber estate for that Act means one of not less than one hundred acres registered under the Rubber Control Act (s.25). Agricultural land above fifty acres is subject to the ceiling (Land Reform Law s.3), and where such land is owned by a private company its shareholders are deemed to own it in proportion to their shares (s.8). Transfer of title of land to a Sri Lankan company with direct or indirect foreign shareholding of fifty per cent or above is prohibited (Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act s.2(1)(b)), and where the transferee is a Sri Lankan company with less than fifty per cent foreign shareholding the Registrar of Lands must require the company secretary to submit documentary proof of that fact (s.4(3)); the certificate of incorporation, articles, share register, register of directors and secretaries and register of charges are records the company must keep available for inspection (Companies Act s.120).

Application: Applying these rules to the estate at Bombuwala, the following must be called for. First, the vendor's chain of notarial deeds, namely Deed of Transfer No. 4562 of 2003, Deed of Gift No. 1120 of 1979 and the earlier deeds behind them, together with any testamentary or partition proceedings through which the land devolved, since only notarially attested writings can pass title (Prevention of Frauds Ordinance s.2) and the notary is bound to obtain the title deed (Notaries Ordinance s.31 rule (33)). Second, a certified extract of the folio of the Kalutara Land Registry in which the land is registered, together with certified copies of every instrument noted on it, obtained under s.42 of the Registration of Documents Ordinance in performance of the search duty under s.31 rule (17)(a); this is what discloses Mortgage Bond No. 987 of 2016, and because of s.7 of that Ordinance the purchaser's protection depends on the state of the register rather than on the vendor's assurance. Third, the instrument of discharge or a formal release of Bond No. 987, since the vendor's statement that the loan is settled is not itself a document of title. Fourth, Plan No. 2143 of 1998 and a current survey plan and report, so that the description by boundaries and extent required by s.31 rule (16)(a) and by s.13(1) of the Registration of Documents Ordinance can be checked against the ground; the two acres fenced by the neighbour for fifteen years must be identified on that survey, because under s.3 of the Prescription Ordinance such possession may already have displaced the paper title to that portion, so evidence of possession and of the neighbour's claim must be called for as well. Fifth, proof that stamp duty was duly paid on the deeds in the chain, since s.33 of the Stamp Duty Act denies effect to an instrument that is not duly stamped. Sixth, the extent and registration status of the estate: the property is twenty acres, so it is below the one hundred acres required by s.25 of the Tea and Rubber Estates (Control of Fragmentation) Act, and the certificate of consent contemplated by s.3(1) and s.3(4) of that Act is not called for, but the extent and registration must be documented before that conclusion can be drawn. Seventh, a declaration of the vendor's agricultural landholdings, and of the holdings of the purchaser's shareholders, to test the fifty-acre ceiling in s.3 of the Land Reform Law read with the deeming provision in s.8. Eighth, the purchaser's own corporate documents, namely the certificate of incorporation, articles, share register, register of directors and secretaries and a board resolution authorising the purchase, and, because thirty per cent of its shares are held by a person who is not a citizen of Sri Lanka, a certificate of the company secretary with the share register behind it showing that foreign shareholding is below fifty per cent, which s.4(3) of the Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act requires the Registrar of Lands to call for and without which registration will be refused.

Conclusion: The documents to be called for are: the vendor's original chain of notarial title deeds and any testamentary or partition proceedings in the chain; a certified extract of the relevant folio of the Kalutara Land Registry with certified copies of the registered instruments noted on it; the discharge of the registered mortgage bond; the survey plan and a current survey report with evidence of possession, including the position of the neighbour occupying two acres; proof of payment of stamp duty on the deeds in the chain; documents establishing the extent and registration status of the estate, which on these facts show that no certificate of consent of the fragmentation Board is required because the estate is twenty acres and not one hundred; declarations of the agricultural landholdings of the vendor and of the purchaser's shareholders; and the purchaser's certificate of incorporation, articles, share register, register of directors and secretaries, board resolution and a company secretary's certificate of foreign shareholding below fifty per cent. The examination is not complete on the papers alone: possession must be verified on the ground, and the undischarged bond and the encroached two acres must be resolved before the transfer is drawn.
