## Original background

Lihini Manapitiya, a lecturer of AVI University purchased a land situated in Rajagiriya in 2001 for Rs.14,000,000/-. She wishes to obtain a loan of Rupees Two Million (Rs.2,000,000/=) from National Savings Bank to build a house.

Lihini Manapitiya was asked to sign a Declaration under section 47(A) of the Mortgage Act by the Bank.

## M059-Q01 (keep, hop count 3)

### Question

Why does bank request her to execute such declaration?

### Legal issues

- ISS-M059-Q01-01: What, under the Mortgage Act, is the legal effect of a mortgagor executing a separate declaration under section 47A at the execution of a mortgage bond?
- ISS-M059-Q01-02: What is the default position under section 46 that such a declaration renounces, and can a lender achieve the same result without it?
- ISS-M059-Q01-03: Are the statutory gateway conditions of section 47A satisfied on these facts, namely a lending institution as mortgagee and a loan principal exceeding one hundred and fifty thousand rupees?

### Draft answer

Issue: Why does the National Savings Bank ask Lihini Manapitiya to execute a separate declaration under section 47A of the Mortgage Act at the time she mortgages her Rajagiriya land to secure the Rs.2,000,000 housing loan?

Rule: Mortgage Act s.46 provides that no decree in a hypothecary action on a mortgage of land, and no decree in an action for the recovery of moneys due on such a mortgage, may order any property other than the mortgaged property to be sold, and no other property is liable to be sold in execution of such a decree. By s.47 that restriction operates notwithstanding anything in any other law or in any mortgage bond or other instrument, so a lender cannot escape it by a clause in the bond. Section 47A creates the single statutory exception. Where, at the time of execution of a mortgage bond in favour of a lending institution for a loan the principal of which exceeds one hundred and fifty thousand rupees, the mortgagor executes a separate instrument attested by the notary attesting the bond and by the witnesses to it, containing a special declaration renouncing the benefit of s.46 together with the notary's endorsement that he has explained the effect of the renunciation, then in addition to the mortgaged property any other property belonging to the mortgagor becomes liable to be ordered to be sold and to be sold under the decree in an action upon the mortgage, with Civil Procedure Code s.218 applying mutatis mutandis to the seizure and sale of that other property. Section 47A(7)(d) includes the National Savings Bank in the definition of 'lending institution'. Section 47A(2) requires that no process issue against other property until the mortgaged property has been sold and its proceeds applied to the decree, and s.47A(3) makes it the notary's duty to explain the renunciation and its consequences to the mortgagor.

Application: The mortgagee is the National Savings Bank, which is a lending institution within Mortgage Act s.47A(7)(d). The principal of the loan is Rs.2,000,000, which exceeds the statutory threshold of one hundred and fifty thousand rupees in s.47A(1). Both gateway conditions are therefore met and the section is available on these facts. Absent the declaration, s.46 would confine the Bank on any decree obtained on the bond to the Rajagiriya land alone, and by s.47 the Bank could not enlarge its remedy by drafting a wider recovery clause into the bond itself. By asking Lihini Manapitiya to sign a separate instrument under s.47A renouncing the benefit of s.46, the Bank secures the right, if the debt is not satisfied, to have her other property ordered sold under the decree in an action upon the mortgage, the seizure and sale being governed mutatis mutandis by Civil Procedure Code s.218. That right is a shortfall remedy rather than a free choice of assets, since s.47A(2) bars process against her other property until the mortgaged land is sold and the proceeds applied. The instrument must be separate, attested by the same notary and witnesses, and carry the notary's endorsement, and under s.47A(3) the notary must explain to her that she is giving up the s.46 protection.

Conclusion: The Bank requests the declaration in order to escape the restriction in Mortgage Act s.46, which would otherwise limit its recovery on a mortgage decree to the mortgaged Rajagiriya land. Because the National Savings Bank is a lending institution under s.47A(7)(d) and the Rs.2,000,000 principal exceeds the Rs.150,000 threshold, a declaration executed under s.47A(1) renouncing the benefit of s.46 makes Lihini Manapitiya's other property liable to be sold under a decree in an action upon the mortgage, subject to the mortgaged land being sold first under s.47A(2). In short, the Bank wants recourse against her general assets for any shortfall, and s.47A is the only lawful way to obtain it. Whether that recourse is ever exercised depends on facts not given, namely what other property she owns and whether the mortgaged land would realise enough to satisfy the debt.
