### Draft answer

Issue: What value should be stated in the Deed of Gift by which Rohan Perera gifts his Maharagama house and land to his son Wasantha, for the purpose of stamp duty, given that he bought the property in December 1977 for Rs.250,000/-, improved it at a cost of Rs.800,000/- and it is now worth Rs.6,500,000/-.

Rule: Stamp duty is charged on every instrument relating to a transfer of immovable property situated in the Western Province (Western Province Financial Statute s.37), and Transfer is defined by s.106 of that Statute to mean a sale and to include a gift, so a Deed of Gift is chargeable although nothing is paid for it. Section 48(1) provides that where property is conveyed by an instrument, the duty is calculated on the value of the property conveyed, so the operative figure is a statutory value and not the consideration. For gifted immovable property, value is defined in s.106. Where the donor acquired the property after 31 March 1977, paragraph (c) of that definition applies: value means the lower of (i) the price the property would have fetched in the open market on the date the donor acquired it, increased by the cost of improvements, alterations and additions made after acquisition and before the date of the gift instrument, and (ii) the price it would fetch in the open market on the date of that instrument. The national Act states the same rule for gifted land in Stamp Duty Act s.71, which continues to speak to instruments relating to the transfer of immovable property because Stamp Duty (Special Provisions) Act s.13 excepts such instruments from the displacement of the 1982 Act. Section 78(2) of the Western Province Financial Statute requires the attesting notary to take all reasonable steps to satisfy himself that the value on which the duty is paid represents the value of the property conveyed, and s.78(1) requires the facts affecting chargeability and the amount of duty to be set out in the instrument.

Application: The land is at Maharagama in the Western Province and the instrument is a Deed of Gift, so s.37 read with the definition of Transfer in s.106 brings it into charge; no exemption in s.39 covers a gift from a father to his son. Duty is therefore calculated under s.48(1) on the value of the property conveyed. Rohan Perera acquired the property in December 1977, which is after 31 March 1977, so paragraph (c) of the definition of value applies and paragraph (b), which is pegged to a 31 March 1977 open market price, does not. The first limb is the open market price on the date he acquired the property, which on the facts given is the Rs.250,000/- he paid at arm's length in December 1977, increased by the Rs.800,000/- spent on the upper floor, garage and boundary wall, all of which were built after he acquired the property and before the proposed deed. That limb comes to Rs.1,050,000/-. The second limb is the open market price on the date of the instrument, which the valuation as at October 2022 puts at Rs.6,500,000/-. Paragraph (c) directs that whichever price is the lower is the value, and Rs.1,050,000/- is the lower of the two.

Conclusion: The value to be mentioned in the Deed of Gift for stamp duty purposes is Rs.1,050,000/-, being the Rs.250,000/- open market price at which Rohan Perera acquired the property in December 1977 together with the Rs.800,000/- cost of improvements made before the gift, that sum being lower than the present open market value of Rs.6,500,000/-. The attesting notary should satisfy himself that this figure represents the statutory value and set it out in the deed, and the figure remains open to the Assessor's own opinion of the open market prices at the two relevant dates.
