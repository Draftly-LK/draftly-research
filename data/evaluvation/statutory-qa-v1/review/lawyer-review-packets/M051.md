## Original background

Mr. Alfred Perera purchased a property in Pannipitiya in January 1978 for Rs.160,000/-. He constructed a house on the property spending Rs.900,000/- and now the property is valued at Rs.9,000,000/-. He intends to gift this property to his daughter Himall.

## M051-Q01 (keep, hop count 2)

### Question

How would an Assessor attached to the Western Provincial Council arrive at the value of the property for the purpose of the stamp duty in this Deed of Gift?

### Legal issues

- ISS-M051-Q01-01: Whether a Deed of Gift of land at Pannipitiya is chargeable with stamp duty under the Western Province Financial Statute No. 6 of 1990, and on what base the duty is calculated.
- ISS-M051-Q01-02: Which limb of the definition of 'value' in section 106 applies where the donor acquired the immovable property in January 1978, and what the Assessor must compare under that limb.
- ISS-M051-Q01-03: What evidence the Assessor may call for, and by what procedure he determines and notifies the value and the duty.

  Why: The operative definition. The donor acquired the Pannipitiya land in January 1978, that is after March 31, 1977, so paragraph (c) governs: the Assessor takes the lower of (i) the open-market price at the date of acquisition plus the cost of improvements made before the gift, and (ii) the open-market price at the date of the Deed of Gift. ### Draft answer

Issue: How an Assessor of Revenue of the Western Provincial Council arrives at the value of the Pannipitiya property for stamp duty on the proposed Deed of Gift, where the donor bought the land in January 1978 for Rs.160,000, spent Rs.900,000 building a house on it, and the property is now worth Rs.9,000,000.

Rule: Stamp duty is charged on every instrument relating to a transfer of immovable property situated in the Western Province (Western Province Financial Statute No. 6 of 1990 s.37(a)), and 'transfer' is defined in s.106 to include a gift, so a Deed of Gift is chargeable. Section 48(1) provides that where property is conveyed by an instrument the duty is calculated on the value of the property conveyed. 'Value' is neither the consideration nor simply today's market price: under the definition in s.106, where the property gifted is immovable property acquired by the donor after March 31, 1977, value means the lower of (i) the price the property would have fetched, in the opinion of the Assessor, if sold in the open market on the date the donor acquired it, increased by the cost of improvements, alterations and additions made after acquisition and before the date of the deed, and (ii) the price it would have fetched, in the opinion of the Assessor, if sold in the open market on the date of the instrument of gift. Had the donor acquired the property on or before March 31, 1977, the first figure would instead be built on the open-market price on March 31, 1977. To obtain the determination the deed, with a copy, is brought to the Assessor under s.62 with the prescribed fee of Rs.250; the Assessor determines the duty in his opinion chargeable and notifies it in writing, and under s.63 he may require the applicant to produce documents and other evidence proving the facts relevant to chargeability and amount. Under s.78(2) the attesting notary must separately take all reasonable steps to satisfy himself that the value on which duty is paid represents the value of the property conveyed.

Application: The gift is of immovable property at Pannipitiya, which lies in the Western Province, so s.37(a) applies and the valuing officer is the Provincial Assessor of Revenue. Mr. Perera acquired the property in January 1978, after March 31, 1977, so paragraph (c) of the definition of 'value' governs and the March 31, 1977 valuation date in paragraph (b) is irrelevant. The Assessor therefore builds two figures. For the first, he forms an opinion of the open-market price of the property as at January 1978; the Rs.160,000 paid on that purchase is evidence of that price, and he may call for the 1978 deed under s.63, though the statutory test is his opinion of the open-market price at that date rather than the price recited in the deed. To that he adds the cost of the improvements made after acquisition and before the deed of gift, which on the facts is the Rs.900,000 spent on constructing the house, supported by plans, bills and receipts he may call for. That gives Rs.160,000 + Rs.900,000 = Rs.1,060,000. For the second, he forms an opinion of the open-market price at the date of the Deed of Gift, which on the facts as stated is Rs.9,000,000. He then takes the lower of the two. Nothing is deducted because the donee is the donor's daughter, and no exemption in s.39 covers a family gift. If the gift were made subject to a reservation in favour of the donor, s.48(3) would require the value to be taken as if no reservation had been made; no reservation appears on these facts.

Conclusion: The Assessor takes the lower of Rs.1,060,000 (open-market price at acquisition in January 1978, evidenced by the Rs.160,000 purchase price, plus the Rs.900,000 cost of the house built afterwards) and Rs.9,000,000 (open-market price at the date of the deed), and so arrives at a value of Rs.1,060,000 for the purpose of stamp duty on this Deed of Gift. Both amounts remain the Assessor's opinion of the open-market price at each date under s.106, so the figures given in the scenario are evidence of value rather than binding on him, and the amounts actually adopted turn on the evidence produced under s.63.
