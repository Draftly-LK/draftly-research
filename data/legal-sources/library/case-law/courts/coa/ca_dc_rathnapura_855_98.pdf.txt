Both counsel submitted that there are other Appellants who have filed Petitions of Appeal but they are not being represented today. Several notices had been sent by the Registrar of this Court directing them to be present in Court to pursue their appeals. Those other Appellants are the I I I l f ! In the light of the above, Court decides to consider the merits of this appeal even though the other Appellants are not present in Court to pursue their appeals. By this appeal, the Appellants sought to set aside the order of the learned District Judge made on 28.09.1998. By that order, the application made in the Petition dated 31.07.1997 had been allowed. Application made in that petition by three Petitioners, had been made claiming them as the heirs of the original 4th defendant. The aforesaid Petition dated 31.07.1997 was made in terms of Section 48(4) of the Partition Act No. 21 of 1977. Accordingly, learned District Judge should have granted special leave at the first instance in view of Section 48(4) ( c ), before he makes a final order on the said application. Section 48 (4) (c) of the Partition Act reads thus: (c) If upon inquiry into such application, after prior notice to the parties to the action deriving any interest under the interlocutory decree the court shall upon such terms and conditions as the court in its discretion may impose, which may include an order for payment of costs as well as an order for security for costs, grant special/eave to the applicant. At this stage, both counsel concede that the learned District Judge had over looked granting special leave referred to in the said section 48(4) ( c ) of the Partition Act. They further submit that no such leave had been granted by the learned District Judge in this instance. Therefore, it is clear that the learned District Judge had not followed the procedure referred to in Section 48{4)( c) of the Partition Act. However, having considered the reasons in the order dated 28.09.1998, it is now clear that the learned District Judge should have made 1 an order granting special leave to proceed with the application made by the three Petitioners without making a final order at that stage. Accordingly, having considered the circumstances under which the application made under Section 48(4) ( c ) had been made, this Court varies the order dated 28.09.1998. Accordingly, this Court makes an order, granting special leave to proceed with the petition dated 31.07.1997. In the circumstances, the learned District Judge is directed to follow the procedure found subsequent to the Section 48(4)( c) of the Partition Act. For the aforesaid reasons, the impugned order of the learned District Judge is varied, to the extent mentioned hereinbefore and accordingly, the appeal is allowed to that extent. Appeal allowed.
