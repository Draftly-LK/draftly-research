Fonseka v. Fernando - NLR - 491 of 15 [1912] LKCA 9; (1912) 15 NLR 491 (13 November 1912)

491

Present: Lascelles C.J. and Wood Renton J.

FONSEKA v. FERNANDO.

216-D. C. Kalutara, 4,827.

Annuity-Tacit hypothec over residuary estate-Sale by residuary legatee of land subject to hypothec-Registration by purchaser-

Probate of will not registered by annuitant-Registration Ordinance, 1891, ss. 16, 22.

The plaintiff, who was entitled to an annuity under his father's will, had a tacit hypothec over the testator's residuary estate for the payment of the annuity. The plaintiff brought this action to have it declared that a land which the defendant had bought from the residuary legatee was bound and executable for the payment of the annuity.

Held, that as the annuitant had not registered the probate of the will producing the inventory and a proper description of the land, the tacit hypothec created by the will was void by reason of the registration of the defendant's deed.

THE facts appear from the judgment.

Sampayo, K.C. (with him Cooray), for the appellant.-A tacit hypothec is incapable of registration. It is not contained in any instrument. It is not correct to say that the will creates it. It is created by law, and not by the will. It cannot be brought within the purview of the Registration Ordinance. Therefore it is not rendered void by the registration of defendant's deed. It is contended that the will, not being registered, is void as against the registered deed of the defendant. The argument will not help the defendant, because the will is itself the foundation of his title. He does not claim adversely to the will.

H. A. Jayewardene (with him A. St. V. Jayewardene), for the respondent-The non-registration of the will renders the tacit hypothec created by it void as against a registered conveyance. Had probate of the will been registered, there would have been notice of the tacit hypothec to all the world. The defendant clearly claims an adverse interest within the meaning of the Registration Ordinance.

Sampayo, K.C., in reply.

November 13, 1912. LasElles C.J.-

The plaintiff in this case is entitled under the will of his father, the late Manuel de Fonseka, who died in 1878, to an annuity of Rs. 480; and in a former action (No. 2,832, D. C. Kalutara) it was decided that, in respect of this annuity, the plaintiff had a tacit hypothec over the testator's residuary estate. The plaintiff

492

in his plaint averred that he had received or recovered all sums which were due to him on account of the annuity up to February 11, 1911, and that there was due to him in respect of the subsequent year the sum of Rs. 480. His claim is that a land named Modellagahawatta which the defendant had bought in 1892 from S. E. Fonseka, the residuary legatee, should be declared bound and executable for payment of this sum. The District Judge has decided the case on the ground that the tacit hypothec created by the will is void by reason of the registration of the defendant's deed. I have come to the conclusion that this decision is correct. It is clear, in the first place, that the probate of a will is a registrable instrument under section 16 of The Local Registration Ordinance 1891, and that, as such, it is an instrument to which the provisions of the following sections are applicable. It is also clear that if the annuitant had registered the probate of the will producing the inventory and a proper description of the land in accordance with section 22, the defendant, if he had examined the register, would have had notice of the annuity, and so would have received the protection which the Ordinance is intended to give to purchasers who register their deeds. But the applicability of section 17 depends upon whether the defendant is claiming under his deed an interest which is adverse to that of the plaintiff under the will of Manuel de Fonseka. Apart from authority, it is clear to me that the defendant's interest under his deed is adverse to that of the plaintiff under the will. The defendant's deed conveyed the property to him without mention of the annuity; it was on its face a conveyance free from this encumbrance.

The plaintiff claims that by virtue of the dispositions of the will the property is charged with the payment of the annuity. The interests are thus clearly adverse. The defendant claims the land free of the annuity; the plaintiff claims a charge of the annuity on the land. This was the view adopted by the Privy Council in a somewhat analogous case, namely, Gauder v. Dissanayake.1

It is ingeniously argued that if the will is declared void as against the defendant's deed the foundation of the defendant's title is cut away, inasmuch as his vendor's title is derived from the will. But the will, of course, is void only quoad the adverse interest claimed by the defendant, that is, the right to hold the land free of the tacit hypothec created by the will. It was also contended that the claim was prescribed, but it is not necessary now to enter into that question.

I would dismiss the appeal with costs.

Wood renton J.-

I have had the advantage of reading, and have little to add to, the judgment of my Lord, the Chief Justice. The appellant's tacit hypothec under the will, and the respondent's interest under his

deed, are clearly adverse, and I quite agree that, both in law and as a matter of policy, the former is void as against the latter by reason of the registration of the deed and the non-registration of the probate. The question as to the prescription of the annuity does not arise in view of the appellant's allegation that he had received or recovered all sums due to him on account of the annuity up to February 11, 1911. But I may point out that if would seem to result from the cases of Edwards v. Warden l and Hughes v. Coles 2 that the right to recover any one payment of an annuity accrues whenever it becomes due; that the right to future payments is enforceable whenever each of these becomes due; and that time runs from the date when the first annuity became payable.

Appeal dismissed.
