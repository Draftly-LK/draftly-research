RIZNIYA
v.
NAUSHAD

COURT OF APPEAL
UDALAGAMA, J. AND
NANAYAKKARA, J.
CALA NO. 105/2001
BOARD OF QUAZI NO. 837/88/T
NOVEMBER 27, 2001

Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act, No. 13 of 1951 - S. 43, 44, 50, 60 (1) -Divorce granted by Quazi - Application in Revision to the Board of Quazi refused Is it a final appeal or interlocutory appeal?

On a preliminary objection, that the appellant is precluded from seeking leave to Appeal?

Held :

(1) Once the Board of Quazi makes an order in Revision in accordance with section 44 (3) it has the effect of an order made by Board of Quazi, on appeal from an order by a Quazi which attracts a right of appeal.

(2) The order complained of is an appealable order with leave of the Court first obtained.

APPLICATION for Leave to Appeal from the order of the Board of Quasi.

Case referred to :

1. Amera Jabir v. Fahis Yasmin Jabir - CA No. 134/80 (Not followed).

2. CA No. 784/96.

Farook Thahir for petitioner.

A. Samsudeen for respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

344

January 23, 2002

NANAYAKKARA, J.

The respondent-petitioner-appellant (appellant) who married the applicant-respondent-respondent (respondent) on 11th April, 1996, under the provisions of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act, No. 13 of 1951 made an application for divorce to the Quazi of Colombo was permitted to pronounce Talak which was registered on 05. 12. 2001.

Being dissatisfied with the said pronouncement of Talak and the order pertaining to it, the appellant filed an application before the Board of Quazis under the provisions of sections 43 and 44 of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act seeking the vacation of the order made by the Quazi. Thereafter, the Board of Quazis having considered the submissions of the parties had made an order dismissing the application of the appellant.

It is against this order made by the Board of Quazis, that the appellant has presented this application by way of leave seeking the reliefs prayed for in the petition.

When this matter was taken up for inquiry on 03. 10. 2001, the learned counsel for the applicant-respondent took up a preliminary objection which has a bearing on the maintainability of this application. Basing his objection on section 44 (1) the learned Counsel contended that as the impugned order which is being canvassed by this application has been delivered by the Board of Quazis in the exercise of its revisionary jurisdiction, the appellant is precluded from seeking relief by way of leave to appeal in terms of section 62 (1) of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act.

345

The only matter for determination now is whether there is any validity in the preliminary objection taken. The question of validity depends upon a careful analysis and construction of relevant sections of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act. Section 44 (3) of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act provides thus:

"44 (3) Every order made by the Board of Quazis under this section shall have the same effect as an order made on appeal from an order made by a Quazi."

The main contention of the applicant-respondent was that since the appellant invoked the revisionary jurisdiction of the Board of Quasi in terms of section 44 (1) of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act, the appellant is precluded from prefering an appeal to this Court against an order under sections 43 and 44 of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act, once the Board has exercised its revisionary jurisdiction. In other words it was the submission of the applicant-respondent, only a party aggrieved by an order of the Board of Quazi made under section 60 of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act with leave of the Court of Appeal first obtained is in position to appeal to that Court from such an order. In support of this proposition the applicant has referred us to the following authority, Amera Jabir v. Fahim Yasmin Jabir (1).  But, an entirely different stance appears to have been taken in case CA No. 784/96 as it has interpreted section 60 (1) of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act with reference to other relevant sections of the Act.

A careful consideration of the judgment in CA No. 784/96 makes  it evident that it has construed section 60 (1) of the Muslim Marriages and Divorce Act in conjunction with the other relevant section 43 (3) which treats an order made by the Board of Quazis in the exercise of its revisionary jurisdiction, as an order made on appeal from an

346

order by a Quazi. Therefore, once the Board of Quazi makes an order in revision in accordance with section 44 (3) it has the effect of an order made by the Board of Quazi on appeal from an order by a Quazi which attracts a right of appeal. Therefore, I am of the view that the order that is being canvassed is an appealable order with leave of the Court first obtained.

UDALAGAMA, J. - I agree.

Preliminary objection overruled. Order is an appealable order with leave of the Court first obtained.
