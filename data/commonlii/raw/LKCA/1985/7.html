Dassanayake v. Eastern Produce and Estates Co., Ltd. - SLR - 258, Vol 1 of 1986 [1985] LKCA 7; (1986) 1 Sri LR 258 (11 October 1985)

258

DASSANAYAKE
v.
EASTERN PRODUCE AND ESTATES CO., LTD.

COURT OF APPEAL.
T .D. G. DE ALWIS, J. AND DHEERARATNE, J.
C.A 334/77 (F).
D.C. MOUNT LAVINIA 471/M.
JULY 30, 1985.

Land Reform Law-Motor car given by the owner of an estate to the Superintendent of the Estate for use during the tenure of his office- Termination of Superintendent's employment by owner-Challenge of termination in Labour Tribunal Proceedings -Passage of Land Reform Law vesting all assets of the owner of the estate in the Land Reform Commission during the pendency of the Labour Tribunal proceedings -Section 42B (5) (a) of Land Reform Law-Can former owner maintain action for recovery of car?-Point of law raised for the first time in appeal.

Where the facts were that a motor car had been given by the owner of an estate to the Superintendent of the estate for use during his tenure of office and the employment of the Superintendent had been terminated resulting in the institution of proceedings in the Labour Tribunal challenging the termination and where during the pendency of the Labour tribunal proceedings the Land Reform Law was passed vesting all the assets of the estate by virtue of section 42 (B) (5) (a) of the Land Reform Law-

Held-

The assets of the estate being vested in the Land Reform Commission the motor car too became the property of the Land Reform Commission. The owner of the estate cannot maintain the action for recovery of the motor car as by virtue of s. 42 (8) (5) (a) of the Land Reform Law all the assets of the estate belonging to the former owner became vested in the Land Reform Commission. The point about the passing of title to the car to the Land Reform Commission is a pure question of Law and can be raised for the first time in appeal.

Cases referred to:

(1) Shaw Wallace and Hedges v. Palmerston Tea Co., Ltd and Others [1982] Sri Lanka L.R. Vol. 1, p. 11.

(2) Seetha v. Weerakoon (1948) 1949 NLR 225.

APPEAL from judgment of District Court of Mount Lavinia.

E. D. Wickremanayake for defendant-appellant.

Dr. H.W. Jayawardene, Q.C. with Lakshman Perera and Miss T. Keenawinna for plaintiff-respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

October 11, 1985.
DHEERARATNE, J.

The plaintiff-respondent, a Limited Liability, Company, duly incorporated under the laws of the United Kingdom was the owner of Rothschild Estate, Pussellawa. The defendant-appellant received from the plaintiff-respondent an appointment as the superintendent of the Rothschild estate. To facilitate the defendant-appellant's carrying out his duties as superintendent, the plaintiff-respondent gave him the use of motor car 3 Sri 2951 which was owned by the plaintiff-respondent. This car which was given primarily for the purposes of carrying on the duties of the superintendent, was also permitted to be used for the defendant-appellant's private running when he was not on duty. By letter dated 29.12.1972, the plaintiff-respondent cancelled the appointment of the defendant-appellant. By further letter dated 1.2.1973 the plaintiff-respondent terminated the services of the defendant-appellant and requested him to return the motor car 3 Sri 2951.

When the defendant-appellant's appointment was cancelled by letter dated 29.12.1972, on 18.01.1973 he made representations to the Commissioner of Labour seeking arbitration to settle the dispute which had arisen. The Commissioner of Labour in pursuance of the representation made, summoned both parties for a conference for 5.02.1973. But, before the conference was held, the defendant-appellant's services were terminated as mentioned earlier by letter dated 1.02.1973. Therefore, the defendant-appellant made representations to the Minister of Labour seeking his intervention to refer the industrial dispute to arbitration. Thereupon, the Minister of Labour, by his orders dated 2.03.1973 and 18.10.1973, referred the dispute for arbitration. After the arbitration proceedings commenced and before they were concluded, an important event occurred. The Law No. 39 of 1975 which amended the Land Reform Law No.1 of 1972, came into operation on 17.10.1975, and the Rothschild estate with all its assets vested with the Land Reform Commission.

As the defendant-appellant failed to return the car No. 3 Sri 2951, by plaint dated 1.12.1975 the plaintiff -respondent sued the defendant-appellant seeking the return of the car, or a payment of Rs.40,000 representing its value, and damages at the rate of Rs.100 per day from 8.02.1973 until the return of the car or payment of the value thereof.

260

On 26.07.1976 the defendant-appellant filed his answer and resisted the plaintiff-respondent's claim on the basis that:-

(a) Since arbitration proceedings in respect of his dismissal were pending, his possession of the motor car after 8.02.1973 was not unlawful.

(b) The motor car could not be returned to the plaintiff- respondent as its ownership had vested with the Land Reform Commission as from 17.10.1975 and further that on the directions of the Land Reform Commission the Motor Car had been handed over to the Janatha Estates Development Board on 7.06.1976.

At the trial, no party led any oral evidence. Both parties were content to rest their respective cases on documents which either party accepted. Thus, the main question the learned trial judge was called upon to decide was whether the possession of the motor vehicle by the defendant-appellant after 08.02.1973 was lawful or not. The parties agreed that in event of the court holding that possession of the car by the defendant-appellant was unlawful then, the damages would be Rs.100 Per day.

On the documents produced and the submission made, the learned trial judge came to the following findings the correctness of which cannot be doubted:-

(1) (a) By operation of law No. 1 of 1972 as amended by law No. 39 of 1975, the ownership of the car 3 Sri 2951 had vested with the Land Reform Commission on 17.10.1975, on which day the amending law had come into operation.

(b) The plaintiff-respondent is entitled to receive compensation from the Land Reform Commission in respect of the said vehicle.

(2) The defendant-appellant had no right to retain the possession of the motor car after his services were terminated and that he should have promptly returned it to the plaintiff-respondent.

(3) The defendant-appellant is liable to pay damages for the period 09.02.1973 to 17.10.1975 in respect of the car at the rate of Rs.100 per day.

261

Learned counsel for the defendant -appellant did not seek to canvass these findings of the learned trial judge. However, he took up a new point in this court which was not raised either in the issues or in the course of the trial in the original court. Learned counsel for the plaintiff-respondent objected to this new point being taken up in appeal. For convenience, I shall deal with this objection later.

The plaint in this case, as referred to by me earlier was filed on 01.12.1975 and was subsequently amended on 12.05.1976 to conform with the provisions of the Administration of Justice Law No. 25 of 1975. The new point raised by the learned counsel for the defendant-appellant is that in view of section 42B(5)(a) of the Land Reform Amending Law No. 39 of 1975, which came into operation on 17.10.1975 the right to sue on the cause of action disclosed in this case, did not remain with the plaintiff-respondent, but had become on exclusive right of the Land Reform Commission.

Section 42B (5) (a) of the Land Reform (Amendment) Law No. 39 of 1975 reads as follows:-

"Subject to the provisions of paragraph (b) where any estate land is vested in the Commission the rights and liabilities of the former owner of such estate land under any contract or agreement express or implied, which relates to the purposes of such estate land and which subsists on the day immediately prior to the date of such vesting and the other rights and liabilities of such owner which relate to the running of such estate land and which subsists on such day, shall become the rights and liabilities of the Commission and the amounts required to discharge all such liabilities shall be deducted from the amount of compensation payable in respect of such estate land. "

The right to sue for the return of the car or its value and for consequential damages, was a right related to running of the estate, and this right subsisted on the date immediately prior to the vesting of the Rothschild Estate with the Land Reform Commission. There is no doubt that this right became a right of the Land Reform Commission by operation of law. Learned counsel for the defendant-appellant also invited our attention to a judgment of Samarakoon, C.J., in Shaw Wallace and Hedges v. Palmerston Tea Co., Ltd. and Two Others (1) in which an analogous situation of a 'Liability' of an estate vested with the Land Reform Commission was considered. It appears to me that

262

the learned counsel for the defendant-appellant is correct in his submission that the right to sue the defendant-appellant in the present cause of action became the sole right of the Land Reform Commission.

Let me now deal with the objection raised by the learned counsel for the plaintiff-respondent for this new point being taken up for the first time in appeal. Learned counsel for the plaintiff-respondent contended that all the requisite material for deciding the new point is not before us and that the plaintiff-respondent would have led further evidence if this matter was raised as an issue at the trial. He cited before us the case of Seetha v. Weerakoon (2).

It appears to me that this new point raised in appeal is a pure question of law as to the applicability of section 42B (5) (a) referred to earlier. I can hardly conceive of any evidence the plaintiff-respondent would have chosen to lead at the trial had this matter been raised, lawfully as it might have been, as an issue at the trial. The new point raised in Seetha v. Weerakoon (supra) was a mixed question of law and fact. I am unable to persuade myself to uphold the objection raised by the learned counsel for the plaintiff-respondent.

I would therefore allow the appeal and dismiss the plaintiff-respondent's action. In the circumstances of this case, the defendant-appellant will not be entitled to his costs in the court below and in this court.

T. D. G. DE ALWIS, J. - I agree.

Appeal allowed.
