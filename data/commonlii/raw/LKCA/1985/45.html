Sarath Hulangamuwa v. Siriwardena, Principal, Visakha Vidyalaya. Colombo 5 and Others - SLR - 275, Vol 1 of 1986 [1985] LKCA 45; (1986) 1 Sri LR 275 (20 December 1985)

275

SARATH HULANGAMUWA
v.
SIRIWARDENA, PRINCIPAL, VISAKHA VIDYALAYA, COLOMBO 5 AND OTHERS

COURT OF APPEAL.
SIVA SELLIAH, J. AND T. D. G. DE ALWIS, J.
C.A. APPLICATION No. 1191/84,
NOVEMBER 21 AND 22, 1985.

Certiorari - Refusal to admit child to school - Delay - Non-disclosure of material facts - Discrimination and denial of equal rights.

1. Certiorari being a discretionary remedy will not be granted where there was -

(a) such delay in seeking the remedy as would frustrate the remedy even if it could be granted as the class had reached the third term and the child herself was now overaged.

(b) the absence of the qualification for admission on the proximity of residence rule - the petitioner being in the category not of chief householders but of sub-tenants and occupants of annexes and therefore needing to reside within 18 chains of the school and not as he actually was within 22 chains.

(c) want of uberrimae fides in that there was a non-disclosure of the material facts that the petitioner had a residence in Dehiwela far way from Visakha Vidyalaya and that his child had since gained admission to Bishop's College, Colombo.

2. Discrimination and denial of equal rights cannot be agitated in an application for writ of certiorari and must form the subject of an action for breach of fundamental rights.

Case referred to:

(1) Bisomenike v. C. R. de Alwis (SC 59/61).

APPLICATION for Writ of Certiorari.

Ranbanda Seneviratne with Kumari Dasanayake for petitioner.

Sarath Silva, D. S. G. with Anil Gunaratne, S.C. for respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

276

December 20, 1985.
SIVA SELLIAH, J.

This is an application for Writs of Certiorari and Mandamus on the 1st respondent who is the Principal of Visakha Vidyalaya, Colombo and on the 2-4 respondents who constituted the Board of Appeal, seeking to quash their orders refusing the application of the petitioner to admit his child Ruwanganie Hulangamuwa aged 5 years 11 months (as on 31.1.84) to the Kindergarten class of the said school and for an order directing the 1st respondent to admit the child. The petitioner has alleged that his child's application conformed to all the requirements set out in the circular No. 1981/6 of the Ministry of Education (A3) governing admission of children to the Kindergarten in government schools regarding proximity of residence to the school and that notwithstanding his having produced documents establishing such proximity by A4 (I) - A4 (VII) in the form of Ration Card, National Identity Card, Bank Statements, T.V. Licence, Grama Sevaka's Certificate, Rent Receipts and letter from the Commissioner of Elections, his application was refused on the ground that "residence had not been proved." The petitioner has also complained that "the respondents have failed to do their legal duty in refusing to admit his daughter to Visakha Vidyalaya and that she was still languishing at home." The petitioner in his counter affidavit and at the hearing before this court further contended that there has been discrimination against his child and that at least 15 students were admitted to the Kindergarten class after the interviews concerning his child were over (vide para 6 of the petitioner's counter affidavit) and that two children mentioned in para 9 of the counter affidavit reside further away and have been admitted without proof of residence. The affidavit of the 1st and 3rd respondents while denying the allegations contained in the petition have in para 11 stated that the petitioner's application did not come within the ambit of a chief householder's category specified in section 4 (VI) (a) of circular A3 but came within the category specified in section 4 (VI) (b) which states as follows:

"The balance 20% shall be elected from among applicants who are not chief householders but who could prove that they are genuine residents as sub-tenants or occupants of "annexes". The admission committee shall take special care to satisfy themselves as to the genuineness of this category of residents". (The emphasis mine.)

277

The petitioner has been heard and the documents produced by him considered both by the Principal and the Admission Committee and also by the Appellate Board. The instant application for Writs is to quash both these orders and to direct the Principal to admit the child. This application has been resisted by the respondents who were represented by the learned Deputy Solicitor General, who contended that the long delay in making this application defeated the claim for a Writ; he also contended that the petitioner has concealed certain material facts from this court which militated against the grant of the Writ; he also stated that if indeed the petitioner's child had been discriminated against and other children who had not been named or identified been admitted with less qualification, then the petitioner's remedy was by way of an action for Fundamental Rights in the Supreme Court and not by way of Writ of Certiorari and Mandamus and that this application was misconceived.

The petitioner was informed by letter A5 dated 23.9.83 that his application for admission of his daughter to the Kindergarten was refused as his residence had not been proved. He appealed from that decision to the Appellate Board which after due inquiry also refuse his appeal. In terms of section 7 of circular A3 this appeal has been disposed of before 15.11.83 and it is there stated that "The decisions of the Appellate Board shall be final". The petitioner however appealed to the Minister of Education by letter dated 2.1.84 (A7) who by letter dated 30.3.84 informed the petitioner that the Appellate Board's decision could not be varied. The petitioner has filed this application for Writ only on 10.9.84. The petition contains no explanation for the long delay in making this application for a Writ. It will be seen that the order of the Admission Committee refusing the application was made on 23.9.83, and the order of the Appellate Board refusing the application was made before 15.11.83 and this order was final. Thus this application has been made more than 10 months later. Writs are extraordinary remedies granted to obtain speedy relief under exceptional circumstances and time is of the essence of the application. Even if the petitioner had sought to explore the possibility of getting relief from the Minister notwithstanding the finality that attached to the order of the Appellate Board, he still has delayed 9 months in making this application although the content of his application was for admission of his child to the Kindergarten in January 1984. Indeed as seen by 1R3 by the time the petitioner made this application for Writ in September 1984, two school terms for the

278

year 1984 had already elapsed and the third term had commenced. Manifestly, the child did not qualify for admission thereafter as she would have been overaged for the Kindergarten. The child was 5 years and 11 months on 31.1.84 (vide para 1 of the petition). Thus at date of application for Writ the child was 7 years and 7 months. The laches of the petitioner must necessarily be a determining factor in deciding this application for Writ as the Court will not lend itself to making a stultifying order which cannot be carried out. Fifteen months have now elapsed and the child will be over-aged for the Kindergarten being 8+; besides it has transpired at this hearing that the child has now obtained admission at the Bishop's College, Colombo and is not "languishing at home" without a school as averred in the petition. The learned counsel for petitioner who informed the court that the child has since been admitted to Bishop's College stated he was persisting in this application to vindicate and enforce his rights which have been denied him. In this context too it must be mentioned that a person cannot sleep over his rights but must seek his legal remedy with expedition - particularly where he seeks a Writ which is an extraordinary remedy granted under exceptional circumstances. In the case of Bisomenike v. C. R. de Alwis (1), Sharvananda, J., (as he was then) stated:

"A Writ of Certiorari is issued at the discretion of the court. It cannot be held to be a Writ of right or one issued as a matter of course. The exercise of this discretion by court is governed certain well accepted principles. The court is bound to issue it at the instance of a party aggrieved by the order of an inferior tribunal except in cases where he has disentitled himself to the discretionary relief by reason of his own conduct, submitting to jurisdiction, laches, undue delay or waiver . . . . . . The proposition that the application for Writ must be sought as soon as injury is caused is merely an application of the equitable doctrine that delay defeats equity and the longer the injured person sleeps over his right without any reasonable excuse the chance of his success in Writ application dwindles and the court may reject a Writ application on the ground of unexplained delay ............... An application for a Writ of Certiorari should be filled within a reasonable time".

In the circumstances of this case I am of the view that there has been undue delay in the making of this application and the court can possibly make an order which manifestly cannot be carried out as child will be overaged for the Kindergarten and has already been

279

accommodated at Bishop's College. The petitioner cannot in law insist that his child must be admitted to a school of his choice especially where he has failed to act with expedition in seeking this Writ. I am of the view that to grant this application for Writ in the context of the long delay in seeking it would be detrimental to the proper administration of the school by the Principal. In the context and content of this application this undue delay has defeated this application.

The counsel for petitioner has also strongly contended at the hearing that he had established proximity of residence to the school by he Documents provided by him-namely, tenancy receipts A2 (1)-A2 (4) in respect of the annexe number 175, Galle Road, Bambalapitiya stated in his application, Ration Card, National Identity Card, Bank Statements, T.V. Licence, Grama Sevaka's certificate and letter from the Commissioner of Elections in accordance with section 4(VIII) of circular A3 but that his application had been refused by A5 dated 23.9.83 on the ground that residence had not been proved. He contended very strongly that in the affidavit of the respondent however it was mentioned in para 11 (1) of the objections filed that-

"As non-chief householder this child was not eligible for admission as he was residing quite away from the school and admission for that category was made from within a distance of 18 chains. Petitioner's appeal was rejected. The decision of the Appellate Board is final."

Counsel urged that there was a marked departure from the grounds stated in A5 and that the matters stated in the affidavit are afterthoughts devised as a defence to the petition. There has been no dispute about the fact that the petitioner did not come within the chief householder category but came within the category of occupant of "annexes". For the latter category only 20% of the vacancies based on proximity to the school were available; for, of the total applications made 50% were reserved on the ground of proximity and 50% for children of past pupils or parents who have a child presently studying in the school-vide section 4 (IV) (V) & (VI). In filling 50% from the category in subsection 4 relating to proximity of residence 80% of the vacancies were to be filled from among chief householders and 20% from genuine residents who were sub-tenants or occupants of annexes. Of the category of 20% relating to annexes or sub-tenants the circular enjoined that the Admission Committee shall take special care to satisfy themselves as to the genuineness of this category of

280

residents. Thus the Admission Committee was invested with a duty and a wide discretion and latitude to scrutinize this type of application carefully so as to prevent malpractices and abuses on the part of applicants and it was quite competent for it to refuse applications under this category if it was not so satisfied. The counsel for petitioner has contended that A5 does not stipulate that the Committee was not satisfied with the genuineness of the application but only that residence has not been proved. A5 embraces 4 broad categories of refusal the last of which was made applicable to the petitioner- namely that residence has not been proved ; in my view in the form in which it is couched it would imply that the Committee was not satisfied with the genuineness of the application as in normal circumstances the documents produced by the petitioner might have otherwise sufficed. This is borne out by the tact that the respondent has subsequently filed affidavit and documents on 8.3.85 to establish that the petitioner was in fact residing at 32, Sri Saranankara Road, Dehiwela since 1979 and not at 175, Bambalapitiya, Colombo 4 as stated by him. Indeed the 1st respondent declared in that affidavit at para 3 that in the electoral register of 1983 (Certified extracts filed as 1R4) the name of the petitioner appears as an occupant of premises 32, Sri Saranankara Road, Dehiwela for the year 1983, that the petitioner has paid rates and taxes in respect of these premises for the year 1984, 1R5 and extract from proceedings before Commissioner of National Housing 1R7 in which it has been represented that petitioner was residing at 32, Sri Saranankara Road, Dehiwela in 1981. It is thus quite clear that even if not set out in A5, the Admission Committee was amply justified in not being satisfied with the genuineness of the petitioner's application regarding residence for 18 months prior to his application at No. 175, Galle Road, Bambalapitiya and in stating that residence has not been proved.

The magnitude of the task faced by the Principal and Admission Committee regarding admission to the Kindergarten section of Visakha Vidyalaya can be gauged from the fact that the Principal has stated in her affidavit that for 70 vacancies there were 725 applications for the school year 1984, and that after processing the application an interviewing about 685 applicants 60 children were selected. She has also stated that of 111 appeals 5 were allowed. It is thus quite evident that the problem faced by the Principal and Admission Committee and by the Appellate Board in scrutinizing the applications ensuring that they conformed to the requisite qualification of proximity of residence

281

to the school etc. as set out in A3 and being satisfied with the genuineness of the residence of the applicants was by any standards a most exacting and formidable one and that in this anxious scramble for a meagre available number of 70 vacancies by 725 applicants much frustration and discontent would be caused in the minds of unsuccessful parents, for, to every parent the admission of his child to one of the best schools is a matter that concerns him very dearly. This problem is compounded by the principal and Admission Committee having to be satisfied with genuineness of the residence of persons who occupied annexes for 18 months prior to the making of the application and that they were not mere ad hoc residences for the purpose of conforming to the proximity of residence qualification set out in A3. The petitioner being outside the category of chief householder had to reside within 18 chains of the school -(vide para II (I) of the 1st respondent's affidavit) whereas the petitioner in fact resided a distance of 22 chains away from the school -(vide his own declaration in the application from 1R1, cage 9). Thus initially his application did not have the requisite residence qualification of proximity to the school. This court will not lightly interfere with the administration of the principal of the school and with the determination of the Admission Committee and Appellate Board unless for very compelling reason as to do so will disorganize the scheme of admission and will be detrimental to the proper administration of the school indeed S. A. de Smith in his treatise on Judicial Review of administrative Action, 4th edition at p. 163 has stated that-

"The allocation of government contracts and university places may cause much hardship to the unsuccessful contender, but it has not yet been held in this country that they have any common law right to go to the courts on the ground that their applications have been summarily rejected, even if the rejection has been based on an adverse undisclosed report".

In the instant case the petitioner has been given a hearing and the documents produced by him have been considered before his application has been refused. I am of the view that in the context of the scramble for admission to 70 vacancies in the Kindergarten of Visakha Vidyalaya by 725 applicants, a person cannot insist as of right that his child must be admitted to that particular school of his choice, however anxious or desirous he may be. Indeed section 8 of the Circular A3 stipulates that the C.E.O. should take action to find places for all rejected cases by 30th November and expressly ensures that

282

every effort will be made to see that every child will be found some school and not left "languishing at home" as alleged by the petitioner. Manifestly the petitioner's residence being not within 18 chains but being 22 chains away from the school as declared by him does not qualify him and the writ cannot lie. The court will also take notice of the fact that the child is now already attending school in Bishop's College, Colombo.

The learned Deputy Solicitor General strongly contended that all these facts pertaining to residence at Dehiwela proved by 1R5 were withheld from this court in the petitioner's application for Writ which is thus lacking in uberrima fides and that on this ground too the application must fail. I am inclined to agree with this submission. A petitioner who seeks relief by Writ which is an extraordinary remedy must in fairness to this court, bare every material fact so that the discretion of this court is not wrongly invoked or exercised. In the instant case the fact that the petitioner had a residence at Dehiwela is indeed a material fact which has an important bearing on the question of the genuineness of the residence of the petitioner at the annexe and on whether this court should exercise its discretion to quash the order complained of as unjust and discriminatory. On this ground too the application must be dismissed for lack of uberrima fides.

The application of the petitioner thus having failed on the above grounds, it is hardly necessary to consider the alleged ground of discrimination against the petitioner's child on the ground that after the refusal of his application some other children with less qualification have gained admission. Discrimination and denial of equal rights cannot be agitated in an application for Writ of Certiorari and must form the subject of an action for fundamental rights which cannot be canvassed in this court. I see no merit in this application for Writ of Certiorari for the reasons set out and dismiss this application with costs fixed at Rs. 315.

T. D. G. DE ALWIS, J. - I agree.

Application dismissed.
