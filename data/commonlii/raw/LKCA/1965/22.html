Peter Perera v. Simon Appu - NLR - 226 of 68 [1965] LKCA 22; (1965) 68 NLR 226 (5 October 1965)

226

1965 Present : T. S. Fernando, J., and Alles, J.

R. A. PETER PERERA and another, Appellants, and M. SIMON APPU,
Respondent

S. C. 184-185 (Inty.) of 1964-D. C. Gampaha., 8347/P

Partition action-Execution of decree in respect of costs of contest and pro rata costs of action-Bights of parties-Partition Act, s. 58 (1) (c)-Civil Procedure Code, s. 346 (1).

Where, as between two parties in a partition action, the sum recoverable as costs of contest by one party is less than the pro rata costs of action payable by him to the other party, the party entitled to the costs of contest cannot, by virtue of the provisions of section 346 (1) of the Civil Procedure Code, take out execution for the lesser sum against the other party.

APPEAL from an order of the District Court, Gampaha.

H. W. Jayewardene, Q.C., with A. Paranavitane, for the 1st purchaser, appellant in Appeal No. 184.

H. A. Koattegoda, for the 2nd purchaser, appellant in Appeal No. 185.

T. "B. Dissanayake, with D. R. Jayakody, for the plaintiff-respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

October 5, 1965. T. S. FERNANDO, J.-

These two appeals raise the question of the correctness of an order of the District Court of Gampaha made on 16th November 1964 setting aside all proceedings arising out of an application for execution of writ made by the 4th defendant against the plaintiff in this action.

In the action which is one for the partition of a certain land the court on 11th July 1962 entered interlocutory decree ordering commission to issue to partition the land and declaring that the parties under mentioned are entitled to undivided shares in the following proportions :-

Plaintiff to an undivided 12/504 share

2nd defendant to an undivided 1 /504 share

3rd defendant to an undivided 1/504 share

4th defendant to an undivided 490/504 share.

The interlocutory decree contained further orders, (i) that the plaintiff do pay Rs. 73-50 as costs of contest to the 4th defendant, and (ii) that the costs of action (under Section 58 (1) (c) of the Partition Act) fixed at Rs. 750 plus the costs of surveys and of preliminary plans be paid to the plaintiff pro rata by the parties to the action.

227

It is not in dispute that the 4th defendant therefore became liable to pay to the plaintiff in respect of the costs of action alone a sum of Rs. 721-20.

On an application made on 1st November 1962 by the 4th defendant for execution of decree in respect of the payment of Rs. 73-50 by the issue of writ against the plaintiff, the court issued writ on 9th November

1962. The writ was returned to court unexecuted on 23rd January 1963, but on an application of 27th February 1963 by the 4th defendant the court ordered a reissue. The writ was returned executed on 10th May

1963. the Fiscal reporting also that interests in two lands seized in execution of the writ had been sold on 2nd May 1963 for the sums of Rs. 25 and Rs. 125 respectively. The first of these interests was an undivided 1/42 share of the land which is the subject of partition in this very case in the course of which the writ was issued, while the second was an undivided 1/7 share of a land of six acres called Millagahawatte. The Fiscal's own valuation of these interests as shown in his report were respectively Rs. 62-50 and Rs. 1,714-29.

At the inquiry held in the District Court prior to the making of the order appealed from, a fiscal's officer stated in evidence that he went to the house of the plaintiff to demand payment of the amount for which writ had been issued but that he did not meet the plaintiff on that occasion. This action was taken by the officer probably in presumed compliance with the requirements of section 226 (1) of the Civil Procedure Code. The same officer stated that on the occasion on which he affixed seizure notices on the land (lands?) he met the plaintiff who in fact told him to get the lands sold to the schoolmaster (one of the defendants). The learned District Judge has apparently rejected this evidence, at least in respect of the meeting and the conversation alleged to have taken place between the fiscal's officer and the plaintiff, because he finds in the course of his order that the evidence does not establish that the plaintiff was aware that his properties were sold for the amount due from him as costs to the 4th defendant.

On 28th May 1963 the plaintiff moved court that the execution proceedings be declared null and void and the sales of the interests in the two lands be set aside.

It was argued both before the District Court and before us that, under the interlocutory decree of 11th July 1962, while the 4th defendant became entitled to what has been termed in the argument an immediate order for payment of costs the plaintiff became entitled only to a contingent order. It was argued that the immediate order became capable of execution the moment the interlocutory decree was entered, while in respect of the contingent order in favour of the plaintiff the amount due had not (at the time of the entering of the decree) been determined in that costs of survey had not been ascertained. We are unable to recognise the existence of the distinction contended for in this case. As

228

we see the problem, it is one of pure construction or interpretation of the relevant statutory provision (section 346 (1) of the Civil Procedure Codes:- "When two parties are entitled under the same decree to recover from each other sums of different amounts, the party entitled to the smaller sum shall not take out execution against the other party "; it is undeniable that both parties, i.e., the 4th defendant and the plaintiff became entitled to recover costs under one and the same decree. Having regard to the respective sums they became entitled to recover thereunder, which sums are apparent from a reading of the decree itself as a whole, it follows that the 4th defendant was disentitled to levy execution against the plaintiff. To recognise any such distinction as has been contended for by the appellants would require a recasting of the wording of the relevant statutory provision, and probably an interpolation of words as well. Such a course of action is, in our opinion, unwarranted, and we think the District Judge was right in declaring that the execution proceedings taken by the 4th defendant were prohibited by section 346. All proceedings had in that regard are, in our opinion, null and void.

Both appeals are therefore dismissed with costs payable in each instance to the plaintiff.

ALLES, J.-I agree.

Appeals dismissed.
