Jeganathan v. Ranasinghe - NLR - 263 of 68 [1965] LKCA 56; (1965) 68 NLR 263 (25 August 1965)

263

1965 Present : H. N. G. Fernando, A.C.J.

M. JEGANATHAN, Appellant, and D. C. RANASINGHE (S. I. Crimes),
Respondent

S. C. 504165-M. G. Hatton, 15208

Motor Traffic Act-Section 45 (1)-Charge of carrying passengers for hire in a private car-Proof-Requirement of production of revenue licence.

The carriage of a passenger for fee or reward in a private car is not punishable without reference to the conditions entered in the revenue licence applicable to the particular car.

264

APPEAL from a judgment of the Magistrate's Court, Hatton. Siva Rajaratnam, for the Accused-Appellant. K. Ratnesar, Crown Counsel for the Attorney-General.

August 25, 1965. H. N. G. FERNANDO, A.C.J.-

The appellant has been convicted of using a motor car for a purpose not authorised by the revenue licence issued in 1965 for that car. The alleged unauthorised purpose was the carriage of a passenger for fee or reward.

The offence constituted by section. 45 (1) of the Act is the use of a motor vehicle for a purpose not authorised by the revenue licence for that vehicle. I do not see how a court can be satisfied that some act is not authorised by the revenue licence unless the licence itself is produced to the court. In this particular case the Magistrate took judicial notice (quite properly) of the provisions of law differentiating between hiring cars and private cars and between! the colours in which the numbers of such cars are respectively exhibited. But there is no provision in the Motor Traffic Act which expressly says that a passenger may not be carried for fee or reward in a car which has been registered as a private car. Therefore, in the absence of the revenue licence applicable to a particular car, and without reference to the conditions which may be entered in such a licence, it is not possible to say beyond doubt that the carriage of a person for fee or reward in a car which does not bear a red number is a purpose not authorised by the revenue licence. Since the revenue licence was not produced in this case the appeal has to be allowed, and the conviction and sentence set aside.

Appeal allowed.
