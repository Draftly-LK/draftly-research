Nalliah v. Pure Beverages Co. Ltd. - NLR - 311 of 68 [1965] LKCA 57; (1965) 68 NLR 311 (22 July 1965)

311

1965 Present  ; G. P. A. Silva, J.

S. A. NALLIAH, Appellant, and PURE BEVERAGES CO. LTD.,
Respondent

S. C. 14/1964-C. R. Colombo, 85001

Cheque-" Holder in due course "-Bills of Exchange Ordinance, ss. 27 (1) (a), 29 (1) (6).

A person who takes a cheque from another without giving valuable consideration and, after it is realised by his Bank, pays the amount of the cheque to the person who gave him the cheque is not a holder in due course within the meaning of section 29 (1) (6) of the Bills of Exchange Ordinance. He will, therefore, be liable to a third party who subsequently claims the amount of the cheque from him on the footing that the cheque had been misplaced and lost by him.

APPEAL from a judgment of the Court of Requests, Colombo.

G. T. Samerawickreme, with N. Kasiraja and W. S. Weerasooria, for Plaintiff-Appellant.

E. B. Vannitamby, for Defendant-Respondent.

July 22, 1965. G. P. A. SILVA, J.-

The plaintiff-appellant in this case sued the defendant-respondent for the recovery of Rs. 250 which was the amount of a cheque which was misplaced by the plaintiff and ultimately found its way to the defendant company through one of its employees who drew cash from the company. When the employee Sirisena wanted cash for the cheque the defendant sent it to its Bank Account and the amount was paid to Sirisena after realisation. The case for the plaintiff was that he had received this cheque from one W. G. de Silva and, before he could part with the cheque, that he had lost it and one Sirisena, an employee of the defendant company, into whose hands it had come, gave it to the defendant company in order to obtain cash. After a lapse of two days the appellant found that the cheque was realised and the amount paid to Sirisena.

312

The learned Commissioner in giving judgment against the plaintiff held that the defendant company was the holder in due course of the cheque and that it had given value for the cheque in good faith. Counsel for the appellant, who drew my attention to the Bills of Exchange Ordinance, relied on section 29 (1) (6), which has to be read with section 27, to show that the defendant company was not the holder in due course according to provisions of that section. I think there is force in his contention. For, even though the cheque was paid for by the company in good faith, it is not possible to say that the company took the cheque for value as denned in section 27 (1) of the Ordinance for the reason that there was no consideration for it of the nature that is contemplated by the provisions of section 27 (1) (a), nor was it given in discharge of an antecedent debt or liability within the meaning of that sub-section. Further, the payment of the amount of the cheque depended on the happening of a certain event, namely, that it was realised by the Bank. It seems to me that it is only on the basis of the defendant company being the holder in due course that it can resist the plaintiff's claim. The learned Commissioner has misdirected himself on this point when he gave judgment dismissing the plaintiff's action. I, therefore, allow the appeal, set aside the order of the learned Commissioner and order that judgment be entered for the plaintiff-appellant with costs in both courts.

Appeal allowed.
