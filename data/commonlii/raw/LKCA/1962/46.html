Town Council, Chavakachcheri v. Devabalan.P - NLR - 10 of 68 [1962] LKCA 46; (1962) 68 NLR 10 (13 November 1962)

10

1982 Present : L. B. de Silva, J., and Sri Skanda Rajah, J.

TOWN COUNCIL, CHAVAKACHCHERI, Appellant, and P. DEVABALAN,
Respondent

S. C. 2S9J60-D. C. Chavakachcheri, 1830/M

Contract-Clouse exempting one party from all liability-Effect thereon of an express limitation-Damages resulting from breach of contract-Quantum.

The defendant (a Town Council) entered into a contract with the plaintiff to supply electricity to a theatre which belonged to the plaintiff. A stipulation in the contract was as follows :-

" Every means and precaution will be taken to ensure an efficient and continuous supply of energy to consumers between the hours of 6 p.m. and 12 m.n. but the Town council will not be held responsible for any interruption, nor shall the Town Council be held liable to the consumer for any loss or damage occasioned, directly or indirectly, by the total or partial interruption of supply." The plaintiff sued the defendant for damages for failure to supply electricity on certain days. The trial Judge held that the failure was due to the defendant Council not taking reasonable steps to maintain an efficient supply of electric energy.

Held, that the plaintiff was entitled to be awarded damages. The first part of the aforesaid stipulation provided an express limitation of the exemption clause in the second part of the stipulation.

Held further. that the plaintiff was bound by law to take reasonable measures to minimise his damages resulting from the defendant's breach of contract.

APPEAL from a judgment of the District Court, Chavakachcheri.

H. W. Jayevardene, Q.C., with B. Nagalingam am L. C. Seneviratne, for the Defendant-Appellant.

C. Ranganathan, with Miss Suriya Wickremasinghe, for the Plaintiff-Respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

November 13,1962. L. B. DE SILVA, J.-

The Plaintiff who was the proprietor of Davendra Theatre applied to the Chairman of the Town Council, Chavakachcheri, for a supply of electricity to his premises on application (P17) dated 12th May, 1951. The application is subject to the conditions set forth overleaf which the applicant declared that he had perused. This application was accepted by the defendant Council and electricity was supplied to the plaintiff.

11

The stipulation relevant to this case is as follows-" Every means and precaution will be taken to ensure an efficient and continuous supply of energy to consumers between the hours of 6 p.m. and 12 m.n. but the Town Council will not be held r3sponsible for any interruption, nor shall the Town Council be held liable to the Consumer for any loss or damage occasioned, directly or indirectly, by the total or partial interruption of supply. "

The plaintiff is suing the defendant for damages for a failure to give the plaintiff an efficient and continuous supply of electric energy for carrying on the business of his theatre and for wrongfully and unlawfully cutting off the supply of electric energy between 6 p.m. and 9 p.m. daily during the period 19th March, 1959 to 22nd June, 1959 and during the entire nights of the 21st & 22nd May, 1959.

The learned District Judge held that the defendant had failed to give an efficient and continuous supply of electric energy to the plaintiff during the period in question and that the cutting off of electric energy to plaintiff's theatre was due to the defendant Council not taking reasonable steps to maintain an efficient and continuous supply of electric energy. He accordingly awarded damages to plaintiff.

This case has been argued before us at considerable length. On a careful examination of all the evidence led, we are unable to hold that the learned District Judge was not justified in coming to the conclusion he arrived at. We do not consider it necessary to re-capitulate the evidence which justified this conclusion.

The learned Queen's Counsel for the defendant-appellant argued that the stipulation in P17 which was referred to earlier, completely exonerated the d3fendant from all claims to damages occasioned directly or indirectly by the total or partial interruption of supply and the defendant was not responsible for any interruption of the supply.

This action is based on a contract entered into by the parties by the defendant's acceptance of the plaintiff's application (P17). There is no doubt that the contract is subject to the conditions on the back of P17. The plaintiff had admitted that he was aware of these conditions when he made his application.

It was further argued that the first part of the stipulation, " Every means and precaution will be taken to ensure an efficient and continuous supply of energy to consumers between the hours of 6 p.m. and 12 m.n. " was a mere assurance from which no legal obligations flowed in view of the second part of this stipulation.

In the Privy Council Case Sze Hai Tong Bank, Lid. v. Rambler Cycle Co., Ltd.[1 (7959) 3 A.E.R. 18t.] Lord Deaning considered an exemption clause in a bill of lading. His Lordship said at p. 185, " The exemption on the face of it could hardly be more comprehensive, and it is contended that it is wide enough

12

to absolve the shipping Company from responsibility for the act of which the respondents complain, that is to say, the delivery of the goods to a person who, to their knowledge, was not entitled to receive them. There is, therefore, an implied limitation on the clause, which cuts down the extreme width of it, and, as matter of construction, their Lordships decline to attribute to it the unreasonable effect contended for. But their Lordships go further. If such an extreme width were given to the exemption clause, it would run counter to the main object and intent of the contract..........The clause must, therefore, be limited and modified to the extent necessary to enable effect to be given to the main object and intent of the contract.........

To which extent is it necessary to limit or modify the clause ? It must, at least, be modified so as not to permit the shipping company deliberately to disregard its obligations as to delivery.......... No Court can allow so fundamental a breach to pass un-noticed under the cloak of a general exemption clause.........."

In the present case the main object of the contract is the supply of electricity. If the exemption clause, as contended for by the appellant, means that the appellant is not liable for any interruption of supply, even a deliberate and wrongful interruption without any cause, it would undoubtedly run counter to the main object and intent of the contract. But in this case, the parties have provided for a reasonable limitation of the exemption clause by the first part of it and it is, therefore, not necessary to construe this clause with any implied limitation as the parties have agreed to an express limitation.

This express limitation to the exemption clause in the contract must be given effect to and it cannot be ignored, as contended by the appellant, as a mere assurance of no legal consequence.

We therefore conclude that the learned District Judge has rightly held that the defendant was liable in damages for cutting off the supply of electric energy to plaintiff's theatre as the defendant had failed to take reasonable precautions to ensure such a supply of energy.

On the question of damages, both parties to this appeal have agreed that the learned District Judge had made a wrong approach to this question.

The plaintiff had been warned by the defendant of an impending interruption of the supply. As a result the plaintiff had purchased a generator to ensure an independent source of supply of electric energy to this theatre. The plaintiff was bound by law to take reasonable measures to minimize his damages resulting from the defendant's breach of contract.

The proper quantum of damages in this case is the extra expense incurred by the plaintiff in running his generator from 6 p.m. to 9 p.m. from 23rd March, 1959, till 22nd June, 1959, and from 9 p.m. till m.n. on the 21st and 22nd May. He will also be entitled to the loss sustained by him for the failure to have any shows for 4 days from the 19th till the

13

22nd March, till he procured and installed the generator and for reasonable depreciation for the use of the generator during the period. The plaintiff has kept back the generator as he may need it in the future.

Plaintiff states that he purchased the generator for Rs. 6,000. He had to pay a high price as he was compelled to buy it in a hurry. It was a generator disposed of by the military. Undoubtedly the plaintiff had purchased a generator and he did so in a hurry. There is only his word that he paid Rs. 6,000 for it. He stated that he spent Rs. 1,200 to instal it but produced no accounts in support. Considering all the circumstances we hold that the plaintiff should be awarded Rs. 1,500 as damages for loss and depreciation on the generator for the period in question and for installing it.

Plaintiff stated that he paid Rs. 400 to a mechanic to drive the generator for the 3 months and about Rs. 800 for oil during this period. The defendant justly complains that the plaintiff could have accurately proved what he paid the mechanic and for the oil from his account books without giving such vague evidence. He further stated that he normally paid about Rs. 300 a month for electricity consumed for both shows. This should be accepted as correct as the defendant could have disproved this if it was not.

Plaintiff would have paid about Rs. 150 a month for electricity consumed for one show daily-i.e. from 6 p.m. to 9 p.m.-i.e. Rs. 450 for the three months. It appeal's to be fair and reasonable to allow him Rs. 300 for the mechanic and Rs. �00 for the oil for the period in question. The cost of running the generator for this period is about Rs. 800. He has thus incurred a loss of Rs. 350 on this account. If this loss is an under-assessment, the plaintiff himself is to blame for his failure to give accurate figures to assess this loss.

The plaintiff has also suffered loss by his failure to have both the shows from the 19th to the 22nd March inclusive. The plaintiff had arranged to show a film called " Saranga Thara " from the 19th March. He however showed that film for 4 days from the 23rd March. He says that the total collection for this film was Rs. 685 for these four days.

The document P26 shows that the total collection from both shows for this film was as follows :-

23. 3. 59  .. Rs. 203 50
24. 3. 59  .. Rs. 104-50
25.3.59    .. Rs. 115-00
26.3.59    .. Rs. 102-00

These figures show that there was a good collection on the first day the film was shown but the receipts had fallen to about half that figure on the subsequent days. The plaintiff pays a percentage of the collections for the hire of the film. It would be fair to aware1 the plaintiff Rs. 250 as damages for the loss sustained during these four days.

We accordingly assess the damages payable to the plaintiff in this Case at Rs. 2,100.

We enter judgment for plaintiff in a sum of Rs. 2,100 as damages. As plaintiff's claim was highly exaggerated, we award the plaintiff only 4 costs of this action in the Rs. 2,103 class. Parties will bear their own costs of the appeal.

We direct the Decree entered in the District Court to be varied accordingly.

SRI SKANDA RAJAH, J.-I agree.

Decree varied.
