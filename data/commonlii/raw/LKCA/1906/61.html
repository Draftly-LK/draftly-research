WAIRAVEN CHETTY v. JUSSAN SAIBO.

D. C, Kandy, 16,903.

Promissory note-Signature by attorney-Form of signature-Custom-Bills of Exchange Act, s. 26 (1).

    Where a promissory note was . signed as follows: " Meeyann Kawanna Dawdo Kanny Saibo's attorney Jana Mohammad Kanny Saibo," and it was contended that Dawdo Kanny Saibo was not liable on the note, but that the attorney was personally liabl under section 26 (1) of the Bills of Exchange Act, the Supreme Court directed the issue to be tried-Whether the signature was in the form usually adopted in similar circumstances in Ceylon by an attorney in executing an instrument in the name of th principal.

    THE plaintiff, the endorsee of a promissory note, sued th defendant, the administrator of the late of the estate Meeyanni Kawanna Dawdo Kanny Saibo, who, the plaintiff alleged, made th note through his attorney Jana Mohammadu Kanny Saibo.

    The note was signed thus: " Meeyanna Kawanna Dawdo Kann; Saibo's attorney Jana Mohammadu Kanny Saibu."

    The defendant contended that the signature did not bind Dawdi Kanny Saibo, his intestate, but that it bound the attorne; personally.

    The District Judge (J. H. de Saram, Esq.), following the judgment of the majority of the Supreme Court in Carimjee Jafferjee v Sebo (1), held that the signature bound Dawdo Kanny Saibo, and that the defendant, as his administrator, was liable to pay the amount of the note.

    The defendant appealed.

    Walter Pereira, K. C, S.-G., for the defendant, appellant.

    Van Langenberg, for the plaintiff, respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

    19th November 1906. WENDT J.-

       It is impossible, as the record stands, to decide the question whethe: Dawdo Kanny Saibo is liable on the note sued upon, the parties no being agreed as to the exact significance of the signature by which
