Chairman, Municipal Council, Colombo v. Fonseka, Et Al. - NLR - 145 of 38 [1936] LKCA 37; (1936) 38 NLR 145 (29 July 1936)

1936 Present : Abrahams C.J. and Koch J.

CHAIRMAN, MUNICIPAL COUNCIL, COLOMBO v.
FONSEKA et al.

9-D. C. (Inty.) Colombo, 2,898.

Land acquisition-Land acquired by Crown within Municipal limits-Street lines laid over building land-Depreciation of land-Compensation Ordinance, No. 19 of 1915, s. 18 (4), and Ordinance No. 3 of 1876, s. 22.

Where street lines have been laid down under section 18 (1) of the Housing and Town Improvement Ordinance, No. 19 of 1915, in respect of land abutting a public street, and the land is subsequently acquired by the Crown by directions issued to the Chairman of the Municipal Council,-

Held, that in determining the amount of compensation to be paid the fact that the land has ceased to be building land may be taken into consideration.


THIS was an appeal from an order of the District Judge of Colombo determining the amount of compensation payable to the respondents in respect of the acquisition of an allotment of land belonging to them situate at Havelock road.

The acquisition was made by the Chairman of the Municipal Council in pursuance of a direction issued to him by His Excellency the Governor. The Chairman fixed the amount of compensation at Rs. 50, while the respondents claimed a sum of Rs. 1,737.50. The matter was then referred to the District Judge, who after inquiry awarded the respondents a sum of Rs. 1,433.43 and condemned the Chairman to pay the respondent's costs.

The Chairman of the Municipal Council appealed.

A. E. Keuneman, K. C. (with him F. C. W. van Geyzel), for the plaintiff, appellant.-The acquisition took place at the instance of the Crown and the only question to be determined is the market value of the land at the time of awarding compensation under section 21 of Ordinance No. 3 of 1876. The entire portion of land acquired falls within street lines that have been duly laid and the land must therefore be valued with reference to the existence of such street lines and the consequent prohibition of building within them. Where property is subject to limited user its valuation must necessarily take that factor into account. (Cowie v. Mc-dermott [1 (1914) A. C. 1056]; Uyagar Lall v. Secretary of State [2 I. L. R. 33 All. 733.] ; Newnham v. Gomis [3 35 N. L. R. 119.].) If the defendant has rights to compensation under other Ordinances like 6 of 1910 and 19 of 1915, by reason of the laying down of street lines those rights should be canvassed in other proceedings and cannot affect the market value under section 21 of 3 of 1876.

There is no evidence of the value of the land acquired other than that given by the appellant's assessor and this must, therefore, it is submitted, be accepted as the market value of the land.

N. E. Weerasooria (with him J. A. T. Perera) for defendant, respondent.-The laying down of street lines is a step in acquisition and any depreciation in value caused thereby should not be taken into

146

consideration in assessing the compensation payable. It was decided in the case of In re Lucas and The Chesterfield Gas and Water Board [1 (1909) 1 K. B. 16.] that no increase in the value of the land resulting from the authorization of the scheme which led to the acquisition should be taken into consideration in assessing the compensation. Section 21 of the Land Acquisition Ordinance provides that compensation should be assessed as at the date of the acquisition. Street lines were laid in 1925, but it is possible to assess the market value in 1935, without taking into consideration any depreciation in value caused by the laying down of street lines in 1925. The laying down of street lines does not render the land sterile. There is no provision of law which prohibits building on land which falls within street lines. Even if the laying down of street lines does render the land sterile and the depreciation in value thereby caused should be taken into consideration, there has, in point of fact, been no depreciation in this case. The land acquired forms part of a building site. This portion would not, in any event, have been built upon. The prohibition against building did not, therefore, cause any depreciation. Portions of a building block which are not built upon should be valued at the same rate as the portion or portions which are built upon. (Government Agent Kandy v. Marikar Saibo'[ 2 (1911) 6 Weer. 36.] )

Keuneman, K. C., in reply.-The two contentions put forward on the respondents' behalf, viz., that the laying down of street lines is the first step in acquisition and that the law does not prohibit building within street lines, are unsound. Acquisition can take place though no street lines have been laid down and, on the other hand, even if street lines have been laid down no acquisition may follow nor is there any obligation to acquire. The primary object of laying down street lines is to prevent building right down to the street and not merely for widening a street.

The respondent's second argument is entirely negatived by the provisions of section 18 (1) (a) of Ordinance No. 19 of 1915, which provides that every building shall be erected either on the line of an existing street or on the line of a new street and according to section 7 (1) of the same Ordinance the Chairman " shall not approve any plan or specification of a building which shall conflict or cause such building to conflict with the provisions " of the Ordinance.

Cur. adv. vult.

July 29,1936. KOCH J.-

The appeal is by the Chairman of the Municipal Council of Colombo from an award of the District Court determining the amount of compensation payable to the respondents in respect of the acquisition of an allotment of land which is described as lot 1 in preliminary plan No. A 391 and was a part of the premises bearing No. 116, situate at Havelock road, belonging to the respondents. This block is 6.95 perches in extent and is 63 feet in length and 30 feet in depth. It ran parallel to and along the line of what was Bambalapitiya road and is now named Havelock road. The acquisition was made in pursuance of the formalities prescribed in the Land Acquisition Ordinance, No. 3 of 1876, where under His

147

Excellency the Governor, for purposes of acquiring land for public purposes, is empowered with the advice of the Executive Council to direct the Chairman of the Municipal Council of Colombo, in the case of lands situate within the limits of the Council, to take order for the acquisition of any such land. At the date of the directions issued in regard to the block in question the Executive Council, was no longer in existence, its place having been taken by the Executive Committee of Local Administration. The Chairman who was duly empowered to do so, took the necessary steps in 1934 and, after serving the required notices, proceeded to inquire into the value of this allotment, and after hearing the respondents fixed the amount of compensation at Rs. 50. The respondents claimed a sum of Rs. 1,737.50. Thus disagreement arose between the parties as to the compensation to be allowed, and the matter was referred to the District Court for determination under section 11 of the Ordinance.

It would thus appear that the acquisition and all the proceedings being under the Ordinance No. 3 of 1876, the value of the compensation payable to the respondents has also to be determined in terms of that Ordinance. Section 21 of the Ordinance lays down that " in determining the amount of compensation to be awarded for land acquired under this Ordinance the District Judge and assessors shall take into consideration the market value, at the time of awarding compensation, of such land ". It will be seen that the crucial date of that market value is the date of awarding compensation, and, under section 8 of the Ordinance, that date is the day on which the Chairman, after hearing the parties concerned, determines the amount. In this case that determination was made in September, 1934. The only question therefore that arises on this appeal is whether the sum of Rs. 50 awarded by the Chairman was the market value of the allotment in September, 1934. As the result of the reference made by the Chairman under section 11, the matter came up for determination before the District Judge and two assessors who after inquiry awarded the respondents a sum of Rs. 1,433.43 and condemned the Chairman to pay the respondents their costs of action (sic). This amount was arrived at as the result of an agreement between the parties that was recorded in Court before evidence was led, which was as follows: " It is agreed that buildable land in that locality is worth Rs. 30,000 an acre". There can be little question that this value was the value prevailing in September, 1934.

The learned District Judge and the assessors arrived at the conclusion, as the result of the proceedings held before them, that the block was buildable land. It is contended by Counsel for the appellant that this block was not buildable land as street lines were validly laid to the old Bambalapitiya road so far back as 1915 under powers set out in Ordinance No. 19 of 1915, and that the entirety of the block fell within the street lines so laid. The District Judge however decided, and the assessors were of that opinion too, that the street lines in question were not validly laid down, as the laying down of these lines was effected with the ultimate object of widening Bambalapitiya road and acquiring this block inter alia for that purpose.

The only evidence recorded at this inquiry was the evidence led by the Chairman. None of the respondents was called nor other evidence put before the Court on their behalf. The Chairman's case consisted of the

148

evidence of Messrs. Stewart Orr, the Municipal Assessor, and Mr. Wijenathan, the Chief Municipal Engineer, and certain formal documents. This evidence clearly showed that the street lines in question were laid down in accordance with the formalities prescribed by law in 1915 several years before the lands that fell within them were actually acquired for widening Bambalapitiya road. Assuming that these lines were laid in 1915 with the ultimate object of widening the road, can it be said that the laying down of them was invalid?

Counsel for the respondents argued that although these street lines were laid down in 1915, nineteen years before this block was acquired, the laying down of these lines constituted the first step in the acquisition proceedings and that therefore, granted the effect of laying down street lines was to render the land within them sterile and unbuildable, such sterility or restriction could not in the circumstances be taken into account. He cited the case of In re Lucas and The Chesterfield Gas and Water Board [1 (1909) 1 K. B. 10.]

In this case the land acquired along with other contiguous lands were required for the purposes of a reservoir. The land was found to possess peculiar natural advantages for a reservoir site. This feature was taken into consideration by the High Court in assessing the compensation. On appeal, however, the Appellate Court was of opinion that the true principle that should guide the assessment of compensation should be what the value of the land was to the owner. He must receive the equivalent of the land he gives up but this equivalent must be the value to him and not the value to the purchaser. Therefore the adaptability of the land for the reservoir was not a fit and proper matter for consideration as an element in the assessment, and it was an absolute rule that the value is to be estimated as it stood before the grant of the compulsory powers. It is the rule enunciated in these last few lines that Mr. Weerasooria specially emphasizes, and he seeks to apply this finding to the circumstances of the present case. The value he contends should be assessed as what it was before the first step in these proceedings. I find difficulty in agreeing with this argument firstly, because I feel that the interpretation put on the words is not correct. The learned Law Justices of Appeal did not intend by these words to mean that the value should be the value before the legal proceedings of acquisition commenced. What they did mean would appear to be clear from the rest of the judgment, and that is that the owner should receive compensation based upon the market value of the land as it stood before the scheme for the building of the reservoir was authorized which would be at a time before the scheme itself materialized and could in any way affect the value of the land either advantageously or detrimentally. There can be little doubt that the principle set out is good law as the same principle is expressly laid down in section 23 of our Land Acquisition Ordinance. Under the head of " -fifthly " the words are " any increase to the value of the land acquired, likely to accrue from the use to which it will be put when acquired shall not be taken into consideration". In other words, the Appellate Court in that case was referring to the scheme of some construction works for the fulfilment of which the land was acquired and not the legal proceedings that had been set afoot for the purpose of acquiring that land.

149

The two assessors in the present case, both laymen, arrived at the decision cadit quaestio I have mentioned, on what they thought the law ought to be and not on what actually is the law.

The learned District Judge in coming to the same decision relied on sections 172 and 173 and also on section 149 of the Municipal Councils Ordinance, No. 6 of 1910. I do not see how these sections help in the present proceedings; in brief the first two sections empower the Council to provide by means of lines the limits by which any street is bounded or a proposed street should be bounded; there is also provision that when this is done and a building or any part of it is taken down the Chairman may require the same to be set back to the street line and the portion of land added to the street line shall become vested in the Council but the Council must make full compensation to the owner. The third mentioned section empowers the Council to lay out and construct new streets and to widen and improve existing streets, in which event it requires the Council to make due compensation to the owners of lands and houses. The effect of these sections is to ensure to the owner whose land or part of land is taken over by the Council the payment of due compensation.

The appellant does not dispute this very commendable principle but what he does insist on is that the compensation he is liable to pay should be the actual market value of the land acquired at the date I have already stated.

The learned District Judge refers to certain English decisions the correctness of which the appellant does not dispute although their relevancy is questioned.

There is one important difference to be noted and that is that these sections referred to apply to cases where the Council itself annexes property for its own purposes by virtue of Ordinance No. 6 of 1910, while on the other hand in these proceedings it is the Crown that is the acquiring authority and it does so under a totally different Ordinance-No. 3 of 1876. The purpose for which the acquisition is made is irrelevant and the instrument used, viz., the Chairman, is purely a formality and a convenience.

Mr. Keuneman on behalf of the Chairman largely depends on the effect of section 18 (1) (a) of the Housing Ordinance of 1915, which reads thus:-

" Every building erected or re-erected after the commencement of this Ordinance within the administrative limits of any local authority, shall be erected either upon the line of an existing street or upon the line of a new street defined or approved by the Chairman or otherwise authorized under this or any other Ordinance ".

The effect of this provision he argues is to effectually prevent a building to be erected within the street lines which have been validly laid and to render the space within those lines sterile and not buildable. I think the argument is sound. All Mr. Keuneman has to prove is that the street lines have been laid down in accordance with the formalities prescribed and that the lot in question falls within those lines. This he has done and the market value of the allotment will have therefore to be ascertained with reference to this restriction. It may be that such a finding may occasion in many cases great hardship on the owner and on owners

150

circumstanced as the respondents are but it is our duty to administer the law as we find it and not to decide on what should be the law. To do otherwise would be to legislate, a proceeding which only comes within the province of the Legislature.

Mr. Keuneman has also referred us to the case of Newnham v. Gomis [1 35 N. L. R. 119.], which he submits is on all fours with the present case. This is a recent judgment by a Bench of two Judges which laid down that in acquisitions under the Land Acquisition Ordinance the compensation to be awarded is dependent on the depreciation in value of the land caused by the laying down of street lines.

Mr. Orr has given his reasons for assessing the compensation payable at Rs. 50. He has in detail both in examination-in-chief and in cross-examination stated that he has given consideration to every circumstance and to every use that this allotment may be put to but has been compelled to come to the conclusion that if the block is unbuildable there is no other profitable purpose that it can serve situated as it is both in relation to Havelock road and the rest of the block which is capable of providing for at least a 40-feet lawn in front of the bungalow that may be later erected on it. There is no evidence to the contrary and it would appear to judge from the proceedings and the agreement recorded-which I have already referred to-that the respondents staked their case on this block being buildable.

I am therefore of opinion that the sum of Rs. 50 represents the market value of the allotment in question in September, 1934, and that the Chairman's tender under section 8 of the Land Acquisition Ordinance was in order. The award of the District Court will be varied to this extent and the appeal allowed with costs. The respondents will have to pay the appellant the costs of the proceedings and the inquiry in the District Court.

ABRAHAMS C.J.-I agree.

Appeal allowed.
