Peiris v. The Building Superintendent of Dehiwela-Mount Lavinia Municipal Council - NLR - 353 of 68 [1964] LKCA 29; (1964) 68 NLR 353 (9 October 1964)

353

1964 Present : Alles, J.

M. E. PEIRIS, Petitioner, and THE BUILDING SUPERINTENDENT
OF DEHIWELA-MOUNT LAVINIA MUNICIPAL COUNCIL
and another, Respondents

S. C. 302/64-Application for Revision in M. G. Colombo South, 25663/N

Erection of unauthorised building-Conviction of offender-Re-erection of building thereafter-Procedure for taking cognizance thereof-Housing and Town Improvement Ordinance, as. 5, 13 (1), 13 (2)-Criminal Procedure Code, ". US (1).

Where a person who has been convicted of erecting an unauthorised building for contravention of the provisions of section 5 of the Housing and Town Improvement Ordinance demolishes that building and subsequently rebuilds it, the only way in which the Magistrate can take cognizance of the fact that the building has again been rebuilt would be after separate proceedings are instituted in the Magistrate's Court under section 148 (1) of the Criminal Procedure Code.

APPLICATION to revise an order of the Magistrate's Court, Colombo South.

E. Weerakoon, for accused-petitioner.

B. J. Fernando, for complainant-respondents.

Cur. adv. vult.
 

October 9, 1964. ALLES, J.-

The petitioner in this case was charged in M. C. Colombo South Case No. 25663 by the Building Superintendent of the Municipal Council, Dehiwela-Mount Lavinia with having erected an unauthorised building at No. 42, Peiris Avenue, Kalubowila West without plans, drawings, and specifications approved in writing by the Mayor of Dehiwela-Mount Lavinia Municipality, in contravention of the provisions of Section 5 of the Housing and Town Improvement Ordinance and thereby committing an offence punishable under Section 13 (1) of the said Ordinance.

To this offence the accused-petitioner pleaded guilty on 27.3.63, and the Magistrate postponed sentence for 22.5.63 on which date he imposed a fine of Rs. 25 in default two weeks imprisonment.

On 4.9.63, the Mayor of Dehiwela-Mount Lavinia Municipal Council applied for a mandatory order of Court under Section 13 (2) of the Ordinance requiring the accused-petitioner, who had been convicted in the case, to demolish the unauthorised building within a specified period. After several unsuccessful attempts, this order was served

354

on the accused-petitioner on 29.1.64 and on 27.5.64 the Proctor appearing on behalf of the Council informed the Court that the shed had been demolished and the Magistrate thereupon discharged the petitioner.

On 10.7.64, Mr. Quentin Perera, Proctor, filed the petition and affidavit of one W. A. L. Fonseka and asked for notice on the accused-petitioner and the Proctor for the Council to appear in Court. In his petition Fonseka alleged that the unauthorised structure had not yet been demolished, and the Magistrate issued notice forthwith on the accused petitioner and the Council for 29.7.64. On that date the Council, the accused-petitioner and Fonseka were represented by Counsel and the Magistrate recorded evidence. The Proctor for the accused-petitioner objected to any evidence being led in support of Fonseka's petition on the ground that the accused had been discharged and that Fonseka had no status to re-open the case. He submitted that the only party who had any right to re-open the case was the Council. The Proctor for the Council stated that he was present only to assist the Court. In spite of the objections of the Proctor for the accused-petitioner, the Magistrate proceeded to record evidence and one of the witnesses whose evidence was recorded was L. M. V. de Silva, the Building Inspector of the Dehiwela-Mount Lavinia Municipality. Mr. Silva stated in evidence that after the building had been demolished it had been rebuilt. The Magistrate appears to have taken the view that the re-erection of the unauthorised structure was a contempt of the authority of the Court and therefore felt justified in holding an inquiry into Fonseka's petition. The inquiry was postponed for 26.8.64, and the Proctor for the accused-petitioner made another application that he be informed even at that stage of the charge or the provision of law under which action was being taken against his client. The Magistrate thereupon made the following order :-

"In an order made on 29.7.64 in the presence of the petitioner Mr. C. R. de Alwis on behalf of the Council and the accused and his Proctor I have stated the reasons why I have caused evidence in this case to be led. I do not intend to repeat myself. As Mr. Quentin Perera stated that he is moving for a postponement on personal grounds as his Counsel is in a difficulty I fix this inquiry for 16.9.64."

Thereupon the accused-petitioner made the present application to this Court in which he prayed for a revision of the Magistrate's order of 26.8.64. His complaint was that he had not yet been informed under what provision of law the inquiry was being conducted and he submitted that he had been treated as an accused person without a formal charge.

355

I think there is substance in the accused-petitioner's complaint. When it was reported that the shed was demolished, and the accused-petitioner discharged, the proceedings which commenced with the report to Court by the Council under Section 148 (J) (b) of the Criminal Procedure Code were terminated. If thereafter the accused-petitioner had rebuilt the unauthorised shed, he was committing another offence for which appropriate proceedings should have been taken under the provisions of the law. There might have been some justification for the Magistrate's attitude had the shed not been demolished, in which case it would have been open to the Magistrate to take action against the petitioner for a disobedience of the mandatory order that was issued by the Court. Such a disobedience might have amounted to a contempt of the authority of the Court. But since the evidence disclosed that the unauthorised structure had again been rebuilt, the only way in which the Magistrate could take cognizance of that fact would have been after proceedings are instituted in the Magistrate's Court under Section 148 of the Criminal Procedure Code. If Fonseka wanted to move in the matter he should have made his representations to the Council, and the Council would have taken steps to file a 148 (1) (b) report to Court as they did on the first occasion. Indeed, Counsel who appeared for the Council before me had no objection to the present application being allowed and he submitted that if there was an infringement of the provisions of the Housing and Town Improvement Ordinance, the Council would again institute proceedings against the accused-petitioner. I agree with Counsel for the accused-petitioner that Fonseka had no status to re-open the inquiry against the accused. Proceedings in a Magistrate's Court can only be instituted in one of the six ways mentioned in Section 148 (1) of the Criminal Procedure Code. The filing of a petition and affidavit by an interested party is not a method warranted by law whereby proceedings can be instituted in Court. It is essential that Magistrates should comply strictly with the provisions of the law in this matter, as otherwise they are likely to leave the door open to a flood of applications from interested persons who are anxious to set the machinery of the Courts in motion to serve their own personal interests I quash the proceedings held by the Magistrate after 10.7.64 on which date Fonseka's petition and affidavit were submitted to Court. If there is any contravention of the Housing and Town Improvement Ordinance hereafter, it will be open to the Council to take appropriate Steps against the accused-petitioner under the provisions of the Criminal Procedure Code.

Proceedings quashed
