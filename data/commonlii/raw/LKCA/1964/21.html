Victor De Silva v. Jinadasa De Silva - NLR - 45 of 68 [1964] LKCA 21; (1964) 68 NLR 45 (9 November 1964)

45

1964 Present : Sirimane, J., and Manicavasagar, J.

 VICTOR DE SILVA and others, Appellants, and JINADASA DE
SILVA and others, Respondents

S. C. 45/62 - -D. C. Tangalle, 6708

Execution of decrees-Order of Court to Fiscal to stay execution of writ-Duty of Fiscal to obey the order whether it he riqh.t or wrong-Cleft Procedure Code, as. 92, -343, 839.

Where the Court orders the Fiscal to stay execution of a sale under a  writ, the Fiscal is bound to obey the order whether it be right or wrong. The Fiscal is an officer of the Court. He executes a writ on the authority of the Court sad ho is subject to its direction and control at every stage of the execution.

The Court ordered the Fiscal to stay the sale of certain properties seized in execution of a. writ. The order was made because the judgment-debtors had presented an application to the Supreme Court to set aside in revision the decree in execution of which the writ had issued. Despite the order of Court to stay execution, and while the application in revision was still pending in the Supreme Court, the Fiscal proceeded with the sale because the Fiscal's charges were not paid. The purchasers of some of the properties seized were the judgment-creditors. After the application in revision had been dismissed by the Supreme Court, the judgment debtors moved the District Court to set aside the sale to the judgment creditors. The District Court thereupon set aside that sale.

Hold, that the District Judge was right in setting aside the sale. He had inherent power under section 839 of the Civil Procedure Code to issue the order to the Fiscal to stay execution of the writ. The Fiscal must obey such an order whether or not it is in conformity with the provisions of the Code. Section 343 of the Code applies to an other made on an application by a party, and not to an order made by Court in the exercise of its inherent powers.

APPEAL from a judgment of the District Court, Tangalle.

H. W. Jayewardene, Q.C., with Nimal Senanayalce and I. S. de Silva, for Plaintiffs-Purchasers-Respondents -Appellants.

N. E. Weerasooria, Q.C., with M. T. M. Sivardeen, for Substituted Defendants Petitioners Respondents.

November 9, 1964. SIRIMANE, J.-

The appellants (as plaintiffs) flied this action against the 1st defendant. respondent and his father, the 2nd defendant, claiming a sum of  Rs. 4,979 - 85 and continuing damages, alleging that they had been wrongfully dispossessed of shares in certain lands. The 2nd defendant died shortly after the action was instituted and his widow, the 2A defendant-respondent, and two children 2B and 2C defendants respondents were substituted in his place after notice (2B defendant being the same as the 19t defendant respondent.

46

These respondents filed proxy and answer but were absent on the trial date, and the appellants obtained a decree nisi against them, which was subsequently made absolute. 2A to 20 defendants- respondents (whom I shall refer to hereinafter as the respondents) made an application to this court to set aside the decree absolute in revision.

While that application was pending certain properties belonging to the respondents were seized and sold in execution of that decree and some of them were purchased by the appellant.

The respondents moved to set aside that sale, and the learned District Judge did so on two grounds, namely:-

(A) that the substitution of the respondents was irregular ; and

(B) that the court having ordered a stay of sale the fiscal had no power to carry out the sale in defiance of such an order.

This appeal is from the learned District Judge's order setting aside the sale.

In regard to (A), the respondents raised no objection to being substituted in place of their father. In fact they filed proxy and answer, and took part in certain subsequent proceedings. There is a decree entered against them by the District Court, and their application to this court to set aside that decree has been refused. In my view it is too late now for the respondents to avoid the consequences of that decree by pointing to some procedural flaw before they filed proxy and answer. Learned Counsel for the respondents did not seek to support the learned District Judge's order on this point.

In regard to (B) the following facts have to be noticed. The fiscal by letter informed the District Judge that he had received a complaint that "the defendant " (presumably the 2B defendant-respondent) had "threatened to cause bodily harm .... to the plaintiff and the fiscal's officers who would be present at the sale . . . . " He asked the Judge for Police protection and requested that the defendant " be kept in custody if possible ", and added " I am awaiting yoiir directions in this matter ".

The learned District Judge has made an endorsement on that letter ordering the fiscal to stay execution as the defendant has filed revision papers.

This order or direction was conveyed to the fiscal before the sale by the Secretary who stated in his letter that he did so " By Order of Court ". The Fiscal then wrote to the Secretary that if the Fiscal's charges were not remitted he would proceed with the sale, and in fact did so.

The questions which arise for decision are whether the court can issue such an order or direction to the fiscal, and, if it does, whether the fiscal is bound to carry out such order. I have no doubt that both these questions must be answered in the affirmative. The Fiscal is an officer of the court. He executes a writ on the authority of the court and he is

47

subject to its direction and control at every stage of the execution. I think the learned District Judge was right when he reached the conclusion that he had inherent power to issue such an order under section 839 of the Civil Procedure Code.

In the case of Pains a. Somasunderam Chetty [(1924)50. 1. Recorder 229.] , De Sampayo, J. said I should say that the court which had ordered the sale had inherent power to control and guide the officer who was authorised to carry it out

It was contended for the appellant that because the Civil Procedure Code laid down the procedure to be followed in the execution of writs, the fiscal was entitled to follow that procedure. The fiscal must undoubtedly follow the procedure laid down in the code, but that does not mean that when the court gives him an order or direction the fiscal is entitled to decide whether such an order is in conformity with the provisions of the code, and disobey it if he thinks that it is not.

Our attention was drawn  to section 343 of the code. which- reads as follows :

(1) "The court may for sufficient cause stay execution proceedings at any stage thereof, and make order for adjournment of sale ".

(2) "An application to court to stay proceedings shall be made by petition, to which all persons interested in the matter of the execution shall be made parties  and no such order shall be made until after payment of all fiscal's fees then due " .

It was contended that a sale could be stayed only on payment of fiscal charges and that sub-section (1) was dependent on sub-section (2). In Peiris a. Somasunderam Chetty (supra) the learned Judge referred to sub-section (1) quite independently of sub-section (2). It is my view that the fiscal is bound to obey an order of court whether it be right or wrong. I am of of the  view that section. 343 applies to an order made on an application by a party, and not to an order made by court in the exercise of its inherent powers.

It was also sought to draw a distinction between judicial sales and execution sales. in the matter of an officer who is entrusted with the execution of a writ obeying an order issued by court, it seems to me that no distinction can be drawn between a judicial sale and an execution sale.

The learned District Judge was, in my opinion, right in setting aside the sale on the second ground. The appeal is dismissed with costs.

Manicavasagar, J.-

I agree  that this appeal should be dismissed with costs.

Mr. Jayewardene for the appellant made two submissions and I wish to state my views.

48

He submitted that the "order" to the Fiscal to stay sale made by the learned District Judge, and communicated by the Secretary of the Court to the Fiscal, was no order at all. I do not agree with this submission. The Judge on receipt of letter (exhibit X) from the Fiscal asking for directions brought his mind to bear on the matter and made order as Judge directing the Fiscal, who is an officer of the Court, to stay sale The Fiscal should have carried out this order, whether it was right or wrong. Mr. Jayewardene contended that if it was an order of the Court, then the request for directions by the Fiscal and the order of the Judge should have been entered in the journal of the action as provided in Section 92 of the Civil Procedure Code; the journal is merely the principal record of the action  that a particular order is not minuted in the journal does not take away from it the character of a judicial order ; I notice from the proceedings that the record at this time was in this Court; even if the record was in the District Court, the fact that the order was not journalized does not make it ineffectual or useless.

Mr. Jayewardene's second submission is that the Judge bad no jurisdiction to issue directions or orders to the Fiscal where the execution of the decree is under the provisions of the Code, unless there be some provision in the Code authorising it ; in effect his submission on this particular matter is that the Court can only act under Section 343 of the Code, and that would be only on the application of the parties to the action. I am unable to adopt this submission. Whether it be a sale in execution by the Fiscal, or by a Commissioner appointed under the Mortgage Act, there is no difference in regard to the powers of the court to control the officer who is entrusted with the sale, and give him directions; my view is that f-he court has the inherent power to make the order it did. I have no doubt at all that a Court has the power to make order in regard to any matter where it believes that the justice of the case demands such an order, though there be no provisio  th the statute. In this case the judge took the view that as there was an application before this Court there was a possibility of injustice being done to the applicant by having his properties sold; I consider it irrelevant to inquire whether his order was right or wrong; the question is whether be had the power to make it; there is no provision in the Code regarding stay of execution where an application has been made to this Court to revise the order of the original Court; our Code is not exhaustive on all matters; one cannot expect a Code to provide for every situation and contingency; if there be no provision, it is the duty of the Judge, and it lies within his inherent power to make such order as the justice of the case requires: I cannot subscribe to the view that the powers of the Court are so completely circumscribed by the Code, that it is powerless to make an order where it is essential that it should be made hi the interests of justice.

Appeal dismissed
