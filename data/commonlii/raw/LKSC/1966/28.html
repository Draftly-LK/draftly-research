Romiel v. Senanayake - NLR - 255 of 68 [1966] LKSC 28; (1966) 68 NLR 255 (17 February 1966)

255

1966 Present : Sansoni, C.J.

S. ROMIEL, Petitioner, and D. S. SENANAYAKE, Respondent

Election Petition No. 36 of 1965-Electoral District No. 130 (Dedigama)


Election petition-Amendment of particulars-Circumstances when the Court trill not permit it.

In an election petition, the petitioner should exercise due care and make proper inquiries before particulars are filed by him. When he has been either negligent, or has deliberately sought to mislead the respondent, an application made by him to amend the particulars will not be granted by the Court.

APPLICATION to amend particulars filed in Election Petition No. 36 of 1965-Electoral District No. 130 (Dedigama).

K. Shinya, with Nimal Senanayake, Miss Manouri de Silva, U. B. Weerasekera, D. Senanayake and T. Edirisuriya, for the Petitioner.

E. G. Wikramanayake, Q.C., with 0. E. Chitty, Q.C., R. A. Kannangara, H. D. Tambiah, M. Underwood and Ajit Amerasinghe, for the Respondent.

256

February 17, 1966. SANSONI, C.J.-

Mr. Senanayake for the Petitioner has applied to amend item 14 of the particulars in respect of the date mentioned in that item. He wishes to amend it to read 11th February instead of 11th January. Mr. Wikramanayake has objected.

The purpose of particulars is " to prevent surprise and unnecessary expense, and to ensure a fair and effectual trial"-see 14 Halsbury (3rd Edn.) para. 492. Particulars were applied for in this case by the Respondent, and upon an order of Court these particulars were supplied. They are necessary from the Respondent's point of view to enable him to know the case he has to meet, by giving him sufficient detailed information to enable him to prepare for the trial. Another purpose of particulars is to enable the Court to exclude irrelevant matters and to concentrate on the real matters in controversy. When a wrong date is given in respect of any charge, it causes prejudice to the party charged, because he will not be able to ascertain the real facts. He is put on a wrong scent by being given wrong information.

In this case it seems to me that dates given in the particulars have been regarded as something insignificant and of no consequence. In respect of several items already dealt with in the evidence before me wrong dates have been supplied in the particulars-if the Information Books maintained by the Police are reliable, as I have no doubt they are. Such items are (1), (5), (18), (19), (25), (27), (41) and (46).

I am aware that an order to amend particulars is entirely within the discretion of the Court. In the ordinary case, one may expect to find occasional errors in the dates supplied. The Court will probably excuse such errors on the ground that a bona fide mistake has been made. But here the number of errors in respect of the dates alone is so large, that I can only conclude that the Petitioner has been either negligent, or has deliberately sought to mislead the Respondent and the Court by supplying wrong information. In either event the Petitioner cannot be heard to say that when the correct date is discovered he should be allowed to amend. He should have exercised due care and made proper inquiries before particulars were filed. A party who makes mistakes on such a scale as I have already referred to is not entitled to relief.

The application to amend item 14 is refused.

Application refused.
