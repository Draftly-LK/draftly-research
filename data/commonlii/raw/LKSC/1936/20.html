Haramanis v. Silva - NLR - 141 of 38 [1936] LKSC 20; (1936) 38 NLR 141 (12 May 1936)

141

1936 Present : Poyser J.

In re AN APPLICATION FOR A WRIT OF QUO warranto.

HARAMANIS v. SILVA.

Writ of quo warranto-Inquiry by Government Agent to an objection to the nomination of candidate-Judicial decision-Issue of writ of quo warranto to canvass decision-Local Government Ordinance, No. 11 of 1920, ss. 29 (2) and 31 (4).

The decision of a Government Agent, following an inquiry under section 31 (4) of the Local Government Ordinance, No. 11 of 1920, into an objection to the nomination of a candidate for election to an Urban District Council, may not be canvassed by the issue of. a writ of quo

142

was an application for a writ of quo warranto to question the validity of the election of a member of the Urban District Council of Ambalangoda. The facts are stated in the judgment.

H. V. Perera (with him G. Wikramanayake), for applicant.

T. S. Fernando, for respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

May 12, 1936. POYSER J.-

This is an application for a writ of quo warranto against Santiappu Arthur de Silva, a member of the Urban District Council of Ambalangoda. . The facts are as follows:-It was necessary under the provisions of section 32 of the Local Government Ordinance, No. 11 of 1920, to hold in the month of November or December, 1935, a general election of members of the Urban District Council of Ambalangoda.

For the purpose of such election, the Government Agent of the Southern Province did, as required by section 29 of the said Ordinance, prepare for each electoral division-

(c) a roll of persons possessing the qualifications to vote, (b) a role of persons qualified to be elected as members.

The name of one Don John Paranavidane was included in both the said rolls prepared for ward No. 6.

An objection was lodged to the inclusion of the name of this person in the said rolls, and the Government Agent, on September 16, 1935, held in accordance with section 29 (2) of the said Ordinance an inquiry into such objection.

The grounds put forward in support of the objection were: -

(1) that he had not paid all the rates and taxes due by him under the said Ordinance,

(2) that he was not possessed of immovable property of the value of not less than Rs. 5,000.

The Government Agent, after hearing evidence, overruled the objection and Don John Paranavidane's name was included in both rolls.

On November 23, 1935, nomination day, Don John Paranavidane was duly nominated as a candidate for ward No. 6. Two other persons were also nominated, viz., the respondent and one Davith Singho de Silva.

A voter then objected, under section 31 (4) of the Ordinance, to Paranavidane's nomination on the ground that he was not duly qualified as he was not possessed of immovable property of the value of Rs. 5,000.

The Government Agent after inquiry and hearing the evidence adduced, upheld the objection and rejected the nomination of Don John Paranavidane. Objection was also taken to the nomination of the respondent but this objection was overruled and as Davith Singho de Silva withdrew his nomination, the respondent was declared to be duly elected.

It was argued on behalf of the applicant that as Paranavidane's name was on the roll of persons qualified under section 27 of the Ordinance and such roll had been duly certified and he had not become disqualified by

143

any subsequent act, that the Government Agent had no jurisdiction to inquire into, under section 31 (4) of the Ordinance, an objection which had already been adjudicated on and in support of this argument emphasis was laid on the words of section 29 (2), viz.:-

" The Government Agent, or some person deputed by him in writing on his behalf shall attend at the time and place so indicated, and shall decide all such claims and objections in a summary manner, and such decision shall be final and conclusive ".

It was also contended on behalf of the applicant that a writ of quo warranto was the appropriate remedy, and in support of this contention the case of Application for a Mandamus on the Chairman of the Municipal Council[1 18 N. L. R. 97. ] was cited.

In that case Wood Renton A.C.J, held that-

" Where a person has been elected de facto to a corporate office, and has accepted and acted in the office, the validity of the election and the title to the office can be tried only quo warranto and a mandamus will not lie unless the election can be shown to be merely colourable ".

It is to be noted, however, that this case was decided before the passing of Ordinance No. 11 of 1920, which gave the Court power to issue a mandate in the nature of quo warranto. Before that amending Ordinance a Full Court had expressed the opinion that the Court had no power to grant such a warrant. See Abeyewardene v. The Chairman of the Municipal Council, Galle [2 9 N. L. R. 304.].

Assuming, therefore, that section 31 (4) of the Ordinance does not empower a Government Agent to inquire into an objection whether a candidate possesses the necessary qualifications, when his name is already on the rolls prepared for the purpose of such election, and his qualifications have already been inquired into for such purpose, should a writ of quo warranto be issued?

This question has already been decided in a case which is binding on me, viz., Application for a writ of quo warranto [3 4 C. L. Rec. 81.] .

In that case it was held that where the Chairman of a Local Board exercises a judicial function, no writ of quo warranto will lie.
This decision is in accordance with a passage in Short on Informations (Criminal and Quo warranto), Mandamus and Prohibitions (1st ed.), at p. 132, which is as follows: -

" If there is any person appointed by law to discharge, at the election to an office, any functions of a judicial character with respect to it, an erroneous decision of such person in that character cannot be questioned by quo warranto ".

There is no doubt in my mind that the Government Agent in exercising his powers under section 31 (4) was exercising a judicial function and there is no doubt that he exercised his powers properly. A full inquiry was held, witnesses were called and cross-examined, and the reasons for allowing the objection were stated.

144

For these reasons, even if the Government Agent was erroneous, and the point is not free from difficulty, the writ of quo warranto should not issue and this application must be dismissed.

Although this application can be decided on the above grounds Mr. Fernando requested me to express an opinion, for the guidance of Government Agents, whether the decision of the Government Agent in this case was in fact erroneous. I do not, however, consider that is necessary in view of the case of In the matter of an Application for a writ of Mandamus on the Government Agent, Western Province [1 S. C. Minutes of April 8, 1036.], which was decided on the day after the argument in this case was concluded.

In that case a similar point arose and my brother Koch discusses at length the effect of section 31 (4) read together with section 29 (2).

In view of the grounds I am deciding this application on, it is unnecessary that I should express any further opinion.

The application is dismissed with costs.


Application dismissed.
