In the Matter of An Application For A Writ of Mandamus On the Government Agent, Western Province - NLR - 139 of 38 [1936] LKSC 17; (1936) 38 NLR 139 (8 April 1936)

139

1936 Present : Koch J.

IN THE MATTER OF AN APPLICATION FOR A WRIT OF Mandamus
ON THE GOVERNMENT AGENT, WESTERN PROVINCE.

Urban District Council-Objection to nomination of candidate-Candidate's name appearing on roll-Failure to object before roll is certified- Objection belated-Ordinance No. 11 of 1920, ss. 29 (6) and 31 (4).

An objection cannot be urged against the nomination of a candidate to an Urban District Council whose name appears on the certified roll, on the ground that he is disqualified, unless the disqualification has arisen since the roll was certified.

140

THIS was an application for a Writ of Mandamus on the Government Agent, Western Province.

C. V. Ranawake (with him Clarence de Silva), in support.

H. V. Perera (with him D. W. Fernando), for second respondent.

M. M. I. Kariapper, C.C., for first respondent (Government Agent, Western Province).

April 8, 1936. KOCH J.-

It is not necessary for me to enter upon a dissertation on the numerous authorities that have been cited by the various Counsel who have appeared in this matter on the preliminary point as to whether the applicant has misconceived his legal remedy if any.

I think the application can without difficulty be disposed of on the merits.

The only point advanced by the applicant is that the first respondent, the Government Agent, Western Province, Colombo, wrongly disallowed the objection that was raised by the applicant to the nomination for election of the second respondent. This objection was that the second respondent having been convicted of forgery in December, 1934, in case No. 25,113 of the Police Court of Colombo, was disqualified under section 27 (h) of Ordinance No. 11 of 1920 to be nominated a candidate under section 31. The decision on this point depends on the interpretation to be put on section 29 (2).

Now this section contains a number of provisions which it is unnecessary to detail, but it is sufficient to say that it declares that for the purpose of every general election of members of a District Council a roll for each electoral division has to be prepared of persons qualified under section 28 (2), and a further roll of persons qualified under section 27. This latter section contains the qualifications (which includes disqualifications) of persons who are eligible to be elected and to serve as a member of an Urban District Council. Section 29 proceeds to lay down that these rolls are open for inspection during office hours at the office, or other places, and at a later date to be duly fixed that the Government Agent will attend at the place notified and hear all objections to any name inserted on these rolls.

The section further provides that after hearing such objections the Government Agent " shall decide the objections in a summary manner and such decision shall be final and conclusive ".

I am decidedly of opinion that the words " final and conclusive" must be given their due weight and that these words mean that once the objection is disallowed the ruling is final for the purpose of the election. After objections (if any) are heard and determined, the section proceeds to state that the rolls shall be revised, and when certified " shall remain in force for the purpose of the Election". Reading this with the words "final and conclusive" referred to before, and in relation to the rest of the context of this section, I am of opinion that the rolls, when certified, have the effect of determining once and for all the

eligibility of a person to be elected a member at the date of such certification. The Ordinance goes on to provide for a nomination day when candidates for election have to be nominated, and section 31 (4) provides for any qualified voter objecting to the nomination of a candidate on the ground that he is not duly qualified. Herein arises a difficulty. Does this mean that although no objection under section 29 was previously taken to the name of the candidate, on the ground that he was disqualified, it is open to a voter to take this objection on nomination day. Mr. Kanawake very earnestly argued that the voter had this right still left to him, and that it would be a scandal to permit a man to be a member of a District Council who had been convicted for forgery. Scandal or no scandal, I cannot agree with Mr. Ranawake that the voter had this right. He previously had the right and did not avail himself of it. He cannot be permitted to exercise this right twice. The meaning I give to this sub-section is that a voter has the right on nomination day to say that a particular candidate for nomination is not duly qualified, that is to say, that his name does not appear on the certified roll or that since that date he has rendered himself disqualified by reason of acts set out in section 27, one of them being the commission of one or more offences as are set out in section 27 (h).

The conviction sought to be made use of took place in 1934, and the list containing the name of the second respondent as duly qualified to be a candidate for election was certified on September 4, 1935. The objection therefore comes too late and the presiding officer has, in my opinion, acted rightly in not entertaining it.

The rule against both respondents is discharged with costs.

Rule discharged.
