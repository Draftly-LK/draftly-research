In Re Karunaratne.T - NLR - 189 of 68 [1965] LKSC 32; (1965) 68 NLR 189 (20 December 1965)

189

1965 Present : Sri Skanda Rajah, J.

IN BE T. KARUNARATNE and 2 others

Election Petition No. 26 of 1965-Electoral District Bentara-Elpitiya

Election petition-Report of Judge as to corrupt practice of a witness-Point of time at which the witness may be called upon to show cause-" At the conclusion of the trial "-Ceylon (Parliamentary Elections) Order in Council, us. 81, 82.

Where a person, not a party to an election petition, is reported by an Election Judge under section 82 of the Ceylon (Parliamentary Elections) Order in Council, the point of time at which he should be called upon to show cause may be soon after, and not before, judgment is delivered as to the validity of the election.

In re Wickremasinghe (55 N. L. R. 324) followed. Ilangaratne v. de Silva (49 N. L. R. 87) not followed.

IN the matter of three witnesses called upon to show cause why they should not be reported to the Governor-General under section 82 of the Ceylon (Parliamentary Elections) Order in Council.

D. S. Wijewardane, with R. N. Hapugala, I. S. de Silva, W. S. Weerasooria and Ranjit Goonetilleke, for Tilak Karunaratne and D. S. Ponnamperuma.

S. D. Jayawardene, for Soma Witanachchi.

M. Kanagasunderam, Crown Counsel, as amicus curiae.

Cur. adv. vult.

December 20, 1965. SRI SKANDA RAJAH, J.-

The above petition was taken up for trial on 13th September, 1965, and after the hearing, which lasted several weeks, the respondent Samaranayake's election as Member of Parliament was declared void on 22nd November, 1965. As soon as that judgment was delivered Tilak Karunaratna and D. E. Ponnamperuma, who were present in Court, were called upon to show cause, if any, why they should not be reported to the Governor-General. Soma Witanachchi was absent and notice was issued on her to show cause, if any, why she too should not be reported to the Governor-General. It is relevant to mention that both Tilak Karunaratne and Soma Witanachchi were called by the respondent as his witnesses in an attempt to prove that the false statements of fact relating to the personal character or conduct of the petitioner were in fact true. Ponnamperuma was alleged to have distributed P2, one of the two pamphlets admittedly published by Tilak Karunaratne. It was also alleged that Ponnamperuma was an agent of the respondent, who was a candidate put forward by the United National Party and supported even by the United National Party Branches in the Bentara-Elpitiya Electorate.

190

The point of law raised by counsel appearing for these three persons is that they should have been called upon to show cause before the judgment was delivered. For this submission reliance is placed on the decision of Windham, J., in Illangaratne v. G. E. de Silva [1 (1047) 49 N. L. R. 87. ] where the learned Judge held that where a person, not a party to an election petition, is charged with corrupt or illegal practice, he must be given an opportunity of being heard and calling evidence before a decision is given as to the validity of the election.

The relevant provisions of the Ceylon (Parliamentary Elections) Order in Council are Sections 81 and 82 which are reproduced hereunder :-

81. "At the conclusion of the trial of an election petition the Election Judge shall determine whether the Member whose return or election is complained of, or any other and what person, was duly returned or elected, or whether the election was void, and shall certify such determination in writing under his hand.

Such certificate shall be kept in the custody of the Registrar of the Supreme Court to be dealt with as hereinafter provided.

82. At the conclusion of the trial of an election petition the Election Judge shall also make a report under his hand setting out-

(a) whether any corrupt or illegal practice has or has not been proved to have been committed by or with the knowledge and consent of any candidate at the election, or by his agent, and the nature of such corrupt or illegal practice, if any ; and

(b) the names and descriptions of all persons, if any, who have been proved at the trial to have been guilty of any corrupt or illegal practice :

Provided, however, that before any person, not being a party to an election petition nor a candidate on behalf of whom the seat is claimed by an election petition, is reported by an Election Judge under this section, the Election Judge shall give such person an opportunity of being heard and of giving and calling evidence to show why he should not be so reported.

Such report shall be kept in the custody of the Registrar of the Supreme Court, to be dealt with as hereinafter provided. "

The words which call for interpretation are : " at the conclusion of the trial ".

It should be observed that in the above case Windham, J., did not specifically consider these words. The same learned Judge, deciding the later case of In re James Appuhamy [11948) 49 N. L. R. 261.] after hearing the Attorney-General, departed from the earlier decision and dealt with James Appuhamy who was noticed, after the court had given its judgment and certificate under  Section 81.

191

In re S. A. Wickremasinghe[1 (1954) 55 N. L. R. 324. ]Swan, J., expressly considered the words, '' at the conclusion of the trial " and held that they denote a point of time when the trial is finally concluded by the delivery of the judgment. As rightly pointed out by Crown Counsel this was the only case in which these words were adverted to. In arriving at his decision Swan, J., reviewed all the earlier cases, including the two decisions of Windham, J., and the two English cases of Cheltenham [6 O' M. & H. 194.  ] and East Dorset [3 6 O 'M. & H. 22.] in both of which proceedings taken were subsequent to judgment.

I would respectfully follow the view expressed by Swan, J. To adopt the earlier of the decisions of Windham, J., will lead to anomalous situations. If, say, 15 persons are noticed before the judgment to show cause why they should not be reported to the Governor-General and one or more of them abscond then the judgment cannot be delivered till they appear before Court. They may by absconding continue to delay the delivery of the judgment till after the life of the Parliament. This will defeat the purpose and object of an election petition, viz., securing, in the public interest, at an early date a final determination of the matter and the representation in Parliament of the constituency affected : Senanayake v. Navaratne [ ' (1954)56 N. L. R. 5 (P. G.)]. Also there will be fifteen trials within one trial.

In James Appuhamy (supra) and S. A. Wickremasinghe (supra) they were not reported though they were noticed after delivery of judgment. Therefore, the further submission that the Court may not change its mind once judgment has been delivered though cause is shown is without substance.

The above are the reasons for holding that the procedure adopted in these three cases is correct.

Tilak Karunaratne.

It was sought to make out that there was a germ of truth in the statement imputed to him. He only intended to say that the petitioner Kariyawasam was late in coming to the Bentara part of the electorate which was also affected by the floods in May, 1963. I cannot agree. When he had visited that area to Tilak Karunaratne's knowledge in about a week of the floods he stated that Kariyawasam visited that area only after several weeks. Besides it was false to say that when Kariyawasam was asked for aid to the flood victims he gave a clever and evasive answer. Therefore, he is guilty of a corrupt practice and he should be reported.

D. B. Ponnamperuma.

He has filed an affidavit. It shows that he was Patron of one of the U. N. P. Branches in that area. The U. N. P. Branches, like the U. N. P.  Youth Leagues, in that area actively supported the respondent's candidature. Where a political party of which the candidate is a member places the services of its offices and its workers at his disposal and addresses meetings on his behalf, as in this case, such party and its active members are constituted agents of the candidate. A similar view was

192

taken in Don Philip v. Illangaratne  [1 (1949) 51 N. L. R. 561.] where the candidate was not even a member of the party which supported him. Therefore, the affidavit goes to establish that he was agent of the respondent. It is highly probable that he would have distributed P2, which was published by Tilak Karunaratne, President of the U. N. P. Youth League and agent of the respondent, in order to further the respondent's candidature. He is guilty of a corrupt practice. Therefore, he should be reported.

Soma Witanachchi.

Her affidavit seeks to reaffirm her evidence that she gave a bribe of Rs. 300 to Kariyawasam. She avers that she has other evidence to prove the truth of the allegation. The respondent who set out to prove the truth of this allegation is not likely to have failed to call such other evidence. I cannot accede to her request to recommend that no action be taken against her till her complaint to the Bribery Commissioner is disposed of. She should be reported as she is guilty of a corrupt practice, though she was made use of by unscrupulous supporters of the respondent.

Witnesses found guilty of corrupt practice.
