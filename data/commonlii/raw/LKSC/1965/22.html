Mohideen v. Village Council, Nintavur - NLR - 469 of 68 [1965] LKSC 22; (1965) 68 NLR 469 (14 September 1965)

469

1965 Present : Sansoni, C.J., and Manicavasagar, J.

S. H. L. MOHIDEEN, Petitioner, and VILLAGE COUNCIL, NINTAVUR
and another, Respondents

S.C.210 /65-In the matter of an Application for a Mandate in the nature of
a Writ of Mandamus

Electrical energy-Discontinuance of supply by licensee-Requirement of prior notice to consumer-Electricity Act, 3. 15-Mandamus.

A licensee who has been supplying electrical energy to a person is not entitled to discontinue the supply summarily without giving the latter an opportunity to put forward his objections as to why the supply should not be discontinued.

APPLICATION for a writ of mandamus.

B. Wanigatunga, with H. W. Senanayake, for petitioner.

Izadeen Mohamed, with S. Sharvananda and S. C. Crossette Thambiah, for respondents.

September 14,1965. SANSONI, C.J.-

In our view the petitioner, having once been given a supply of electrical energy, should not have been deprived of that supply. According to the documents filed in this case, there is a dispute as to whether the petitioner undertook, before the supply was granted to him, to produce a. letter of consent from the owner of the land over which the electrical wires were taken to the petitioner's house.

If the respondents had been conscious of their statutory obligations, they would have taken action under Section 15 of the Act before giving the petitioner his supply. They failed to do so, and after supplying electrical energy to the petitioner for some months, they, more or less peremptorily, discontinued the supply without even giving him an opportunity to put forward his objections before them as to why the supply should not be discontinued. The discontinuance was unreasonable in these circumstances, and also unjustified.

It would appear that this action was taken because a person called Marikkar Sikkanthar objected to the wires running over his land bearing premises No. 133. The respondents have filed, in support of their objections, an affidavit from this person. It is clear from that affidavit that Sikkanthar is neither the owner of those premises, nor the occupier. The owners are, apparently, the two minor children of Sikkanthar's deceased sister, who are in the custody of their maternal grand-mother. If anybody had a right to object to the continuance of the supply of electrical energy to the petitioner, it was these minor children. It is not at all clear to us as to why, on Sikkanthar's objection, the respondents summarily took action to discontinue the supply which they had been giving to the petitioner for some months.

470

For these reasons, the petitioner is entitled to ask that the supply of electrical energy, which was discontinued, should be restored to him. We therefore allow the writ to go. The petitioner is entitled to the costs of this application.

MANICAVASAGAR, J.-I agree.

Application allowed.
