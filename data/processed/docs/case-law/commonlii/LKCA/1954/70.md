SAN5OM, J.-The only question for decision on this appeal is whether the dowry deed of gift P1 dated 14th December, 1915, created a fldeicommissum in favour of the plaintiff-respondent. He is the only child of his mother Thangapillai and his father Arulapapillai. When his parents were about to get married Thangapillai 's father donated certain lands to them "by way of dowry " and the deed further provided that they shall "take charge of their properties and their rights and they and their children by her (Thangapillai's) womb shall enjoy the possession of these properties according to their wish for ever ". Arulapapillai died after the marriage his widow then married the defendant-appellant ; thereafter she died in 1948. The plaintiff-respondent brought this action claiming the entirety of the lands donated in the deed P1 on the basis that the deed created a fldeicommissum in his favour. 186 The learned District Judge decided that the deed created a fideicommissum because, in his opinion, "the intention of the donor was these properties should never go out of the family and hence there an implied prohibition against alienation out of the family and the commissarii had been clearly designated as the donees' children and descendants from generation to generation ". His attention does appear to have been drawn to the case of Kurunathappillai v. Sinnapillai [(1907) 3 Balasingham 194.] which was also a case heard in the District Court of Batticaloa, that case a deed of gift containing the following clause was considered "The garden, house, well and plantations of this value shall for ever this day be possessed and enjoyed by them and the children of the w of the said Sinnatankachi from generation to generation as dowry Middleton, J. and Grenier, J. held that these words did not create a conmissum. Middleton, J. said in the course of his judgment case decided by Mr. Justice Withers on the 26th September, 1898, C. R. Batticaloa 1150), almost identically the same words were employed and that learned judge, who must have had considerable experience construing documents of this description, said that he could find no words of prohibition or precatory words indicating that those to whom the gift first came should hand it over to those who came after. L present case the same observations, it seems to me, apply ". He quoted with approval the words of Wendt, J. in Ibanu Agen v. A sekara [(1903) 6 N. L. R. 344.] " Where the intention to substitute another for the first I is expressed or is to be gathered by necessary implication from language of the will, a fideicommissum is constituted ". Grenier said "It seems clear to my mind on a careful consideration of the in this deed that there was absolutely no intention on the pa the grantor to impose any kind of burden on the property which he had downed to the plaintiff. Indeed it seems to me that the fact of the property in question being downed property rendered it h improbable that the grantor would have imposed a fldeicommissum it ". With respect, I think that these reasons are quite sufficient justify a finding that the deed in question did not create a fideicommissum, in view of the similarity of the wording of the two deeds. Mr. Chelvanayakam argued that there was a clear indication of over to the children of the donees but I am unable to agree with While mention is made of the children, they are not mentioned as beneficiaries whose rights are to accrue in succession to the immediate do for I cannot find a clear indication of a fideicommissary obligation having been imposed by the donor upon the donees for their benefit is it reasonably clear when their rights, if any, are to vest in them Pabilina v. Karunaratne et al ['(1948) 50 N. L. R. 169.] '. Both these requirements are essential to the existence of a valid fideicommissum. The children might have been referred to merely to indicate the motive for the gift, that the contemplated marriage was the consideration for the gift. I do not think it is necessary to refer in detail to the authorities by Mr. Chelvanayakam. It is sufficient to say that in my opinion the 187 wording of the documents construed in the cases of Sitti Kadija v. de Saram [] and Noordeen v. Badurdeen [] is not comparable with the wording of the deed in this case. As so often happens in these cases the difficulty lies not in ascertaining the principles, but in applying the principles to the particular document under consideration. For these reasons I would set aside the judgment of the learned District Judge and hold that the deed P1 did not create a fideicommissum. The plaintiff-respondent is therefore declared entitled only to an undivided one fourth share of the lands in dispute. He is not entitled to ejectment of the defendant-respondent or to damages. He must pay the defendant-respondent the costs of this action in both Courts. FERNANDO, A.J .-I agree. Appeal allowed.
