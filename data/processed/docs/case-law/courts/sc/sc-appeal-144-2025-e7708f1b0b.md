The Applicant states further that he inquired from the Employer thereaŌer on several occasions over the telephone and even by leƩer dated 16th January 2019 as to when he could report for duty, but that he did not receive any response from the Employer. 3 (2) On 29th January 2019, the Applicant filed an applicaƟon in the Labour Tribunal of Gampaha [the Labour Tribunal] complaining that his services have been terminated unfairly. (3) AŌer inquiry, the Labour Tribunal held by its Order dated 15th November 2021 that the services of the Applicant had been terminated unfairly and awarded the Applicant compensaƟon in a sum of Rs. 270,000, being the equivalent of six months salary. Aggrieved, the Employer invoked the jurisdicƟon of the High Court of the Western Province holden in Gampaha [the High Court]. Having heard both parƟes, the High Court set aside the Order of the Labour Tribunal by its judgment delivered on 11th January 2024. (4) This appeal arises from the said judgment of the High Court. On 29th July 2025, leave to appeal was granted on the following three quesƟons of law: (a) Did the High Court err in law when it failed to consider that the Employer’s appeal should have been dismissed in limine as the Employer had failed to deposit the compensaƟon awarded before filing the appeal in the High Court? (5) When this maƩer was taken up for hearing, the learned Counsel for the Applicant submiƩed that the 1st quesƟon of law relates to the jurisdicƟon of the High Court to have entertained the appeal filed by the Employer, and that even though he took up the said objecƟon in the wriƩen submissions filed before the High Court, the High Court has not addressed the said objecƟon in its judgment. 4 (6) SecƟon 31D(3) of the Industrial Disputes Act, as amended [the Act] provides that a workman, a trade union which makes an applicaƟon to a Labour Tribunal, or the employer to whom that applicaƟon relates and who is dissaƟsfied with the order of the Labour Tribunal may appeal from that order on a quesƟon of law to the High Court established under ArƟcle 154P of the ConsƟtuƟon. (7) SecƟon 31D(4)(a)(i) provides as follows: “Every employer who – (a) appeals to a High Court established under ArƟcle 154P of the ConsƟtuƟon, against an order of a labour tribunal or makes an applicaƟon in revision against any such order; or (b) ... shall furnish to such labour tribunal, security in cash - (i) in any case where the order which is the subject of such appeal or applicaƟon directs only the payment of a sum of money to the workman, of an amount equal to such sum; ...” (8) SecƟon 31D(6) provides further as follows: “Every peƟƟon of appeal to a High Court established under ArƟcle 154P of the ConsƟtuƟon shall bear uncancelled stamps to the value of five rupees and in every case where the applicant is required to furnish security, be accompanied by a cerƟficate issued under the hand of the President of the Labour Tribunal to the effect that the appellant has furnished such security. ...” (9) It was the contenƟon of the learned Counsel for the Applicant that it was mandatory for the Employer to have deposited a sum of Rs. 270,000 with the Labour Tribunal within the period of appeal and thereaŌer to have annexed to the peƟƟon of appeal a cerƟficate issued under the hand of the President of the Labour Tribunal confirming that the said sum has been deposited. The learned Counsel for the Applicant submiƩed further that the Employer has failed to comply with such mandatory requirement and hence the High Court had no jurisdicƟon to have entertained and/or heard the appeal filed by the Employer. He cited the judgment of this Court in 5 Wimalasiri Perera and others v Lakmali Enterprises Diesel and Petrol Motor Engineers and others [(2003) 1 Sri LR 62] where Mark Fernando, J confirmed that the requirement to deposit security is mandatory. (10) I have examined the case record of the Labour Tribunal. The journal does not indicate that the Employer has deposited the said sum of Rs. 270,000 with the Labour Tribunal or that the President of the Labour Tribunal has issued a cerƟficate to that effect, nor has the Employer annexed a cerƟficate to its peƟƟon of appeal as required by SecƟon 31D(6). (11) The learned Counsel for the Employer did not contradict the submission of the learned Counsel for the Applicant nor did he provide any evidence to this Court that the Employer has complied with the aforemenƟoned requirement. (12) I am in agreement with the submission of the learned Counsel for the Applicant that it is mandatory for an employer who wishes to appeal against an order of a Labour Tribunal that has ordered the payment of a specified sum of money to an employee to deposit such sum in cash prior to filing an appeal. Doing so is a condiƟon precedent to an employer exercising its right of appeal. (13) The failure to comply with SecƟon 31D(4)(a)(i) of the Act amounts to there being no properly consƟtuted appeal before the High Court and is fatal to the maintainability of an appeal filed by an employer in the High Court. Thus, the peƟƟon of appeal filed by the Employer against the final order of the Labour Tribunal must stand dismissed, without any further consideraƟon by the High Court of the merits of such appeal. (14) A similar requirement by way of SecƟon 31DD(1A) with regard to the filing of an appeal to the Supreme Court by an employer who is dissaƟsfied by a judgment of the High Court was introduced by the Industrial Disputes (Amendment) Act, No. 22 of 2022. This provision was considered by this Court in Duro Pipe Industrial (Pvt) Ltd v Heƫge Pradeep Silva [SC Appeal No. 111/2022; SC minutes of 2nd December 2024] where Samayawardhena, J held that the deposiƟng of security is mandatory and that the Supreme Court shall not have jurisdicƟon to entertain such appeal if there is no compliance with such requirement. 6 (15) There is one maƩer that I wish to advert to prior to concluding. While I am not inclined to do so, I hold that the Applicant shall be enƟtled to a sum of Rs. 270,000 together with legal interest on such sum from the date of the Order of the Labour Tribunal unƟl payment is made in full. (16) In the above circumstances, I answer the first quesƟon of law in the affirmaƟve. The necessity to answer the second and third quesƟons of law does not arise.
